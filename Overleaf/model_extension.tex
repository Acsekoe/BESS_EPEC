\section{Components Shared by Both EPECs}

\subsection{Market physics}

Let \(y=(P,p^{ch},p^{dis},e,z)\) collect the lower-level variables. Both models use fixed demand, no load shedding, and the same PTDF DC network and storage equations:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:common-market}
\begin{align}
z_{n,t}
&=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_n}P_{g,t}
 +\sum_i\left(p^{dis}_{i,n,t}-p^{ch}_{i,n,t}\right)-D_{n,t},
&&\forall n,t, \label{eq:nodal-injection}\\
\sum_n z_{n,t}&=0,
&&\forall t, \label{eq:system-balance}\\
0\le P_{g,t}&\le\overline P_{g,t},
&&\forall g,t, \label{eq:generation-bounds}\\
-\overline F_l\le\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}&\le\overline F_l,
&&\forall l,t, \label{eq:line-bounds}\\
e_{i,n,t+1}&=e_{i,n,t}+\eta p^{ch}_{i,n,t}
-\eta^{-1}p^{dis}_{i,n,t},
&&\forall i,n,t, \label{eq:soc-balance}\\
0\le e_{i,n,t}&\le X^E_{i,n},
&&\forall i,n,t, \label{eq:soc-bounds}\\
e_{i,n,0}&=e_{i,n,|\mathcal T|},
&&\forall i,n. \label{eq:cyclic-soc}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The model-specific charging and discharging bounds and the market objective are given separately below.

\subsection{Investment feasibility and investor profit}

Each best response takes rival capacities as fixed. The active investor's feasible capacity set is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:capacity-feasibility}
\begin{align}
0\le X^P_{i,n}&\le \overline X_n-\sum_{j\ne i}X^P_{j,n},
&&\forall n, \label{eq:private-headroom}\\
r^{min}X^P_{i,n}\le X^E_{i,n}&\le r^{max}X^P_{i,n},
&&\forall n. \label{eq:duration-envelope}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Thus every jointly feasible outcome satisfies \(\sum_iX^P_{i,n}\le\overline X_n\). The daily economic profit used in both models is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:investor-profit}
\begin{align}
\Pi_i
&=R_i^{BESS}+R_i^{gen}-C_i^{deg}-K_i, \label{eq:profit}\\
R_i^{BESS}
&=\sum_{n,t}\pi_{n,t}
\left(p^{dis}_{i,n,t}-p^{ch}_{i,n,t}\right), \label{eq:bess-revenue}\\
R_i^{gen}
&=\sum_{g,t}s_{i,g}
\left(\pi_{n(g),t}-C_g\right)P_{g,t}, \label{eq:generation-rent}\\
C_i^{deg}
&=\frac{d_i}{2}\sum_{n,t}
\left(p^{ch}_{i,n,t}+p^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right), \label{eq:degradation}\\
K_i
&=\frac{CRF_i}{365.25}\sum_n
\left(c^P_iX^P_{i,n}+c^E_iX^E_{i,n}\right), \label{eq:daily-capex}\\
CRF_i
&=\frac{r_i(1+r_i)^{L_i}}{(1+r_i)^{L_i}-1}. \label{eq:crf}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here \(\pi_{n,t}=\lambda_{n,t}\) under nodal settlement and \(\pi_{n,t}=\lambda^{sys}_t\) under the optional single-zone settlement. Stand-alone merchants have \(s_{i,g}=0\); portfolio investors internalize their owned generators' spot rents.

\subsection{Lower-level embedding}

For fixed upper-level decisions, each market-clearing problem below is a linear program. In investor \(i\)'s MPEC it is replaced by primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and strong duality:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mpec-embedding}
\operatorname{PF}(y;x_i,x_{-i}),\qquad
\operatorname{DF}(u;x_i,x_{-i}),\qquad
Z^{P}(y;x_i,x_{-i})=Z^{D}(u;x_i,x_{-i}),
\end{equation}
where \(u\) collects the lower-level dual variables. This is the implemented primal--dual reformulation; no explicit complementarity products are added.

\section{Model 1: Capacity-Only EPEC}

In the capacity-only model investor \(i\) chooses only installed MW and MWh,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-strategy}
x_i^{C}=\left\{X^P_{i,n},X^E_{i,n}\right\}_{n\in\mathcal N}.
\end{equation}
The ISO retains full control of storage dispatch within installed power:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-dispatch-bounds}
0\le p^{ch}_{i,n,t}\le X^P_{i,n},\qquad
0\le p^{dis}_{i,n,t}\le X^P_{i,n},
\qquad\forall i,n,t.
\end{equation}
Given the capacities, the ISO solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-iso}
\min_{y}\quad
Z_C^P=
\sum_{g,t}C_gP_{g,t}
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,n,t}d_i
\left(p^{ch}_{i,n,t}+p^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:common-market},\ \eqref{eq:capacity-dispatch-bounds}.
\end{equation}
Investor \(i\)'s capacity-only MPEC is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-mpec}
\boxed{
\max_{x_i^C,y,u}\ \Pi_i
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:capacity-feasibility},\
\operatorname{PF}_C,\operatorname{DF}_C,\ Z_C^P=Z_C^D
}
\qquad\text{given }x_{-i}^C.
\end{equation}
Storage bids are not upper-level decisions in this model. The degradation term in \eqref{eq:capacity-iso} therefore guides ISO dispatch, while \eqref{eq:degradation} records the same physical cost in private profit; these are two appearances of one cost, not two charges to the investor.

\section{Model 2: Strategic-Operation EPEC}

The strategic-operation model adds hourly charge/discharge quantities and two-sided prices to the investment decision:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-strategy}
x_i^{S}=\left\{X^P_{i,n},X^E_{i,n},
q^{ch}_{i,n,t},q^{dis}_{i,n,t},
b^{ch}_{i,n,t},b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right\}_{n,t}.
\end{equation}
The submitted quantities must be feasible, and the ISO may accept any dispatch inside them:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:strategic-quantity}
\begin{align}
0\le q^{ch}_{i,n,t}&\le X^P_{i,n}, &
0\le q^{dis}_{i,n,t}&\le X^P_{i,n}, &&\forall i,n,t,\label{eq:offer-capacity}\\
0\le p^{ch}_{i,n,t}&\le q^{ch}_{i,n,t}, &
0\le p^{dis}_{i,n,t}&\le q^{dis}_{i,n,t}, &&\forall i,n,t.\label{eq:offer-dispatch}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
With the symmetric price bound \(\overline b\), the two-sided prices satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-price-feasibility}
-\overline b\le b^{ch}_{i,n,t},b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\le\overline b,
\qquad
b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\ge\frac{b^{ch}_{i,n,t}}{\eta^2},
\qquad\forall i,n,t.
\end{equation}
The second inequality prevents bids that reward an instantaneous charge--discharge loss cycle. A charging bid is a willingness to pay and consequently enters market cost with a negative sign; a discharging offer enters with a positive sign. The ISO problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-iso}
\min_y\quad
Z_S^P=
\sum_{g,t}C_gP_{g,t}
+\sum_{i,n,t}\left(
b^{dis}_{i,n,t}p^{dis}_{i,n,t}
-b^{ch}_{i,n,t}p^{ch}_{i,n,t}
\right)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:common-market},\ \eqref{eq:offer-dispatch}.
\end{equation}
Submitted prices replace degradation coefficients only in the ISO objective. Physical degradation remains \eqref{eq:degradation} in realized investor profit. The quantity-only variant is recovered by fixing truthful prices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantity-only-prices}
b^{ch}_{i,n,t}=-\frac{d_i}{2},
\qquad
b^{dis}_{i,n,t}=\frac{d_i}{2},
\qquad\forall i,n,t.
\end{equation}

The strong-duality term associated with the two dispatch upper bounds changes because their right-hand sides are offers rather than installed power:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-dual-change}
\underbrace{X^P_{i,n}\left(\rho_{i,n,t}+\sigma_{i,n,t}\right)}_{\text{capacity-only}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{q^{ch}_{i,n,t}\rho_{i,n,t}
+q^{dis}_{i,n,t}\sigma_{i,n,t}}_{\text{strategic operation}}.
\end{equation}

The strategic-operation MPEC is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-mpec}
\boxed{
\max_{x_i^S,y,u}\
\Pi_i-\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}-\Phi_i^{\epsilon}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:capacity-feasibility},\
\eqref{eq:strategic-quantity},\
\eqref{eq:strategic-price-feasibility},\
\operatorname{PF}_S,\operatorname{DF}_S,\ Z_S^P=Z_S^D
}
\qquad\text{given }x_{-i}^S.
\end{equation}
The optional numerical selectors are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-regularization}
\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}=\frac{\kappa_k}{2}
\left\|w_i^S-w^{S,ref}_{i,k}\right\|_W^2,
\qquad
\Phi_i^{\epsilon}=\epsilon\sum_{n,t}
\left[\left(b^{ch}_{i,n,t}\right)^2+
\left(b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right)^2\right].
\end{equation}
Here \(w_i^S\) stacks power capacity, scaled energy capacity, withheld quantities \(X_i^P-q_i^{ch/dis}\), and scaled bid prices; the weights reproduce the hourly averaging and unit normalization used in the implementation. The proximal term is zero in the unregularized model. The direct \(\epsilon\)-term acts only on bid prices. Neither term is included in reported economic profit \(\Pi_i\).

\section{Equilibrium and Settlement}

For either model \(m\in\{C,S\}\), a generalized Nash equilibrium is a fixed point of the investor best responses:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epec-equilibrium}
x_i^{m,*}\in
\arg\max_{x_i^m\in\mathcal X_i(x_{-i}^{m,*})}
\left\{\Pi_i-\Phi_i^m\right\},
\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal I,
\end{equation}
where \(\Phi_i^C=0\), \(\Phi_i^S=\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}+\Phi_i^\epsilon\), and \(\mathcal X_i\) contains the corresponding MPEC constraints and the rival-dependent nodal headroom. Both the capacity-only and strategic-operation implementations support damped Gauss--Seidel and Gauss--Jacobi diagonalization. In strategic Gauss--Jacobi mode, one complete snapshot of capacities, hourly quantity offers, and two-sided prices is frozen for every investor in a sweep; all damped best-response proposals are then applied simultaneously. Proportional projection onto the shared nodal capacity limits is retained as a feasibility safeguard.

The fully simultaneous strategic Gauss--Jacobi implementation removes artificial within-sweep early- and late-mover advantages and is therefore the preferred symmetric algorithmic convention for a simultaneous-investment game. Its independent investor problems can be solved in separate processes; a same-snapshot serial-versus-parallel check produced identical capacities, hourly bids, objectives, termination states, strong-duality gaps, and joint-settlement price bounds. This algorithmic symmetry does not by itself guarantee convergence or a unique equilibrium: robustness must still be assessed across common initial states and damping parameters, and projection events, regularization terms, damped steps, and raw undamped best-response residuals are reported explicitly.

After the final iterate, the ISO is cleared independently with all capacities and, for Model~2, all offers fixed. Reported prices and profits use this joint re-clear. Embedded MPEC prices are retained only as diagnostics because lower-level dual non-uniqueness permits optimistic price selection.
