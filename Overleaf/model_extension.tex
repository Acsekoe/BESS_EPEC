\section{Scope}

The model is a deterministic equilibrium problem with equilibrium constraints
(EPEC) for battery energy storage system (BESS) capacity investment in a nodal
electricity market. The structure is bilevel. At the lower level, an
independent system operator (ISO) clears a fixed-demand DC spot market and
minimizes the physical cost of generation and BESS operation. At the upper
level, each investor chooses nodal power and energy capacity anticipating that
market clearing.

The presentation below follows that order: the lower-level linear program
(LLP) is stated first, its Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) conditions are derived
next, and the upper-level investor problem and the resulting EPEC are stated
last.

The formulation uses fixed demand, exact nodal and system balances, and an
exact representation of lower-level optimality. It contains no strategic
operating bids, load shedding, dispatch regularization, leader-side price
penalty, proximal term, Tikhonov term, minimum-norm price selector, or relaxed
complementarity condition. During an investor's best response, rival
capacities are fixed parameters; the capacity profile is updated outside the
mathematical program by diagonalization.

\paragraph{Notation conventions.}
Sums over several indices run over the product of the corresponding sets, so
$\sum_{g,t}$ abbreviates $\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}$.
Quantifiers are abbreviated in the same way: $\forall g,t$ means
$\forall g\in\mathcal G,\ t\in\mathcal T$. Dual variables are stated in
parentheses to the right of the constraint they price. All multipliers of
inequality constraints are nonnegative; the sign convention and its relation
to the implementation are made explicit in Section~\ref{sec:kkt}.

\section{Nomenclature}

Power, energy, and monetary quantities are measured in MW, MWh, and EUR,
respectively. Because each dispatch period lasts one hour, every multiplier of
a power or energy constraint carries the unit EUR/MWh.

\section{Lower level: market clearing}
\label{sec:llp}

The capacity profile $X=(X^P,X^E)$ of all investors enters the lower level as
a fixed parameter. Collecting the primal decisions in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:llp-variables}
\Xi^{\mathrm{LL}}
:=\left\{P_{g,t},\ p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t},\ p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t},\
e_{j,n,\tau},\ z_{n,t}\right\},
\end{equation}
the ISO minimizes the physical cost of serving demand,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:llp-objective}
\min_{\Xi^{\mathrm{LL}}}\quad
Z^P:=\sum_{g,t}C_gP_{g,t}
+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j,n,t}d_j
\bigl(p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}+p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}\bigr),
\end{equation}
subject to
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:llp-constraints}
\begin{align}
\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_n}P_{g,t}
+\sum_j\bigl(p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}-p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}\bigr)
-z_{n,t}
&=D_{n,t},
&& (\lambda_{n,t})
&& \forall n,t,
\label{eq:nodal-balance}\\
\sum_n z_{n,t}
&=0,
&& (\lambda^{\mathrm{sys}}_t)
&& \forall t,
\label{eq:system-balance}\\
P_{g,t}
&\leq\overline P_{g,t},
&& (\nu_{g,t})
&& \forall g,t,
\label{eq:generation-bound}\\
\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}
&\leq\overline F_l,
&& (\mu^+_{l,t})
&& \forall l,t,
\label{eq:line-forward}\\
-\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}
&\leq\overline F_l,
&& (\mu^-_{l,t})
&& \forall l,t,
\label{eq:line-reverse}\\
p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}
&\leq X^P_{j,n},
&& (\rho_{j,n,t})
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:charge-bound}\\
p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}
&\leq X^P_{j,n},
&& (\sigma_{j,n,t})
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:discharge-bound}\\
e_{j,n,t}-e_{j,n,t-1}
-\eta p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}
+\eta^{-1}p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}
&=0,
&& (\gamma_{j,n,t})
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:soc-transition}\\
e_{j,n,\tau}
&\leq X^E_{j,n},
&& (\delta_{j,n,\tau})
&& \forall j,n,\tau,
\label{eq:soc-bound}\\
e_{j,n,0}-e_{j,n,T}
&=0,
&& (\rho^{\mathrm{per}}_{j,n})
&& \forall j,n,
\label{eq:soc-periodicity}\\
P_{g,t},\ p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t},\
p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t},\ e_{j,n,\tau}
&\geq 0,
&&
&& \forall j,g,n,\tau,t.
\label{eq:llp-nonnegativity}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Constraint~\eqref{eq:nodal-balance} defines the net injection $z_{n,t}$ and
prices it at the LMP $\lambda_{n,t}$; \eqref{eq:system-balance} enforces the
system-wide balance required by the PTDF representation. Renewable
curtailment is implicit in \eqref{eq:generation-bound}, since $\overline
P_{g,t}$ is the hourly available output. The pair
\eqref{eq:line-forward}--\eqref{eq:line-reverse} states the symmetric line
limit $|f_{l,t}|\leq\overline F_l$ as two one-sided inequalities so that each
direction carries its own nonnegative multiplier. Charging and discharging are
bounded by the installed power capacity in
\eqref{eq:charge-bound}--\eqref{eq:discharge-bound}, and the stored energy
follows the round-trip-efficiency transition \eqref{eq:soc-transition} within
the installed energy capacity \eqref{eq:soc-bound}. Condition
\eqref{eq:soc-periodicity} imposes a periodic daily cycle; the model does not
prescribe a fixed initial state of charge.

Problem \eqref{eq:llp-objective}--\eqref{eq:llp-constraints} is a linear
program in $\Xi^{\mathrm{LL}}$ for any fixed $X$. It is feasible whenever
demand can be served, and bounded because all cost coefficients are
nonnegative and all primal variables are either bounded or determined by the
balances. Its KKT conditions are therefore necessary and sufficient for
optimality.

\section{Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions of the lower level}
\label{sec:kkt}

Attach the multipliers listed in \eqref{eq:llp-constraints} and form the
Lagrangian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagrangian}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L
={}&
\sum_{g,t}C_gP_{g,t}
+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j,n,t}d_j
\bigl(p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}+p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}\bigr)\\
&-\sum_{n,t}\lambda_{n,t}
\Bigl[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_n}P_{g,t}
+\sum_j\bigl(p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}-p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}\bigr)
-z_{n,t}-D_{n,t}
\Bigr]
-\sum_t\lambda^{\mathrm{sys}}_t\sum_n z_{n,t}\\
&+\sum_{g,t}\nu_{g,t}
\bigl(P_{g,t}-\overline P_{g,t}\bigr)
+\sum_{l,t}\mu^+_{l,t}
\Bigl(\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}-\overline F_l\Bigr)
+\sum_{l,t}\mu^-_{l,t}
\Bigl(-\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}-\overline F_l\Bigr)\\
&+\sum_{j,n,t}\rho_{j,n,t}
\bigl(p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}-X^P_{j,n}\bigr)
+\sum_{j,n,t}\sigma_{j,n,t}
\bigl(p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}-X^P_{j,n}\bigr)
+\sum_{j,n,\tau}\delta_{j,n,\tau}
\bigl(e_{j,n,\tau}-X^E_{j,n}\bigr)\\
&-\sum_{j,n,t}\gamma_{j,n,t}
\Bigl[
e_{j,n,t}-e_{j,n,t-1}
-\eta p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}
+\eta^{-1}p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}
\Bigr]
-\sum_{j,n}\rho^{\mathrm{per}}_{j,n}
\bigl[e_{j,n,0}-e_{j,n,T}\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Multipliers of the two equality balances enter with a negative sign so that
$\lambda_{n,t}=\partial Z^{P\star}/\partial D_{n,t}$ is the marginal cost of
nodal demand, that is, the LMP in its usual positive orientation. Multipliers
of the inequality constraints, which are all written in the form
$g(\Xi^{\mathrm{LL}})\leq 0$, enter with a positive sign and are therefore
nonnegative.

The KKT system consists of primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and
complementary slackness.

\subsection{Primal feasibility}

Primal feasibility is \eqref{eq:llp-constraints} itself, that is, the
balances \eqref{eq:nodal-balance}--\eqref{eq:system-balance}, the transition
and periodicity conditions
\eqref{eq:soc-transition} and \eqref{eq:soc-periodicity}, the bounds
\eqref{eq:generation-bound}--\eqref{eq:soc-bound}, and the nonnegativity
restrictions \eqref{eq:llp-nonnegativity}.

\subsection{Dual feasibility}

Dual feasibility has two parts: sign restrictions on the multipliers and
stationarity of the Lagrangian.

\paragraph{Sign restrictions.}
Multipliers of inequality constraints are nonnegative and multipliers of
equality constraints are free:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:dual-signs}
\begin{align}
\nu_{g,t},\ \mu^+_{l,t},\ \mu^-_{l,t},\
\rho_{j,n,t},\ \sigma_{j,n,t},\ \delta_{j,n,\tau}
&\geq 0,
&& \forall j,g,l,n,\tau,t,
\label{eq:nonnegative-duals}\\
\lambda_{n,t},\ \lambda^{\mathrm{sys}}_t,\
\gamma_{j,n,t},\ \rho^{\mathrm{per}}_{j,n}
&\in\mathbb R,
&& \forall j,n,t.
\label{eq:free-duals}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

\paragraph{Stationarity.}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:lagrangian} with respect to each primal variable
gives the reduced costs
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:reduced-costs}
\begin{align}
\pi^{\mathrm{g}}_{g,t}
&:=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial P_{g,t}}
=C_g-\lambda_{n(g),t}+\nu_{g,t},
&& \forall g,t,
\label{eq:rc-generation}\\
\pi^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}
&:=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}}
=\tfrac{d_j}{2}+\lambda_{n,t}+\rho_{j,n,t}+\eta\gamma_{j,n,t},
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:rc-charge}\\
\pi^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}
&:=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}}
=\tfrac{d_j}{2}-\lambda_{n,t}+\sigma_{j,n,t}-\eta^{-1}\gamma_{j,n,t},
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:rc-discharge}\\
\pi^{\mathrm{e}}_{j,n,\tau}
&:=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial e_{j,n,\tau}}
=\delta_{j,n,\tau}-\gamma_{j,n,\tau}+\gamma_{j,n,\tau+1}
-\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{\tau=0\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{\tau=T\}}\bigr)
\rho^{\mathrm{per}}_{j,n},
&& \forall j,n,\tau,
\label{eq:rc-soc}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the boundary convention $\gamma_{j,n,0}=\gamma_{j,n,T+1}=0$ makes
\eqref{eq:rc-soc} valid for every $\tau\in\mathcal T_0$. Because the four
variables in \eqref{eq:reduced-costs} are sign restricted by
\eqref{eq:llp-nonnegativity}, stationarity takes the inequality form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationarity-inequalities}
\pi^{\mathrm{g}}_{g,t}\geq 0,
\qquad
\pi^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}\geq 0,
\qquad
\pi^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}\geq 0,
\qquad
\pi^{\mathrm{e}}_{j,n,\tau}\geq 0,
\qquad \forall j,g,n,\tau,t.
\end{equation}
The net injection $z_{n,t}$ is a free variable, so its stationarity condition
is an equality:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationarity-injection}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial z_{n,t}}
=\lambda_{n,t}-\lambda^{\mathrm{sys}}_t
+\sum_l H_{l,n}\bigl(\mu^+_{l,t}-\mu^-_{l,t}\bigr)
=0,
\qquad \forall n,t.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:stationarity-injection} is the price-decomposition
identity: the LMP equals a uniform system price plus the congestion component
transmitted through the PTDF matrix.

\subsection{Complementary slackness}

Each inequality is complementary to its multiplier. Writing
$0\leq a\perp b\geq 0$ for $a\geq 0$, $b\geq 0$, $ab=0$, the complementarity
conditions are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:complementarity}
\begin{align}
0\leq P_{g,t}
&\perp \pi^{\mathrm{g}}_{g,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall g,t,
\label{eq:cs-generation-variable}\\
0\leq \overline P_{g,t}-P_{g,t}
&\perp \nu_{g,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall g,t,
\label{eq:cs-generation-bound}\\
0\leq \overline F_l-\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}
&\perp \mu^+_{l,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall l,t,
\label{eq:cs-line-forward}\\
0\leq \overline F_l+\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}
&\perp \mu^-_{l,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall l,t,
\label{eq:cs-line-reverse}\\
0\leq p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}
&\perp \pi^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:cs-charge-variable}\\
0\leq X^P_{j,n}-p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{j,n,t}
&\perp \rho_{j,n,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:cs-charge-bound}\\
0\leq p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}
&\perp \pi^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:cs-discharge-variable}\\
0\leq X^P_{j,n}-p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{j,n,t}
&\perp \sigma_{j,n,t}\geq 0,
&& \forall j,n,t,
\label{eq:cs-discharge-bound}\\
0\leq e_{j,n,\tau}
&\perp \pi^{\mathrm{e}}_{j,n,\tau}\geq 0,
&& \forall j,n,\tau,
\label{eq:cs-soc-variable}\\
0\leq X^E_{j,n}-e_{j,n,\tau}
&\perp \delta_{j,n,\tau}\geq 0,
&& \forall j,n,\tau.
\label{eq:cs-soc-bound}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
No complementarity condition is attached to $z_{n,t}$, to the two balances, or
to \eqref{eq:soc-transition} and \eqref{eq:soc-periodicity}, because those
variables are free and those constraints are equalities.

Conditions \eqref{eq:llp-constraints},
\eqref{eq:dual-signs}--\eqref{eq:stationarity-injection}, and
\eqref{eq:complementarity} are jointly necessary and sufficient for optimality
of the lower-level LP.

\subsection{Disjunctive linearization of the complementarity conditions}
\label{sec:bigm}

Each product in \eqref{eq:complementarity} is bilinear. Every pair
$0\leq a\perp b\geq 0$ is replaced by the exact disjunction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bigm}
a\leq M^{\mathrm{s}}\beta,
\qquad
b\leq M^{\mathrm{d}}\bigl(1-\beta\bigr),
\qquad
\beta\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
with one binary per pair. The slack bound $M^{\mathrm{s}}$ is set
constraint-wise from data: $\overline P_{g,t}$ for generation,
$2\overline F_l$ for line flows, the nodal power headroom for charging and
discharging, and the corresponding energy headroom for the state of charge.
The dual bound $M^{\mathrm{d}}$ is a single user-set parameter and must be
large enough to contain all optimal multipliers; it is the only
approximation-prone element of the formulation and is verified ex post by
checking that no multiplier attains its bound. Because the reduced costs
\eqref{eq:reduced-costs} and the slacks are linear in the primal variables and
the multipliers, \eqref{eq:bigm} keeps the lower-level representation linear
and turns the best-response problem into a mixed-integer program.

\subsection{Equivalent strong-duality representation}
\label{sec:strong-duality}

For a linear program the KKT system can be replaced by primal feasibility,
dual feasibility, and a zero duality gap. From the constant terms of
\eqref{eq:lagrangian}, the dual objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-objective}
Z^D
:=\sum_{n,t}D_{n,t}\lambda_{n,t}
-\sum_{g,t}\overline P_{g,t}\nu_{g,t}
-\sum_{l,t}\overline F_l\bigl(\mu^+_{l,t}+\mu^-_{l,t}\bigr)
-\sum_{j,n,t}X^P_{j,n}\bigl(\rho_{j,n,t}+\sigma_{j,n,t}\bigr)
-\sum_{j,n,\tau}X^E_{j,n}\delta_{j,n,\tau},
\end{equation}
and lower-level optimality is imposed by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-duality}
Z^P=Z^D.
\end{equation}
Replacing \eqref{eq:complementarity} by \eqref{eq:strong-duality} avoids
binaries and yields a nonconvex nonlinear program instead of a mixed-integer
program. Both representations describe the same solution set. The maintained
implementation solves the strong-duality form by default and uses the
Big-M form of Section~\ref{sec:bigm} as an independent cross-check.

\subsection{Sign convention used in the implementation}

The derivation above uses the textbook convention in which all inequality
multipliers are nonnegative, which answers the sign question directly: with
the constraints written as $g(\Xi^{\mathrm{LL}})\leq 0$ in a minimization,
$\nu,\mu^+,\mu^-,\rho,\sigma,\delta\geq 0$. The code stores the negated
multipliers of the upper bounds, so that reported values satisfy
$\tilde\nu,\tilde\mu^{+},\tilde\rho,\tilde\sigma,\tilde\delta\leq 0$. The
translation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-map}
\tilde\nu=-\nu,
\qquad
\tilde\mu^{+}=-\mu^{+},
\qquad
\tilde\mu^{-}=\mu^{-},
\qquad
\tilde\rho=-\rho,
\qquad
\tilde\sigma=-\sigma,
\qquad
\tilde\delta=-\delta,
\end{equation}
while $\lambda$, $\lambda^{\mathrm{sys}}$, $\gamma$, and
$\rho^{\mathrm{per}}$ are unchanged. Under \eqref{eq:sign-map} the
stationarity conditions \eqref{eq:stationarity-inequalities} become the
upper-bound inequalities coded in the model, for example
$\lambda_{n(g),t}+\tilde\nu_{g,t}\leq C_g$, and \eqref{eq:dual-objective}
becomes an expression with uniformly positive signs.

\section{Upper level: investor problem}

Let $X_i=(X_i^P,X_i^E)$ denote investor $i$'s capacity decision and let
$X_{-i}$ collect the fixed capacities of its rivals. Feasible investment
decisions satisfy
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:investment}
\begin{align}
0
&\leq X^P_{i,n}
\leq \overline X_n-\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathcal I\\j\neq i}}X^P_{j,n},
&& \forall n,
\label{eq:headroom}\\
r^{\min}X^P_{i,n}
&\leq X^E_{i,n}
\leq r^{\max}X^P_{i,n},
&& \forall n.
\label{eq:duration}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Constraint~\eqref{eq:headroom} is the shared nodal connection limit, which is
the only direct coupling between investors at the upper level;
\eqref{eq:duration} restricts the admissible storage duration.

The capital-recovery factor and daily annualized investment cost are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:capital-cost}
\begin{align}
\operatorname{CRF}_i
&=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{w_i(1+w_i)^{L_i}}{(1+w_i)^{L_i}-1},
& w_i>0,\\[1.1ex]
\displaystyle \frac{1}{L_i},
& w_i=0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:crf}\\
K_i
&=\frac{\operatorname{CRF}_i}{365.25}
\sum_n
\left(c_i^P X^P_{i,n}+c_i^E X^E_{i,n}\right).
\label{eq:daily-capital-cost}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Investor $i$ maximizes the daily profit
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:profit}
\begin{align}
\Pi_i
&=R_i^{\mathrm{BESS}}+R_i^{\mathrm{gen}}
-C_i^{\mathrm{deg}}-K_i,
\label{eq:profit-total}\\
R_i^{\mathrm{BESS}}
&=\sum_{n,t}
\lambda_{n,t}
\left(p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{i,n,t}-p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{i,n,t}\right),
\label{eq:storage-revenue}\\
R_i^{\mathrm{gen}}
&=\sum_{g,t}
s_{i,g}\left(\lambda_{n(g),t}-C_g\right)P_{g,t},
\label{eq:generation-rent}\\
C_i^{\mathrm{deg}}
&=\frac{d_i}{2}
\sum_{n,t}
\left(p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{i,n,t}+p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{i,n,t}\right).
\label{eq:degradation-cost}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
For a stand-alone merchant, $s_{i,g}=0$ for every $g$. A portfolio investor
additionally internalizes the spot-market rent of its owned generation shares.
The dispatch quantities and the prices in \eqref{eq:profit} are lower-level
outcomes, so the upper level cannot be evaluated without the lower level.

\section{Capacity-only EPEC}

Let $y:=\Xi^{\mathrm{LL}}$ and $u:=\Xi^{\mathrm{D}}$ collect the lower-level
primal and dual variables. Given $X_{-i}$, investor $i$ solves the
single-level mathematical program with equilibrium constraints (MPEC)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:best-response}
\begin{aligned}
\underset{X_i^P,X_i^E,y,u}{\operatorname{maximize}}
\quad & \Pi_i\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&\eqref{eq:investment},\
\eqref{eq:llp-constraints},\
\eqref{eq:dual-signs}\text{--}\eqref{eq:stationarity-injection},\
\eqref{eq:complementarity},
\end{aligned}
\qquad \text{given }X_{-i},
\end{equation}
in which the complementarity block \eqref{eq:complementarity} may be replaced
either by its Big-M form \eqref{eq:bigm} or by the strong-duality condition
\eqref{eq:strong-duality}. Products between endogenous capacities and dual
variables in \eqref{eq:dual-objective}, together with LMP settlement in
\eqref{eq:profit}, make the best-response problem nonconvex.

Define $\operatorname{BR}_i(X_{-i})$ as investor $i$'s best-response
correspondence. A capacity profile $X^\star$ is an EPEC equilibrium if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epec}
X_i^\star\in\operatorname{BR}_i(X_{-i}^\star),
\qquad \forall i\in\mathcal I.
\end{equation}
The implementation searches for a fixed point using simultaneous
Gauss--Jacobi updates. At sweep $k$, all best responses are computed against
the same frozen capacity profile:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:jacobi}
\begin{align}
\widehat X_i^k
&\in\operatorname{BR}_i(X_{-i}^k),
&& \forall i\in\mathcal I,
\label{eq:raw-best-response}\\
X_i^{k+1}
&=(1-\omega)X_i^k+\omega\widehat X_i^k,
&& \forall i\in\mathcal I.
\label{eq:damped-update}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Damping affects the applied iterate but not the equilibrium condition.
Accordingly, convergence is assessed from the undamped residual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-residual}
r^k
:=\max_{i\in\mathcal I}
\left\lVert\widehat X_i^k-X_i^k\right\rVert_\infty,
\end{equation}
with separate implementation tolerances for power and energy capacity.

Because a fixed-capacity LP can admit multiple dual-optimal LMP vectors, the
formulation uses an optimistic convention: the embedded dual vector may be
selected in the active investor's favour. No secondary minimum-norm or other
price-selection rule is imposed. Consequently, embedded investor-specific LMPs
should be distinguished from prices obtained by an independent common-market
re-clear.

\section{Implementation}

The maintained implementation is organized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{primal\_market\_clearing\_model.py}: lower-level primal LP
      \eqref{eq:llp-objective}--\eqref{eq:llp-constraints};
\item \texttt{dual\_market\_clearing\_model.py}: explicit lower-level dual LP
      \eqref{eq:dual-objective};
\item \texttt{mpec\_strong\_duality.py}: best-response MPEC with
      \eqref{eq:strong-duality};
\item \texttt{mpec\_kkt\_bigm.py}: best-response MILP with the Big-M
      complementarity form \eqref{eq:bigm};
\item \texttt{jacobi\_diagonalization.py}: simultaneous multi-investor updates
      \eqref{eq:jacobi};
\item \texttt{run\_model.py}: solver settings, convergence, output, and resume.
\end{itemize}

\section{Test system}
\label{sec:test-system}

The numerical study uses a calibrated version of the IEEE 9-bus system shown
in Figure~\ref{fig:ieee9}, with the generation portfolio of
Table~\ref{tab:ieee9-generation}. Three dispatchable units sit at Buses~1--3
and three renewable units are added: wind at Bus~1 and photovoltaics at
Buses~6 and~8. Demand is concentrated at Buses~5, 6, and~8.

The network is deliberately calibrated so that congestion is occasional rather
than permanent. The lines of the outer ring (L14, L45, L57, L72, L93) are
rated at 400~MW and are effectively non-binding, whereas the two renewable
export corridors L46 and L69 are rated at 180~MW and the corridors L78 and L98
at 210~MW. These four lines are the only ones that bind, and they do so in the
midday hours when photovoltaic output at Buses~6 and~8 exceeds local demand.
The result is a price pattern in which the renewable-rich nodes separate from
the thermally priced nodes for part of the day, which is what makes nodal BESS
siting economically meaningful.
