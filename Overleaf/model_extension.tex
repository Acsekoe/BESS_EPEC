\section{Model Layers and Reading Guide}

The formulation is deliberately separated into an economic core and optional
numerical devices. This distinction is important because a penalty that helps
diagonalization converge is not part of the unregularized economic game.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.21\textwidth}p{0.70\textwidth}}
\hline
\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Contents} \\
\hline
Core shared model & Fixed-demand PTDF market clearing, generation and network
limits, BESS physics, endogenous MW/MWh investment, shared nodal access limits,
LMP settlement, physical degradation, CAPEX, and portfolio generation rent. \\
Capacity-only case & Investors choose nodal MW and MWh; every installed MW is
available to the ISO at the physical degradation coefficient. \\
Strategic-operation case & Investors additionally choose hourly charge and
discharge quantity offers and, in the two-sided-price version, charging buy bids
and discharging sell offers. \\
Optional numerical layer & Lower-level quadratic dispatch tie-break, proximal
strategy penalty, direct squared-price epsilon selector, damping, projection,
and canonicalization. These devices are identified explicitly below and are
zero or absent in the core model. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The core capacity-only and strategic-operation games are obtained by setting
the dispatch coefficient \(\kappa^{disp}=0\), the proximal coefficient
\(\kappa_k^{prox}=0\), and the direct price coefficient \(\epsilon=0\).
Price and dual bounds remain explicit finite implementation bounds and are not
objective penalties.

\section{Components Shared by Both EPECs}

\subsection{Market physics}

Let \(y=(P,p^{ch},p^{dis},e,z)\) collect the lower-level variables. Both models use fixed demand, no load shedding, and the same PTDF DC network and storage equations:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:common-market}
\begin{align}
z_{n,t}
&=\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_n}P_{g,t}
 +\sum_i\left(p^{dis}_{i,n,t}-p^{ch}_{i,n,t}\right)-D_{n,t},
&&\forall n,t, \label{eq:nodal-injection}\\
\sum_n z_{n,t}&=0,
&&\forall t, \label{eq:system-balance}\\
0\le P_{g,t}&\le\overline P_{g,t},
&&\forall g,t, \label{eq:generation-bounds}\\
-\overline F_l\le\sum_n H_{l,n}z_{n,t}&\le\overline F_l,
&&\forall l,t, \label{eq:line-bounds}\\
e_{i,n,t}&=e_{i,n,t-1}+\eta p^{ch}_{i,n,t}
-\eta^{-1}p^{dis}_{i,n,t},
&&\forall i,n,t, \label{eq:soc-balance}\\
0\le e_{i,n,\tau}&\le X^E_{i,n},
&&\forall i,n,\tau, \label{eq:soc-bounds}\\
e_{i,n,0}&=e_{i,n,|\mathcal T|},
&&\forall i,n. \label{eq:cyclic-soc}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The model-specific charging and discharging bounds and the market objective are given separately below.
There is no fixed initial state of charge; only daily periodicity is imposed.
There is also no binary restriction excluding simultaneous charge and discharge.
Efficiency losses, submitted bids, and physical operating costs normally make
such a loop unattractive; any residual simultaneous operation is reported.

\subsection{Investment feasibility and investor profit}

Each best response takes rival capacities as fixed. The active investor's feasible capacity set is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:capacity-feasibility}
\begin{align}
0\le X^P_{i,n}&\le \overline X_n-\sum_{j\ne i}X^P_{j,n},
&&\forall n, \label{eq:private-headroom}\\
r^{min}X^P_{i,n}\le X^E_{i,n}&\le r^{max}X^P_{i,n},
&&\forall n. \label{eq:duration-envelope}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Thus every jointly feasible outcome satisfies \(\sum_iX^P_{i,n}\le\overline X_n\). The daily economic profit used in both models is
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:investor-profit}
\begin{align}
\Pi_i
&=R_i^{BESS}+R_i^{gen}-C_i^{deg}-K_i, \label{eq:profit}\\
R_i^{BESS}
&=\sum_{n,t}\pi_{n,t}
\left(p^{dis}_{i,n,t}-p^{ch}_{i,n,t}\right), \label{eq:bess-revenue}\\
R_i^{gen}
&=\sum_{g,t}s_{i,g}
\left(\pi_{n(g),t}-C_g\right)P_{g,t}, \label{eq:generation-rent}\\
C_i^{deg}
&=\frac{d_i}{2}\sum_{n,t}
\left(p^{ch}_{i,n,t}+p^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right), \label{eq:degradation}\\
K_i
&=\frac{CRF_i}{365.25}\sum_n
\left(c^P_iX^P_{i,n}+c^E_iX^E_{i,n}\right), \label{eq:daily-capex}\\
CRF_i
&=\frac{r_i(1+r_i)^{L_i}}{(1+r_i)^{L_i}-1}. \label{eq:crf}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here \(\pi_{n,t}=\lambda_{n,t}\) under nodal settlement and \(\pi_{n,t}=\lambda^{sys}_t\) under the optional single-zone settlement. Stand-alone merchants have \(s_{i,g}=0\); portfolio investors internalize their owned generators' spot rents.

\subsection{Lower-level embedding}

For fixed upper-level decisions, each market-clearing problem below is a linear
program when \(\kappa^{disp}=0\) and a convex quadratic program when
\(\kappa^{disp}>0\). In investor \(i\)'s MPEC it is replaced by primal
feasibility, dual feasibility/stationarity, and strong duality:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mpec-embedding}
\operatorname{PF}(y;x_i,x_{-i}),\qquad
\operatorname{DF}(u;x_i,x_{-i}),\qquad
Z^{P}(y;x_i,x_{-i})=Z^{D}(u;x_i,x_{-i}),
\end{equation}
where \(u\) collects the lower-level dual variables. This is the implemented
primal--dual reformulation; no explicit complementarity products are added.
Section~\ref{sec:kkt-details} writes out the stationarity and dual-objective
terms that differ between the two cases. Because the investor maximizes over a
lower-level primal--dual optimum, a non-singleton optimal face is resolved
optimistically in the investor's favour. This price-selection convention is
part of the MPEC interpretation, not a regularization term.

\section{Model 1: Capacity-Only EPEC}

In the capacity-only model investor \(i\) chooses only installed MW and MWh,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-strategy}
x_i^{C}=\left\{X^P_{i,n},X^E_{i,n}\right\}_{n\in\mathcal N}.
\end{equation}
The ISO retains full control of storage dispatch within installed power:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-dispatch-bounds}
0\le p^{ch}_{i,n,t}\le X^P_{i,n},\qquad
0\le p^{dis}_{i,n,t}\le X^P_{i,n},
\qquad\forall i,n,t.
\end{equation}
Given the capacities, the ISO solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-iso}
\min_{y}\quad
Z_C^P=
\sum_{g,t}C_gP_{g,t}
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,n,t}d_i
\left(p^{ch}_{i,n,t}+p^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right)
+R^{disp}(y)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:common-market},\ \eqref{eq:capacity-dispatch-bounds}.
\end{equation}
The core capacity-only model sets \(R^{disp}(y)=0\). The optional expression
and its interpretation are given in Section~\ref{sec:dispatch-regularizer}.
Investor \(i\)'s capacity-only MPEC is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capacity-mpec}
\boxed{
\max_{x_i^C,y,u}\ \Pi_i
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:capacity-feasibility},\
\operatorname{PF}_C,\operatorname{DF}_C,\ Z_C^P=Z_C^D
}
\qquad\text{given }x_{-i}^C.
\end{equation}
Storage bids are not upper-level decisions in this model. The degradation term in \eqref{eq:capacity-iso} therefore guides ISO dispatch, while \eqref{eq:degradation} records the same physical cost in private profit; these are two appearances of one cost, not two charges to the investor.

\section{Model 2: Strategic-Operation EPEC}

The strategic-operation model adds hourly charge/discharge quantities and two-sided prices to the investment decision:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-strategy}
x_i^{S}=\left\{X^P_{i,n},X^E_{i,n},
q^{ch}_{i,n,t},q^{dis}_{i,n,t},
b^{ch}_{i,n,t},b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\right\}_{n,t}.
\end{equation}
The submitted quantities must be feasible, and the ISO may accept any dispatch inside them:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:strategic-quantity}
\begin{align}
0\le q^{ch}_{i,n,t}&\le X^P_{i,n}, &
0\le q^{dis}_{i,n,t}&\le X^P_{i,n}, &&\forall i,n,t,\label{eq:offer-capacity}\\
0\le p^{ch}_{i,n,t}&\le q^{ch}_{i,n,t}, &
0\le p^{dis}_{i,n,t}&\le q^{dis}_{i,n,t}, &&\forall i,n,t.\label{eq:offer-dispatch}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
With the symmetric price bound \(\overline b\), the two-sided prices satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-price-feasibility}
-\overline b\le b^{ch}_{i,n,t},b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\le\overline b,
\qquad
b^{dis}_{i,n,t}\ge\frac{b^{ch}_{i,n,t}}{\eta^2},
\qquad\forall i,n,t.
\end{equation}
The second inequality prevents bids that reward an instantaneous charge--discharge loss cycle. A charging bid is a willingness to pay and consequently enters market cost with a negative sign; a discharging offer enters with a positive sign. The ISO problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-iso}
\min_y\quad
Z_S^P=
\sum_{g,t}C_gP_{g,t}
+\sum_{i,n,t}\left(
b^{dis}_{i,n,t}p^{dis}_{i,n,t}
-b^{ch}_{i,n,t}p^{ch}_{i,n,t}
\right)
+R^{disp}(y)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:common-market},\ \eqref{eq:offer-dispatch}.
\end{equation}
Again, \(R^{disp}(y)=0\) in the core strategic-operation game.
Submitted prices replace degradation coefficients only in the ISO objective. Physical degradation remains \eqref{eq:degradation} in realized investor profit. The quantity-only variant is recovered by fixing truthful prices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantity-only-prices}
b^{ch}_{i,n,t}=-\frac{d_i}{2},
\qquad
b^{dis}_{i,n,t}=\frac{d_i}{2},
\qquad\forall i,n,t.
\end{equation}

The strong-duality term associated with the two dispatch upper bounds changes because their right-hand sides are offers rather than installed power:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-dual-change}
\underbrace{X^P_{i,n}\left(\rho_{i,n,t}+\sigma_{i,n,t}\right)}_{\text{capacity-only}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{q^{ch}_{i,n,t}\rho_{i,n,t}
+q^{dis}_{i,n,t}\sigma_{i,n,t}}_{\text{strategic operation}}.
\end{equation}

The strategic-operation MPEC is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strategic-mpec}
\boxed{
\max_{x_i^S,y,u}\
\Pi_i-\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}-\Phi_i^{\epsilon}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:capacity-feasibility},\
\eqref{eq:strategic-quantity},\
\eqref{eq:strategic-price-feasibility},\
\operatorname{PF}_S,\operatorname{DF}_S,\ Z_S^P=Z_S^D
}
\qquad\text{given }x_{-i}^S.
\end{equation}
The economic core is recovered by setting both penalties to zero. Their exact
forms, units, and effects are separated from the model in
Section~\ref{sec:numerical-layer}.

\section{Explicit Lower-Level Optimality System}\label{sec:kkt-details}

This section makes the MPEC embedding explicit. Define the dispatch upper
bounds and the marginal storage coefficients seen by the ISO as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:case-map}
\begin{array}{c|cc|cc}
&\overline q^{ch}_{i,n,t}&\overline q^{dis}_{i,n,t}
&a^{ch}_{i,n,t}&a^{dis}_{i,n,t}\\
\hline
\text{capacity-only}
&X^P_{i,n}&X^P_{i,n}&d_i/2&d_i/2\\
\text{strategic prices and quantities}
&q^{ch}_{i,n,t}&q^{dis}_{i,n,t}&-b^{ch}_{i,n,t}&b^{dis}_{i,n,t}
\end{array}
\end{equation}
so that the storage part of the ISO objective is
\(\sum_{i,n,t}(a^{ch}_{i,n,t}p^{ch}_{i,n,t}
+a^{dis}_{i,n,t}p^{dis}_{i,n,t})\).

The implementation uses \(\nu_{g,t}\le0\) for the generation upper bound,
\(\mu^{up}_{l,t}\le0\) and \(\mu^{dn}_{l,t}\ge0\) for the line bounds,
\(\rho_{i,n,t}\le0\) and \(\sigma_{i,n,t}\le0\) for the charging and
discharging upper bounds, and \(\delta_{i,n,\tau}\le0\) for the SOC upper
bound. The balance, SOC-transition, and periodicity multipliers
\(\lambda_{n,t}\), \(\lambda_t^{sys}\), \(\gamma_{i,n,t}\), and
\(\rho^{per}_{i,n}\) are free. Because the corresponding primal variables are
nonnegative, their stationarity conditions take inequality form:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:stationarity}
\begin{align}
\lambda_{n(g),t}+\nu_{g,t}
&\le C_g+\kappa^{disp}P_{g,t}, &&\forall g,t,
\label{eq:stat-generation}\\
-\lambda_{n,t}+\rho_{i,n,t}-\eta\gamma_{i,n,t}
&\le a^{ch}_{i,n,t}+\kappa^{disp}p^{ch}_{i,n,t},
&&\forall i,n,t,\label{eq:stat-charge}\\
\lambda_{n,t}+\sigma_{i,n,t}+\eta^{-1}\gamma_{i,n,t}
&\le a^{dis}_{i,n,t}+\kappa^{disp}p^{dis}_{i,n,t},
&&\forall i,n,t,\label{eq:stat-discharge}\\
-\lambda_{n,t}+\lambda_t^{sys}
+\sum_lH_{l,n}(\mu^{up}_{l,t}+\mu^{dn}_{l,t})
&=\kappa^{disp}z_{n,t}, &&\forall n,t.
\label{eq:stat-injection}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
SOC stationarity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stat-soc}
\delta_{i,n,\tau}
+\mathbf 1_{\{\tau\in\mathcal T\}}\gamma_{i,n,\tau}
-\mathbf 1_{\{\tau+1\in\mathcal T\}}\gamma_{i,n,\tau+1}
+\mathbf 1_{\{\tau=0\}}\rho^{per}_{i,n}
-\mathbf 1_{\{\tau=|\mathcal T|\}}\rho^{per}_{i,n}
\le \kappa^{disp}e_{i,n,\tau}.
\end{equation}

For fixed demand, the dual objective used in strong duality is
\begin{align}
Z^D={}&
\sum_{n,t}D_{n,t}\lambda_{n,t}
+\sum_{g,t}\overline P_{g,t}\nu_{g,t}
+\sum_{l,t}\overline F_l(\mu^{up}_{l,t}-\mu^{dn}_{l,t})
\notag\\
&+\sum_{i,n,t}\left(
\overline q^{ch}_{i,n,t}\rho_{i,n,t}
+\overline q^{dis}_{i,n,t}\sigma_{i,n,t}\right)
+\sum_{i,n,\tau}X^E_{i,n}\delta_{i,n,\tau}
-R^{disp}(y).
\label{eq:dual-objective}
\end{align}
Primal feasibility, the sign restrictions and stationarity equations above,
and \(Z^P=Z^D\) characterize a lower-level optimum. Equation
\eqref{eq:case-map} shows exactly what changes between the capacity-only case
and the strategic operation case. Products such as \(X^P\rho\), \(q\rho\), and
\(b p\), together with LMP settlement revenue, make the upper-level MPEC
nonconvex even though the lower-level market problem is convex for fixed bids
and capacities.

The implemented electricity-price bounds are
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{n,t},\lambda_t^{sys}\in[-500,500]
\quad\text{\texteuro{}/MWh}.
\end{equation}
Other embedded duals are bounded in absolute value by 10,000. These are finite
numerical and price-selection bounds, not additions to either objective.

\section{Optional Regularizers and Algorithmic Safeguards}
\label{sec:numerical-layer}

None of the following objects should be silently interpreted as physical cost
or market design. Every reported experiment must state which coefficients were
active.

\subsection{Lower-level quadratic dispatch tie-break}
\label{sec:dispatch-regularizer}

The optional ISO-level term is
\begin{align}
R^{disp}(y)=\frac{\kappa^{disp}}{2}\Bigg[&
\sum_{g,t}P_{g,t}^2
+\sum_{i,n,t}\left((p^{ch}_{i,n,t})^2+(p^{dis}_{i,n,t})^2\right)
\notag\\
&+\sum_{i,n,\tau}e_{i,n,\tau}^2
+\sum_{n,t}z_{n,t}^2\Bigg].
\label{eq:dispatch-regularizer}
\end{align}
Its coefficient has units \(\text{\texteuro{}}/(\mathrm{MW}^2\mathrm{h})\).
It turns the lower-level LP into a convex QP and selects a primal dispatch among
otherwise equivalent solutions. It does not directly penalize investment,
withholding, or bid prices, and strict primal convexity does not in general
guarantee a unique dual price vector. The core model uses
\(\kappa^{disp}=0\). The term \(-R^{disp}(y)\) in
\eqref{eq:dual-objective} is the Wolfe-dual correction required by the
quadratic objective.

\subsection{Full-strategy proximal penalty}

Let a bar denote investor \(i\)'s reference strategy, normally its previous
iterate, and define withheld quantities
\begin{equation}
w^{ch}_{i,n,t}=X^P_{i,n}-q^{ch}_{i,n,t},\qquad
w^{dis}_{i,n,t}=X^P_{i,n}-q^{dis}_{i,n,t}.
\end{equation}
With energy scale \(h_E>0\), price scale \(s_b>0\), and
\(T=|\mathcal T|\), the exact implemented squared distance is
\begin{align}
\mathcal D_{i,k}={}&
\sum_n(X^P_{i,n}-\overline X^P_{i,n})^2
+\sum_n\left(\frac{X^E_{i,n}-\overline X^E_{i,n}}{h_E}\right)^2
\notag\\
&+\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{n,t}\left[
(w^{ch}_{i,n,t}-\overline w^{ch}_{i,n,t})^2
+(w^{dis}_{i,n,t}-\overline w^{dis}_{i,n,t})^2\right]
\notag\\
&+\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{n,t}\left[
\left(\frac{b^{ch}_{i,n,t}-\overline b^{ch}_{i,n,t}}{s_b}\right)^2
+\left(\frac{b^{dis}_{i,n,t}-\overline b^{dis}_{i,n,t}}{s_b}\right)^2
\right].
\label{eq:prox-distance}
\end{align}
The price line is omitted in quantity-only mode. The objective penalty is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prox-penalty}
\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}=\frac{\kappa_k^{prox}}{2}\mathcal D_{i,k}.
\end{equation}
The current scales are \(h_E=4\)~h and \(s_b=10\)~\texteuro{}/MWh. Measuring
\(X^P-q\) means that the term penalizes a \emph{change in withholding}; it does
not reward full availability and does not double-penalize offers when installed
power changes. A fixed coefficient can be used, or a staircase continuation
can set
\begin{equation}
\kappa_k^{prox}=\Delta\kappa
\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{m}\right\rfloor,
\end{equation}
where \(m\) is the number of sweeps in each block. A positive proximal
coefficient changes the best-response objective and therefore selects a
regularized fixed point; it is not evidence of a unique fixed point of the raw
game. It is excluded from the automatic Jacobi initializer.

\subsection{Direct squared-price epsilon selector}

When two-sided strategic prices are enabled, the optional price-only term is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epsilon-penalty}
\Phi_i^\epsilon=\epsilon\sum_{n,t}\left[
(b^{ch}_{i,n,t})^2+(b^{dis}_{i,n,t})^2\right].
\end{equation}
There is no factor one half. This selector directly affects only bid prices; it
contains no MW, MWh, or quantity-offer term. Even if its realized monetary
value is tiny, a run with \(\epsilon>0\) is not literally unregularized in bid
prices. Economic profit \(\Pi_i\) is always reported separately from both
\(\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}\) and \(\Phi_i^\epsilon\).

\subsection{Devices that are not objective regularizers}

For Jacobi damping, all best responses use the same frozen rival snapshot and
the applied update is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:damped-update}
x_i^{k+1}=(1-\alpha)x_i^k+\alpha\,BR_i(x_{-i}^k),
\qquad 0<\alpha\le1.
\end{equation}
Gauss--Seidel instead lets later investors observe earlier within-sweep
updates. Damping changes the iteration path, not the best-response objective.
Four parallel workers merely solve the four independent same-snapshot Jacobi
problems concurrently; they do not change the mathematical update.

The proportional shared-limit projection is a final feasibility safeguard for
an overloaded iterate. It preserves each affected battery's energy-to-power
ratio and scales its quantity offers consistently; it is not an access auction
or an equilibrium condition. The \(10^{-4}\) MW/MWh zero-capacity cleanup,
truthful-price assignment at zero offered quantity, slack-offer
canonicalization, and finite price/dual bounds are likewise numerical
normalizations or diagnostics rather than economic penalties.

\subsection{Recent 200 MW strategic Jacobi experiment}

The recent 100-iteration experiment used the following specialization:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.48\textwidth}p{0.34\textwidth}}
\hline
Nodal connection limit & \(\overline X_n=200\) MW \\
Update and workers & Jacobi, four workers \\
Damping & \(\alpha=0.25\) \\
Strategic quantities and prices & enabled \\
Dispatch regularizer & \(\kappa^{disp}=0\) \\
Fixed/staircase proximal penalty & both zero \\
Direct squared-price selector & \(\epsilon=10^{-6}\) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Consequently, the run had no dispatch or proximal regularization, but it did
have a very small direct bid-price selector. It reached the iteration limit
without convergence; stable aggregate physical investment should therefore be
described as a candidate, not as a converged strategic price-and-quantity
equilibrium.

\section{Equilibrium and Settlement}

For either model \(m\in\{C,S\}\), a generalized Nash equilibrium is a fixed point of the investor best responses:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epec-equilibrium}
x_i^{m,*}\in
\arg\max_{x_i^m\in\mathcal X_i(x_{-i}^{m,*})}
\left\{\Pi_i-\Phi_i^m\right\},
\qquad\forall i\in\mathcal I,
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal X_i\) contains the corresponding MPEC constraints and the
rival-dependent nodal headroom. For the economic core,
\(\Phi_i^C=\Phi_i^S=0\). In a declared regularized strategic experiment,
\(\Phi_i^S=\Phi^{prox}_{i,k}+\Phi_i^\epsilon\). The capacity-only model has no
upper-level proximal or epsilon penalty; only the optional lower-level dispatch
term can be enabled. Both implementations support damped Gauss--Seidel and
Gauss--Jacobi diagonalization. In strategic Gauss--Jacobi mode, one complete
snapshot of capacities, hourly quantity offers, and two-sided prices is frozen
for every investor in a sweep; all damped best-response proposals are then
applied simultaneously. Proportional projection onto the shared nodal capacity
limits is retained as a feasibility safeguard.

The fully simultaneous strategic Gauss--Jacobi implementation removes
artificial early- and late-mover effects within a sweep. It is therefore the
preferred symmetric algorithmic convention for a simultaneous investment game.
Its independent investor problems can be solved in separate processes. A
same-snapshot serial-versus-parallel check produced identical capacities,
hourly bids, objectives, termination states, strong-duality gaps, and joint
settlement price bounds. This algorithmic symmetry does not by itself guarantee
convergence or a unique equilibrium. Robustness must still be assessed across
common initial states and damping parameters, while projection events,
regularization terms, damped steps, and raw undamped best-response residuals
are reported explicitly.

After the final iterate, the ISO is cleared independently with all capacities and, for Model~2, all offers fixed. Reported prices and profits use this joint re-clear. Embedded MPEC prices are retained only as diagnostics because lower-level dual non-uniqueness permits optimistic price selection.
