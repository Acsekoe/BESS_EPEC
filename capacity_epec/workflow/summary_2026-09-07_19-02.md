## 2. Baseline and the concentration question

Matched energy-only baseline: `output/energy_only_linear_ipopt_final_20260907/`
(identical damping/proximal/multistart/solver settings, `afrr_enabled: false`).
Converged in the fixed-point sense at sweep 13; audit still failed at 68,005
EUR/day for I3.

Capacity I1 63.34 / I2 63.44 / I3 62.50 MW — a spread of **0.94 MW**, i.e. no
differentiation at all. Nodal capacity concentrated on N3/N6/N8/N9 with the
other five nodes pinned at an identical 0.396 MW.

**The concentration is real economics, not a numerical artifact.** Measured LMP
spread over the day:

| Node group | LMP range | Spread |
|---|---|---|
| N3, N6, N8, N9 | ~11–15 → 60 EUR/MWh | **45–49 EUR/MWh** |
| N1, N2, N4, N5, N7 | 52.2 → 60 EUR/MWh | **7.8 EUR/MWh** |

A ~6x difference in arbitrage opportunity. Confirmed by five prior variants
(no-proximal, decoupled/no shared cap, 40 MW node cap, quadratic cost, tight vs
loose solver budget) all reproducing the same four-node pattern.

### Per-node quadratic investment cost (new capability, tested, insufficient alone)

The pre-existing `--quadratic-cost-power-eur-per-mw2` curves on the **portfolio
total**, `0.5*qP*(sum_n X[n])^2`. Its gradient w.r.t. any node is
`qP * sum_m X[m]` — identical at every node — so it can only tax aggregate size
and **cannot break a siting tie**. That is why the earlier `qP2000_qE500`
attempt shrank totals and destabilised the run without moving the nodal split.

Added a genuine per-node convexifier, `0.5*qP*sum_n(X[n]^2)`, as
`quadratic_cost_power_per_node_eur_per_mw2` /
`quadratic_cost_energy_per_node_eur_per_mwh2` (additive, defaults 0, existing
runs bit-compatible). Verified: same totals split across two nodes cost 205.51
vs 209.51 EUR/day concentrated on one.

Run `output/percapacity_quad_qP50_qE50_noprox_nomultistart/` at qP=qE=50
(~11% marginal nudge at the observed 15 MW node scale): rebalanced *within* the
big four (I1 N8 17.23→16.81, N6 14.97→14.69) but moved **exactly zero** capacity
to the dead nodes (0.119 MW, identical to 7 significant figures). A cost-side
nudge cannot bridge a 6x revenue gap at any magnitude that does not also destroy
the good nodes' economics.

Side finding: loosening the solver budget from 1,000 iter/30 s to 3,000/120 s
turned nearly all sub-solves from `maxIterations` to certified `optimal`. The
earlier terminations were a starved solver, not a badly conditioned problem.

## 3. Root cause: a price-support knife edge

Probes at fixed baseline capacities (exact reclear + settle):

**I3 scaling only its own storage, rivals fixed:**

| I3 storage | I3 profit | delta | gen. rent | storage surplus | midday LMP N6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 62.5 MW (equilibrium) | 138,189 | — | 134,715 | 4,712 | 17.29 |
| **75.0 MW (+20%)** | 205,869 | **+67,680** | 207,355 | **~0** | **38.97** |
| 125 MW | 204,879 | +66,690 | 207,355 | ~0 | 38.97 |

I3 overbuilds storage **purely to prop up the midday price for its own PV** —
its storage arbitrage surplus collapses to zero, so the extra capacity earns
nothing as storage. Control: I1, owning no generation, only loses from the same
overbuild (−5,370 at 1.5x).

**+67,680 ≈ the 68,005 EUR/day audit gap present in every run.** The gap was
never a solver artifact. The best response sits on a discontinuity: below a
threshold nothing happens, above it the price regime flips and saturates. No
stabilizer can close a discontinuity, which is why proximal / multistart /
shared-cap / quadratic-cost / solver-budget changes all left it at ~68k.

Root cause of the discontinuity, in the model rather than the economics:
- Demand effectively perfectly inelastic — `demand_adjustment_penalty` 500
  EUR/MW^2 buys 0.12 MW of response per node at 60 EUR/MWh (~0.02% of demand).
- Only three thermal steps (52.2 / 60 / 67.15), so nothing offers between
  storage's charging value (~12–17) and the cheapest thermal (52.2).
- Zero RES curtailment: storage exactly absorbs the midday RES surplus, and
  that balance is what pins the low midday price.

## 4. Strategic RES withholding — assessed, not adopted

Measured on the unsmoothed model, capacities fixed:

| I3 withholds PV | I3 profit | delta | midday LMP N6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| w = 1.0000 | 138,189 | — | 17.29 |
| w = 0.9999 (0.01%) | 159,765 | +21,576 | 23.10 |
| w = 0.9990 (**0.1%**) | 206,095 | **+67,906** | 38.07 |
| w = 0.95 | 199,736 | +61,547 | 40.35 |

Withholding one tenth of one percent of PV was worth +67,906 EUR/day — the same
mechanism and magnitude as the storage channel above. Wind withholding was
strictly unprofitable for I2 at every level (−626 at w=0.98 to −73,652 at
w=0.30), because wind sits in flat 60 EUR/MWh hours. I1 goes to −1,163
(bankrupt) whenever I3 flips the regime.

Conclusion: economically sound but the wrong next step — the model already
contained the same market power, the lever was a knife edge driven by model
artifacts, and it would have produced one dominant player rather than a split.
Smooth price formation first. See [`summary.md`](summary.md).

the smoothed input; it belongs in a separate scenario file, and the write-up must
state that wind's market power was designed in rather than discovered.

~1.4 MW against a 0.5 MW tolerance with positions still moving monotonically —

Final profits: I1 195, I2 71,962, I3 107,662 EUR/day. I3's storage operating
surplus is 4,938 against 2,795 of capex — the 130 MW is a price-support
instrument protecting 105,519 EUR/day of owned-PV rent, not an arbitrage
business. I1 remains marginally profitable (409 surplus vs 214 capex), so it
shrinks to a small viable niche rather than exiting outright.

This reproduces, in capacity space and with no strategic-offer variable, the
structure recorded in [[portfolio-bidding-game]] for the offer-based game
(there: I4 107.2 > I3 43.8 > I1 41.1 MW, spread 66 MW against a 9.7 MW
storage-only baseline).

### Nodal dynamics: systematic capture, no oscillation

96% of final capacity (181.3 of 188.2 MW) sits at N6+N8 — exactly I3's PV nodes.

| Node | sweep 0 → 80 | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| N6 (`RES_PV_N6`) | 52.0 → 74.1 MW | I3 share 64% → **85%** |
| N8 (`RES_PV_N8`) | 68.1 → 107.2 MW | I3 ~63%; I2 crowds in 12.8 → 38.9 MW |
| N3 | 52.5 → 5.3 MW | abandoned by all |
| N9 | 15.2 → 1.6 MW | I2 peaked at 24 MW, then exited |
| N1/N2/N4/N5/N7 | ~0 | dead |

Live positions are strictly monotone over the last 10 sweeps (I3@N6 60.19 →
63.30; I3@N8 64.55 → 67.07; I2@N8 34.88 → 38.85; I2@N3 8.60 → 2.74). The only
non-monotone series are sub-2 MW residuals being wound down. No node-hopping.

### Next step

Damping 0.25 with a provably monotone, non-oscillatory dynamic — raising it to
~0.4–0.5 should converge much faster at low overshoot risk. Prefer a clean
symmetric-start run at `--max-solve-seconds 300` over extending the warm-started
chain, so the citable artifact is a single run.
