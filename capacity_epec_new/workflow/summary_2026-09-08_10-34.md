## The result in one paragraph

224 damped-Jacobi sweeps on the smoothed data drive **two of three investors to a
clean fixed point** and leave the third on a knife edge that does not resolve.
I1 and I2 finish with profitable deviations of **0.3** and **45.4 EUR/day** —
converged by any reasonable standard. I3 finishes with **12,235 EUR/day**, and
that deviation is only **0.86 MW** away from where it stands. Between sweep 110
and 224 I3 grew **130.4 → 182.0 MW (+39.6%)** and gained **+2.00% of profit**.
It is not converging slowly; it is being pulled along a discontinuity whose
gradient *steepens* as it grows. The next step is therefore not more sweeps but a
targeted 1-D experiment: **fix I1 and I2 at their converged values and scan I3
broadly.**

**That experiment was run this session and it worked** (§5). I3's conditional
optimum is a well-defined interior peak at **184.5 MW**; placing it there drops
the total profitable deviation from **12,281 to 985 EUR/day**, with I3's own
deviation falling from 12,235 to **5.0**. One 1-D scan achieved an order of
magnitude more than 224 Jacobi sweeps.

## 1. I1 and I2 are converged *in a conditional, relative sense* — the numbers

Read the heading precisely. I2 is at **0.064% relative regret**, but still has
**45.4 EUR/day absolute regret, a 0.734 MW power deviation and a 4.12 MWh energy
deviation** — economically close under a declared relative ε, not an exact fixed
point. And it is converged only **conditional on I3 staying where it is**:
moving I3 by 2.5 MW (§5) immediately raises I2's regret to 964 EUR/day. Neither
investor is at a Nash point; they are at best responses to a non-equilibrium I3.

Final audit at sweep 224 (`smoothed_continued_from_sweep174/final_audit.csv`):

| | I1 | I2 | I3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power | 1.00 MW | 31.15 MW | **182.01 MW** |
| Energy | 5.3 MWh | 125.7 MWh | 536.5 MWh |
| Profit EUR/day | 30.7 | 71,361.9 | 109,813.3 |
| **Profitable deviation** | **0.3** | **45.4** | **12,235.1** |
| deviation as % of profit | 0.0% | **0.06%** | **11.1%** |
| BR distance (power) | 0.069 MW | 0.734 MW | 0.860 MW |
| embedded−reclear gap | 0.006 | 0.493 | 0.262 |

I1 has effectively exited: 10.7 MW (s110) → **1.00 MW**, profit 195 → 30.7.
Its entire opportunity set is now worth 30.7 EUR/day. This is a fixed point,
not a transient.

I2 shed a third of its capacity at almost no cost: **47.2 → 31.15 MW (−34%)**
for a profit change of **71,962 → 71,362 (−0.8%)**. It discovered the capacity
was not earning its keep.

**REVISED — free-riding ended.** The sweep-110 note recorded I2 as abandoning
N3/N9 and crowding into N8 to free-ride on I3's supported prices. Over the
longer run it did the opposite: it vacated N8 entirely and returned to its own
nodes.

```
final siting (sweep 224)
  I3:  N6 95.82   N8 86.04   N3 0.15      (its own PV nodes)
  I2:  N4 24.82   N9  6.32   N8 ~0        (moved off I3's nodes)
  I1:  N6  1.00
```

**REVISED — I2 is not "free-riding at a stable share", and I1 is not
"marginalised but marginally profitable".** Both statements described a
sweep-110 snapshot that the trajectory has since passed through. I2 relocated;
I1 exited.

## 2. I3 drifts 130 → 182 MW for +2% profit — the plateau

| | sweep 110 | sweep 224 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| I3 power | 130.37 MW | 182.01 MW | **+39.6%** |
| I3 profit | 107,661.9 | 109,813.3 | **+2.00%** |
| I3 storage surplus | 4,938 | 5,886 | |
| I3 capex | 2,795 | 3,610 | |
| I3 owned-PV rent | 105,519 | 107,538 | +1.9% |

**The storage business nets 2,276 EUR/day against 107,538 of owned-PV rent — 2%
of the business.** A 40% capacity buildout bought a 2% profit gain. The
price-support reading from the earlier note is strengthened, not weakened.

### The plateau is real and it widens

I3's best response is genuinely indifferent across a wide set of nodal
allocations. Backing the raw best responses out of the damped state
(`BR = (x_{t+1} − 0.75·x_t)/0.25`) shows it flip-flopping between two
configurations at constant profit:

| | N3 | N6 | N8 | I3 BR profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| config A | 155–175 MWh | 205–230 | 85–105 | −2.2 EUR/day per sweep |
| config B | 87–103 MWh | 240–250 | 134–142 | −2.2 EUR/day per sweep |

Through sweeps ~146–174 this was confined to energy (~60 MWh wide). From sweep
175 it propagated into **power**: at sweep 179 the power BR swung 27 MW at N3
and 17 MW at N8, and the BR profit moved 122,168.4 → 122,166.2 → 122,164.0 — the
same smooth −2.2/sweep as every other sweep. **A 27 MW reallocation is worth
nothing.**

## 3. The criterion is broken — and this is the important finding

The distance test (`tolerance_mw 0.5`, `tolerance_mwh 1.0`) measures solver
tie-breaking on the plateau, not economics. Worse, at the end of the run the two
metrics **flatly contradict** each other:

- final raw power residual: **0.860 MW** — the lowest of all 224 sweeps
- I3's profitable deviation at that same point: **12,235 EUR/day**

**The defensible claim is exactly this: a small power residual can coexist with a
very large profitable deviation.** That alone shows capacity distance cannot
certify equilibrium.

⚠ An earlier draft of this note claimed a 1.0 MW tolerance would have declared
convergence here. **That was wrong** — the energy residual at sweep 224 is
**4.121 MWh**, above the 1.0 MWh tolerance, and the test requires both plus
`consecutive_sweeps` qualifying sweeps
([`capacity_game.py:951-952`](../model/capacity_game.py)). The genuine
near-miss is in the follow-up run below (power 0.449 MW, under tolerance),
not here.

**The zero-proximal audit is what catches this.** `equilibrium_candidate =
state.converged and report.passed`; the audit is the only thing standing between
a low residual and a false equilibrium claim. (Note it is redundant whenever
`proximal_penalty` is already 0 *and* the residual is obviously failing — as at
sweep 174 — because `audit_equilibrium` differs from a sweep only by
`replace(config, proximal_penalty=0.0)`. It becomes essential exactly when the
residual looks good.)

**Action: replace the distance test with an ε-equilibrium test on profitable
deviation.** Everything reported as "not converged" in this chain should be
re-stated against that criterion, under which I1 and I2 *have* converged.

### RETRACTED: "the gradient steepens as I3 grows"

An earlier draft divided profitable deviation by *max nodal power deviation* and
called the result a gradient in EUR/day per MW, reporting a 1.38× steepening
between sweeps 110 and 224. **That quantity is not a derivative and the
steepening claim is withdrawn.** The deviation it divides by also changes
energy capacity (up to 3.23 MWh at sweep 224), several nodal capacities, and
portfolio duration simultaneously — and the *identity* of the binding node
differs between the two sweeps, so the two ratios are not even commensurable.

At most it may be called "profit regret per maximum nodal MW displacement", and
it should not be compared across profiles. A real local sensitivity needs the
controlled perturbation experiment in §6.

(Separately: do **not** compare any of this to the pre-smoothing 68k figure —
that came from a large discrete ~26 MW overbuild on different data.)

## 4. Why more sweeps cannot settle it

I3 has been pulled upward monotonically for 114 sweeps, 130 → 182 MW, at a
near-constant +0.41 MW/sweep. **The pull has never once reversed.** Its best
response sits persistently ~0.9–1.7 MW ahead, worth five figures, and the
boundary sharpens as it grows.

There is also a real branch change in the chain, distinct from the plateau
noise, at sweeps ~190–205: I3 closed N3 (48.6 → 0.15 MW) and returned to a
two-node portfolio while I2 vacated N8. It cost I3 ~4,800 EUR/day of status-quo
profit and drove its gap from 9,285 back up to 14,027 — erasing 30 sweeps of
progress — before recovering to 12,235.

**The gap metric separates the two phenomena that the residual conflates:**

| event | power residual | gap movement | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| sweep 180 | 24.4 MW | 32 EUR/day | plateau noise |
| sweeps 190–205 | comparable | 763 → 4,800 EUR/day | real branch change |

This is the methodological case for the ε-criterion, and it is worth reporting
in its own right.

**Hypothesis raised and then REFUTED, same session.** The working hypothesis was
that no pure-strategy equilibrium exists in I3's capacity — a discontinuous
best-response correspondence with a persistent one-directional pull. The
conditional scan in §5 disproved it: I3's best response is a well-defined
interior maximum at **184.5 MW**. What was actually happening is simpler — I3
was crawling up a near-vertical cliff at 0.41 MW/sweep under damping 0.25 while
the cliff receded as I2 kept moving. Recorded here because the wrong hypothesis
is what motivated the experiment that settled it.

## 5. Next: fix I1/I2, scan I3 broadly

The two-of-three convergence is what makes this legitimate now and did not
before. With I1 and I2 genuinely at their best responses, holding them fixed is
not an arbitrary restriction — it is conditioning on a converged sub-profile.

**Design.** Hold

```
I1: N6 0.9958 MW / 5.3006 MWh
I2: N4 24.8221 / 90.2093   N9 6.3163 / 35.4726
```

and sweep I3's capacity across roughly **120–280 MW**, scaling its sweep-224
nodal allocation (N6 95.82, N8 86.04, N3 0.15) along a ray at fixed per-node
duration (N6 3.452 h, N8 2.386 h, N3 2.841 h). At each point clear the market
and settle I3's profit.

### RESULT — the scan (`output/i3_conditional_scan/`)

`i3_ray_scan.csv` (4 MW grid, 60–376 MW) and `i3_ray_scan_fine.csv`
(0.5 MW grid, 160–200 MW). I3's profit along the ray has three regimes:

| range | behaviour | slope |
|---|---|---|
| 60–164 MW | flat ramp — storage earns nothing | ~16 EUR/day per MW |
| 164–184 MW | steep climb — price regime flipping | ~3,330 EUR/day per MW |
| **184.5 MW** | **PEAK, profit 122,041** | conditional optimum |
| 186–190 MW | decline | ~735 EUR/day per MW |
| 190–376 MW | slow decline, pure capex drag | ~20 EUR/day per MW |

**Validation — near-optimal, but the ray IS a restriction.** The ray peak
(122,041.4) matches the full MPEC audit's best-response profit at sweep 224
(122,048.7) to **0.006%**, and the implied gap (184.5 − 182.01 = 2.5 MW,
+12,228 EUR/day) reproduces the audit's 12,235.1.

But the two strategies differ qualitatively. The unrestricted best response
(from `starts_final_audit.json`) is:

```
unrestricted BR:  N3 0.288  N6 96.521  N8 85.490  N9 0.860   total 183.159 MW
ray optimum:      N3 0.156  N6 97.130  N8 87.214  N9 0       total 184.500 MW
```

**The unrestricted response opens a fourth node, N9, which the ray can never
do** — N9 is zero in the base allocation, so scaling keeps it zero. (The audit's
0.860 MW "max power deviation" *is* that N9 entry.) The ray needs 1.34 MW more
capacity to land within 7 EUR/day of the unrestricted optimum.

So: the ray is an excellent near-optimal slice through a flat region, worth
essentially all the attainable profit. It is **not** identical to the
unrestricted best response, and 0.006% profit agreement is evidence of
near-optimality, not of equivalence.

### RESULT — audit at the conditional optimum

I3 placed at 184.5 MW, I1/I2 held at their sweep-224 values
(`audit_i3_184p5.csv`, profile in `capacities_i3_184p5.csv`):

| | status-quo | best response | **deviation** | dP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | −10.7 | 4.9 | 15.6 | 0.695 MW |
| I2 | 70,423.6 | 71,387.5 | **963.9** | 6.316 MW |
| I3 | 122,041.4 | 122,046.4 | **5.0** | 1.101 MW |

**Total profitable deviation: 12,281 → 984.5 EUR/day.** I3's own deviation fell
from 12,235.1 to **5.0** — a factor of ~2,400. Not yet an equilibrium: the move
knocked I2 off its best response (45 → 964) and pushed I1 to a loss, so I1 now
wants to finish exiting. But one 1-D scan achieved an order of magnitude more
than 224 Jacobi sweeps.

### RESULT — Jacobi from the conditional optimum (`output/from_i3_scan_optimum/`)

30 sweeps, same settings. **It moves away from the good point.**

| | total profitable deviation |
|---|---|
| sweep 224, old chain | 12,280.8 |
| **at the conditional optimum** | **984.5** |
| after 30 Jacobi sweeps from it | 5,909.3 |

Final audit: I1 0.0 (fully exited at 0.22 MW), I2 370.0, **I3 5,539.3**.
I3 drifted 184.5 → 186.53 MW and its profit fell 122,041 → 116,466.

**Do not judge this run by its residual.** Power residual finished at
**0.449 MW — under the 0.5 MW tolerance** — while I3 carried a 5,539 EUR/day
deviation. Convergence was blocked only by the *energy* tolerance
(2.215 > 1.0 MWh). The convergence test requires both
([`capacity_game.py:951-952`](../model/capacity_game.py)).

**Trap worth stating explicitly in the write-up:** the energy residual is the
metric §2 shows is economically meaningless — plateau tie-breaking worth under
1 EUR/day. It is now the *only* thing preventing a false
`iteration_converged: true` at a 5,539 EUR/day gap. Anyone who diagnoses the
plateau and relaxes `tolerance_mwh` to a sensible-looking 5 MWh gets a false
convergence on this exact run.

### RESULT — round-2 scan, and the mechanism

Re-scanning I3 against the *new* rival profile
(`i3_ray_scan_round2.csv`):

| | rivals I1+I2 | I3 conditional optimum | peak profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| round 1 | 32.15 MW | 184.5 MW | 122,041 |
| round 2 | 28.31 MW | **188.0 MW** | **122,001** |
| change | **−3.84 MW** | **+3.50 MW** | −40 (−0.03%) |

⚠ **HYPOTHESIS, NOT A MEASUREMENT.** The 3.50/3.84 ≈ 0.91 ratio is a secant
between **two** aggregate observations, and the two rounds do not hold anything
else fixed. Confounds, all verified:

- **I2 relocated, it did not merely shrink.** Round 1: N4 24.82, N9 6.32,
  N8 0.01. Round 2: N4 24.61, **N8 3.16**, N9 0.15, N1 0.18. It moved off N9
  and **onto N8 — one of I3's own PV nodes.** Node identity is the entire
  price-support mechanism, so "rival total fell 3.84 MW" hides the change that
  probably matters most.
- **I3's own ray shape and durations changed** between rounds (N6 share
  52.65% → 53.35%, N6 duration 3.452 h → 3.545 h), so round 2 scans a
  different ray.
- I1 went 1.00 → 0.22 MW at the same time.
- The market may have crossed active-set boundaries in between.

So the following is a hypothesis consistent with the data, **not** a measured
∂BR_I3/∂x_I2 and not yet a citable characterisation:

> **I3 replaces exiting rival storage near one-for-one, and its attainable
> profit is invariant at ~122,000 EUR/day.**

Independent corroboration (also not a measurement): I3's best-response profit
sat in 122,030–122,050 across 250+ sweeps while its capacity grew 130 → 187 MW.
Flat attainable profit under large capacity growth is what the hypothesis
predicts. Testing it properly requires §6.

If it holds, the mechanism behind the chain would be:

- I3's best-response profit was static at 122,030–122,050 across 250+ sweeps
- I3's capacity grew monotonically 130 → 187 MW
- rivals shrank monotonically
- the target receded exactly as fast as I3 approached it

I3's price support requires a certain *total* system storage at the PV nodes.
It does not care who owns it. As I1 and I2 exit, I3 must buy their MW to hold
the same support — paying capex for capacity that was previously free to it.
Its profit is flat because it is buying back what it used to get for nothing.

**Candidate characterisation (unverified): the composite best-response map has
slope ≈ 1**, making the system marginally stable — which would explain why 254
sweeps produced a slow monotone march rather than either convergence or
divergence. The march *is* monotone, which is at least consistent with a
positive real eigenvalue near 1 rather than with oscillation.

But this rests on the unverified 0.91 secant above, and it needs a second
number, ∂BR_I2/∂x_I3, which has **never been measured**. Treat it as the
leading hypothesis among three (non-existence, ordinary slow convergence,
marginal stability), not as settled.

### For the paper — the search scheme

1. **Jacobi sweeps** until the easy players stop moving (here I1, I2).
2. **Identify the stalled player by profitable deviation, not residual.** This
   step is essential and non-obvious: the raw residual was at its 224-sweep
   *minimum* (0.860 MW) exactly where I3's gap was 12,235 EUR/day.
3. **Condition on the converged sub-profile and scan the stalled player in
   1-D**, along the ray through its current nodal allocation at fixed duration.
4. **Re-audit.** Here: 12,281 → 985 EUR/day.
5. **Repeat.** ⚠ **Not with Jacobi** — that walks back off the optimum
   (12,281 → 984 → 5,909). The repeat must be another *conditional scan*.

Cost: ~80 reclears per scan (minutes), versus hours per Jacobi segment. The
profit curve of §5 with the Jacobi trajectory creeping up the cliff face and the
scan jumping to the peak is the figure.

**Open question for the scheme.** Round 2 shows the conditional optimum moves
~0.9 MW per MW of rival exit, so alternating scans chase a target that recedes
almost as fast as they advance — the same marginal stability, just with a bigger
step. A scheme that converges here probably has to solve the fixed point
jointly (e.g. Newton on the stacked best-response residual, or a diagonalisation
with an over-relaxation factor tuned to the ≈1 slope) rather than alternate.
Two scan rounds are not enough to settle this; a third round would show whether
the step sizes are shrinking.

## Verification guidance (carried forward, plus new)

- Judge convergence from **profitable deviation**, not raw residuals, and never
  from investor totals.
- Cheap gap metric: `gap = embedded_profit (history.csv) − recleared_profit at
  the state`. The second term needs **one** `clear()`, not three MPEC solves.
  Caveat: history's BR at sweep *t* is solved against rivals at *t−1*, so the
  estimate degrades exactly when rivals move fast — sanity-check it against
  I1's gap, which must be ≥ 0 by construction.
- The embedded-vs-reclear gap held at **0.006–0.49 EUR/day** across all 224
  sweeps. The smoothing fix is robust; if this grows, suspect price formation.
- **Housekeeping risk:** `capacity_epec/` is untracked in git and
  `model/output/` is gitignored. Both run directories are single uncommitted
  copies of ~7 h of compute. The d=0.5 negative result the previous note
  described as "kept as evidence" is already gone from disk.
