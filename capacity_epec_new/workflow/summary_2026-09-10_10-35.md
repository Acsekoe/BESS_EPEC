# Capacity EPEC cleanup and quadratic-cost assessment

**Written:** 2026-09-10 10:35 CEST (UTC+02:00)  
**Project:** `capacity_epec`  
**Reference data:** `model/input/market_data_smoothed.json`  
**Reference market setting:** energy-only, `DemandAdjustment = 0` (`rho = 0`)  
**Reference MPEC relaxation:** relaxed KKT, complementarity epsilon `1e-6`

## Executive status

The requested pre-audit cleanup and quadratic-cost experiments are complete.
No strong-duality audit or equilibrium certification was performed in this
stage.

The most important result is that quadratic investment curvature is not the
immediate numerical blocker. With `rho = 0`, the ISO clearing becomes an LP and
can have non-unique dual prices. The MPEC can select an investor-favourable LMP
from the lower-level dual solution set, while an exact market reclear returns a
different valid LMP. This produced embedded-versus-recleared profit gaps of
several thousand EUR/day even though complementarity products, primal-dual
gaps, constraint violations, and artificial-bound utilization looked small.

Strong duality alone will not resolve this ambiguity: multiple dual-price
vectors can satisfy the same primal optimum and the same strong-duality
equality. An explicit, reproducible lower-level price-selection rule is needed
before rho-zero equilibrium results can be interpreted economically.

## 1. Demand-adjustment cleanup

`rho = 0` is now an explicit inelastic-demand benchmark:

- `DemandAdjustment[n,t]` is fixed to zero at every node and hour.
- Its stationarity equation is omitted from both MPEC builders.
- This avoids the erroneous equation `0 = LMP`.
- Positive rho remains available as a sensitivity, but is documented as real
  demand elasticity rather than a neutral numerical regularizer.
- At positive rho, the first-order condition remains
  `DemandAdjustment = LMP/rho`, so rho can change dispatch, congestion regimes,
  prices, and investment incentives.

The common data loader and the maintained command-line runners now default to
the smoothed dataset. The principal runners expose an effective rho override
and record it separately from the input-file hash.

## 2. Reference numerical settings cleaned up

Maintained command-line defaults now use:

- `market_data_smoothed.json`;
- `rho = 0`;
- complementarity epsilon `1e-6`;
- zero proximal penalty;
- regret monitoring for the Jacobi driver;
- 400 maximum sweeps and no intermediate multistart by default, leaving the
  final audit as the acceptance test.

The core MPEC default complementarity epsilon was also changed from `1e-4` to
`1e-6`.

The grid-response tool was relabelled as a fixed-shape grid screen followed by
a local audit. It no longer treats a conditional grid argmax as proof of a
continuum or a full-game equilibrium. It now:

- requires the initial capacity profile explicitly;
- distinguishes the best audited candidate from a candidate that actually
  passes the requested certification target;
- only writes `certified_capacities.csv` after the target passes;
- removes stale certificate files when its output directory is reused.

The alternating Gauss-Seidel experiment likewise requires its starting profile
explicitly so an archived positive-rho trajectory cannot silently become the
start of a cleaned run.

## 3. Audit acceptance strengthened

The numerical acceptance gate now requires all of the following:

- valid and locally optimal MPEC termination;
- exact-recleared profitable deviation within the declared tolerance;
- embedded-versus-recleared profit gap within tolerance;
- maximum complementarity product within the declared Scholtes epsilon plus
  solver tolerance;
- no complementarity nonnegativity or upper-bound violation;
- lower-level absolute primal-dual gap within tolerance;
- no primal-variable bound violation;
- less than 99% utilization of artificial price or dual bounds.

Artificial sign restrictions on inequality multipliers are not classified as
artificial caps. The utilization diagnostic only measures the finite outer
price and dual bounds added to contain the NLP search.

Relative regret is now normalized investor by investor:

```text
max_i max(0, deviation_profit_i - incumbent_profit_i)
      / max(1, abs(incumbent_profit_i))
```

This prevents a material deviation by a small merchant from being hidden by a
renewable owner with a much larger generation rent.

The audit remains a finite-multistart, single-solver local audit. It is not a
global best-response proof.

## 4. Quadratic investment-cost formulations

All quadratic investment coefficients remain optional and default to zero.
They are annualized using

```text
CRF(8%, 15 years) / 365.25 = 0.0003198618616 per day.
```

Four common formulations are supported:

1. Portfolio power:
   `0.5*qP*(sum_n X_power[n])^2`.
2. Portfolio energy:
   `0.5*qE*(sum_n X_energy[n])^2`.
3. Common per-node power:
   `0.5*qP_node*sum_n X_power[n]^2`.
4. Common per-node energy:
   `0.5*qE_node*sum_n X_energy[n]^2`.

Portfolio curvature is rank one across nodal capacity coordinates. It curves
total portfolio size but leaves pure redistributions across nodes without
objective curvature. Per-node curvature is full rank in nodal coordinates but
explicitly discourages concentration and therefore changes siting economics.

Genuinely node-specific overrides are now supported by `run_model.py`:

```powershell
--quadratic-cost-power-by-node-eur-per-mw2 N1=515 N6=5125 N8=5125
--quadratic-cost-energy-by-node-eur-per-mwh2 N1=57 N6=569 N8=569
```

A missing node uses the common per-node coefficient. The overrides apply to
all three investors unless investor-specific configuration is constructed in
Python.

At fixed duration `h`, energy curvature affects an MW-direction expansion by
`h^2` times the same numeric power coefficient. Thus the like-for-like
conversion is

```text
qE = qP / h^2.
```

For a three-hour battery, `qP = 1250` is comparable to approximately
`qE = 138.9`, not to `qE = 1250`.

At 25 MW:

| Overnight coefficient | Marginal qP cost | Marginal qE cost at 3 h |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 250 | 2.00 EUR/MW/day | 17.99 EUR/MW/day |
| 500 | 4.00 EUR/MW/day | 35.98 EUR/MW/day |
| 1,250 | 10.00 EUR/MW/day | 89.96 EUR/MW/day |
| 2,500 | 19.99 EUR/MW/day | 179.92 EUR/MW/day |

## 5. Initial-value calibration experiment

`model/screen_quadratic_costs.py` performs exact market clears from an empty
storage portfolio. It installs a small battery for one investor at one node,
measures incremental private value, and then evaluates alternative quadratic
costs without claiming an equilibrium.

The full screen covered:

- investors I1, I2, and I3;
- nodes N1, N6, and N8;
- durations 2, 3, and 4 hours;
- a 0.25 MW local probe;
- q values 250, 500, 1,250, and 2,500;
- portfolio and per-node power and energy formulations.

For a three-hour battery, coefficients that make marginal quadratic cost at
25 MW equal to 50% of the measured positive initial net value are:

| Node | Gross private value | Net value before q | Calibrated qP | Calibrated qE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| N1 | 28.39 EUR/MW/day | 8.24 EUR/MW/day | 515 | 57 |
| N6 | 102.11 EUR/MW/day | 81.96 EUR/MW/day | 5,125 | 569 |
| N8 | 102.11 EUR/MW/day | 81.96 EUR/MW/day | 5,125 | 569 |

At this small probe, all three investors had the same incremental value because
the added capacity did not yet move owned-generation rents. At larger capacities
the renewable owners' value can diverge sharply when a congestion threshold
changes their generation rents.

The roughly tenfold difference between N1 and N6/N8 means that a single common
q cannot be justified by one common initial-arbitrage value. A common q remains
a valid modelling sensitivity, but it must be described as a convention or
regularization choice rather than an empirically identified cost coefficient.

These local values are conditional on IPOPT's returned LP dual prices. They are
not identified market prices while the rho-zero price-selection ambiguity
remains unresolved.

## 6. Matched IPOPT/MPEC benchmark

`model/benchmark_quadratic_mpec.py` solves single-start local best responses
from the same incumbent profile: 5 MW per investor and node with a three-hour
duration. It is a conditioning diagnostic, not an equilibrium search.

Results for I1 were:

| Case | Result | Time | Returned total power | Embedded/reclear profit gap |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| q = 0 | local optimum | 47.7 s | 146.80 MW | 8,025.48 EUR/day |
| portfolio qP = 500 | local optimum | 88.3 s | 125.11 MW | 4,963.71 EUR/day |
| common per-node qP = 500 | screened incumbent retained; no accepted local refinement | 65.1 s | 45.00 MW | unavailable |

For both locally optimal MPEC results:

- maximum complementarity product was about `9.7e-7`;
- lower-level primal-dual gap was about `0.00235 EUR/day`;
- primal bound violation was below `1e-13`;
- maximum artificial-bound utilization was about `0.094`.

The portfolio-q case was approximately 85% slower than zero q and changed the
economic optimum substantially. This does not prove q always hurts IPOPT, but
it disproves the assumption that adding a nonzero objective Hessian must help.

IPOPT already sees a nonzero Hessian of the Lagrangian from nonlinear and
bilinear MPEC constraints. Portfolio q only adds rank-one objective curvature,
and neither portfolio nor per-node investment curvature resolves lower-level
complementarity degeneracy or non-unique LMP selection.

## 7. Interpretation of rho zero

The rho-zero experiment exposed a structural issue:

1. The exact ISO market is a linear program.
2. Its primal dispatch can be optimal while nodal-balance duals are non-unique.
3. KKT or strong duality permits any compatible dual optimum.
4. Because profit is settled at those dual prices, an optimistic MPEC can
   select the dual vector preferred by the active investor.
5. Exact reclearing selects another valid dual vector, producing a large
   settlement-profit discrepancy.

The observed EUR 8,025/day and EUR 4,964/day gaps are therefore more informative
than the small complementarity or primal-dual gaps. The latter establish that
the lower-level solution is nearly optimal; they do not establish that its
price selection matches the market-clearing implementation.

Quadratic *investment* costs cannot repair this because they act in the upper
level. A valid rho-zero EPEC requires a price-selection convention that is
implemented identically in the exact clear and embedded formulation.

Potential approaches for the next stage are:

- a lexicographic secondary lower-level price/dual selection problem;
- an economically justified, strictly convex generator offer curve or dispatch
  regularization, with its economic effect reported;
- a small positive demand elasticity retained explicitly as part of the market
  model and tested for sensitivity, rather than called a numerical tie-break.

The first option preserves inelastic primal demand but is the most involved to
embed consistently. The other options select prices by changing market
economics and must be defended as such.

## 8. Data and interpretation cautions

`market_data_smoothed.json` is a synthetic scenario. Its nine-step thermal
ladder materially lowers the load-weighted thermal offer relative to
`market_data.json`. The source workbook is not stored in this repository.

The input JSON files retain legacy keys such as fixed storage capacities and
VOLL that are not consumed by the maintained loader. They therefore do not
alter the capacity game, but the effective rho must be recorded separately
because command-line overrides are not represented by the source-file hash.

The model uses one deterministic 24-hour profile while annualized capital cost
is divided by 365.25. Its economic interpretation is that this same day repeats
throughout the year; there are no representative-day weights, seasonal states,
or inter-day chronology.

Generation ownership affects who captures congestion-relief price effects, but
neither common nor split renewable ownership by itself proves a unique capacity
split or a continuum. Those remain equilibrium results to be established with
unrestricted best responses under the cleaned market specification.

## 10. Recommended next step

Do not interpret a rho-zero MPEC result as an equilibrium until the exact clear
and embedded problem use the same explicit price-selection rule. Strong
duality should be tested only after that rule is chosen, because strong duality
by itself does not eliminate the dual-price multiplicity observed here.

Once price selection is consistent, compare at least these cases using the
same initial profiles and unrestricted multistart audit:

1. zero quadratic costs;
2. common portfolio qP values 500 and 1,250;
3. common per-node qP values 500 and 1,250;
4. energy-equivalent qE values `qP/h^2`;
5. the documented node-calibrated sensitivity N1=515, N6=5125, N8=5125;
6. at least one deliberately weaker and one stronger node-calibrated case.

Report capacity, nodal allocation, solve time, termination, exact-recleared
profit, embedded/reclear gap, complementarity product, primal-dual gap,
artificial-bound utilization, and multistart regret for every case.
