# Locating the rho-zero profit gap: knife-edge price degeneracy, not dispatch selection

**Written:** 2026-09-10 11:53 CEST (UTC+02:00)
**Project:** `capacity_epec`
**Reference data:** `model/input/market_data_smoothed.json`
**Reference market setting:** energy-only, `DemandAdjustment = 0` (`rho = 0`)
**Reference MPEC relaxation:** relaxed KKT, complementarity epsilon `1e-6`

## Executive status

The EUR 8,025/day embedded-versus-recleared profit gap reported on 2026-09-10
10:35 has been located exactly and reproduced to the cent. It is **genuine
lower-level dual multiplicity**, confirming that summary's cause (a), and
confirming its claim that strong duality cannot detect it.

Its **scope was wrong**, and that changes the plan. The degeneracy is not a
generic property of `rho = 0`. At every uniform capacity profile tested the
market LP has a unique primal *and* a unique dual. The degeneracy exists only at
the congestion-relief threshold, and the MPEC's optimum sits exactly on it —
because that is where profit is discontinuous, so an optimiser drives to it and
then selects the favourable branch.

No equilibrium was searched for or certified in this stage. No model
formulation was changed.

## 1. Method

Two exact linear-programming characterisations, one per source of ambiguity.
Both are measured at a stated capacity profile; neither is an equilibrium claim.

**Price face.** Pin the dispatch at the exactly cleared optimum, then maximise
and minimise investor profit over dual-feasible prices whose dual objective is
within `slack` of the optimal system cost. For an LP, complementary slackness
between *any* primal optimum and *any* dual optimum is automatic, so this is the
dual-optimal set exactly — no tolerance-based active-set guessing. Profit is
affine in the duals once dispatch is fixed, so each direction is one LP.

**Dispatch face.** Hold prices at their cleared values, then maximise and
minimise profit over dispatches whose system cost is within `slack` of optimal.
This is what the ISO's indifference between co-located batteries is worth.

**The slack band is the instrument, not a convenience.** These faces can have
empty interior, which defeats interior-point solvers. Measuring at two slacks
separates the two regimes:

- width tracking the slack (width(1.0) ≈ 100 × width(0.01)) means a **unique**
  optimum: the only profit on offer is bought one-for-one with system cost;
- width far above the slack and insensitive to it is a **real face**, and the
  excess is what selection moves at no cost to the ISO.

Solved with HiGHS via Pyomo APPSI. IPOPT is the wrong tool here and was the
source of spurious "locally infeasible" returns on faces with empty interior;
that failure mode is itself diagnostic and should not be read as infeasibility.

**Self-validation.** The exact clear's own settlement must lie inside every
measured interval. It did in every successful solve, and at the corner the two
endpoints reproduced the embedded and recleared profits to the cent (below).

## 2. Away from the corner the market is not degenerate

Starting the price face from `lam = 80` everywhere, both directions converged to
precisely the recleared profit for all three investors. The dispatch face first
reported exactly 1.00 EUR/day at a 1.00 slack; at a 0.001 slack it reported
0.00, i.e. it had been measuring the slack and nothing else.

## 3. Approaching the corner: hyperbolic sensitivity, no face

Interpolating from the 5 MW/node uniform profile to the capacity I1's MPEC
actually returns (rivals held at 5 MW/node), price-face excess for I1:

| alpha | total MW | excess @ slack 0.01 | excess @ slack 1.0 | ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.900 | 226.62 | 0.18 | 18.10 | ~100 |
| 0.950 | 231.71 | 0.37 | 37.23 | ~100 |
| 0.980 | 234.77 | 0.95 | 94.63 | ~100 |
| 0.990 | 235.78 | 1.90 | 190.30 | ~100 |
| 0.995 | 236.29 | 3.82 | 381.63 | ~100 |
| 0.999 | 236.70 | 19.12 | 1,441.83 | ~75 |

The ratio pinned at ~100 across three decades of alpha means the true face is
**empty** everywhere on this path: width is purely `k * slack`. But the
sensitivity `k` diverges as roughly `1.9 / (1 - alpha)`. The market is not
degenerate on the approach; it is increasingly *ill-conditioned*, and prices
become arbitrarily sensitive to a vanishing cost perturbation.

## 4. At the corner the face opens, and it is large

At alpha = 1.000 (236.80 MW total; I1 at N3 = 18.04, N6 = 70.22, N8 = 40.54,
N9 = 18.01, rivals 5 MW/node):

| investor | recleared profit | price-face width | slack-independent? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| I1 | 1,466.92 | 8,025.49 | yes |
| I2 | 72,432.93 | 1,874.73 | yes (1,874.73 vs 1,938.97) |
| I3 | 109,698.31 | 54,377.30 | yes (54,377.30 vs 54,898.58) |

I1's endpoints are the decisive measurement: the maximum is **9,492.41
EUR/day**, exactly the embedded profit the MPEC reported, and the minimum is
**1,466.92 EUR/day**, exactly the recleared profit. The gap that motivated this
work is the width of this face, reproduced to the cent from an independent
formulation.

I1's face was measured at band 0.01 with the MPEC's own price/dual bounds via a
targeted run; the sweep's slack-0.01 cell returned `nan` because HiGHS failed on
that particular zero-interior LP. Corroboration: the same face at slack 1.0
returned 8,055.63, i.e. 8,025 plus the expected `k ~ 30` slack term.

## 5. What is actually degenerate

At the corner, embedded and exact agree on system cost to **0.00 EUR/day**
(both 214,988.09) and the embedded strong-duality residual is **0.0024
EUR/day**. So the embedded primal is cost-optimal and the embedded duals are
optimal duals: both solutions are genuine optimal primal-dual pairs of the same
LP. They differ only in price, at N8 hours 12-15:

```text
LMP N8, hours 12-15:   embedded 0.000    exact 20.552
exact LMP range:  alpha <= 0.999 -> [0.00, 47.00];  alpha = 1.000 -> [16.03, 47.00]
```

The zero prices in the pocket disappear exactly at the corner. That is the
congestion-relief threshold: the corridor constraint sits precisely at its limit
with zero slack, so its dual is indeterminate between "binding, positive dual"
(pocket price 0) and "not binding, zero dual" (pocket price 20.55). Both are
valid clearings of the same market at the same cost.

I1 is a merchant charging in the pocket at midday, so it selects the congested
branch and charges for free. **The congestion-relief cliff documented in the
README and this price indeterminacy are the same object.** The threshold is
where the LMP is undefined.

## 6. The co-located-battery indifference does not bite here

All three investors carry identical degradation cost, so the ISO is exactly
indifferent about which battery at a node cycles. That indifference was expected
to open a dispatch face. It does not: **dispatch-face excess was 0.00 at every
profile, every alpha, and every investor.**

The reason is saturation. At the profiles tested, storage cycles at its power
and energy limits, so the cost-optimal dispatch is unique and there is nothing
to reallocate. At the 5 MW/node uniform profile all three investors record an
identical storage settlement of 4,095.97 EUR/day — an even split that is forced,
not chosen.

This matters for a proposed extension (below): the ambiguity in this model is
entirely in **prices**, not in **dispatch**.

## 7. Consequences for the plan

1. **"Rho-zero results are uninterpretable" is too strong.** Away from the
   threshold, prices are unique and reproducible, and the profits reported in
   the 10:35 summary's calibration screen are not compromised by multiplicity.
2. **The narrower statement is worse.** The one place the model wants to sit is
   the one place the price is undefined, and an investor whose profit depends on
   which branch is chosen has an incentive to drive the model there. A
   price-selection rule is therefore not a numerical tidy-up: it *determines*
   the equilibrium.
3. **The degeneracy is a knife edge, not a fat region.** It was not resolvable
   at 0.001 in alpha, about 0.1 MW of capacity. So a lexicographic tie-break
   would be selecting on a measure-zero set while the profit function stays
   discontinuous across it. It makes the two implementations agree without
   making the economics well-posed.
4. **This vindicates the historical positive-rho workaround as regularisation,
   not concealment.** Positive rho adds strict curvature that makes the dual
   unique, which is exactly the defect found here. As the README already states,
   it also picks the congestion regime at the threshold — so it makes the same
   decision, by economic assumption rather than solver accident. That is
   defensible in a way a tie-break is not, provided the elasticity is reported
   as part of the market model and its sensitivity shown.

## 8. Parked: investor offer curves (raised 2026-09-10, not implemented)

Proposal: let investors submit price *and* quantity offers for owned generation
(24 PV, 24 wind) and battery discharge, which would require SOC constraints in
the upper level.

Assessment, to be revisited rather than acted on now:

- It would break the co-located-battery tie by making the ISO strictly prefer
  one unit over another. But section 6 shows that tie is **not currently
  binding**, so this is not a fix for the measured gap.
- The degeneracy found here is in a **transmission** dual at the congestion
  threshold, not in the storage merit order. Battery offer prices do not remove
  it; the corridor still sits exactly at its limit.
- It is a change of research question, from capacity-only investment to a joint
  capacity-and-bidding game, with an upper level that gains SOC dynamics and
  therefore a much larger MPEC.
- Only a strictly convex offer curve would make the dual unique, and that is the
  same regularisation as positive rho, obtained at far higher modelling cost.

## 10. Does positive rho fix it? Measured, same session

Re-running the price face at the corner under positive rho, and clearing the
same profile across four orders of magnitude of rho:

**The multiplicity is removed completely.** The price face collapses from
8 025.49 / 1 874.73 / 54 377.30 EUR/day (I1/I2/I3) at `rho = 0` to 0.00–0.22
EUR/day at every rho in {25, 500, 5 000} — below the measurement slack, i.e.
numerically a single point. Even `rho = 25` suffices.

**Away from degeneracy rho is exactly neutral.** At the uniform 5 MW/node
control profile, N8 h12–15 prices and all three profits are identical to the
cent for every rho from 25 to 20 000, and identical to the `rho = 0` clearing
(I1 1 159.73, I2 73 087.36, I3 55 326.27). Only `rho = 5` deviates, and only
because it conjures 3.67 % of supply.

**At the threshold rho decides, and the decision does not vanish.** At the
corner:

| rho | N8 h12-15 | I1 profit | I2 profit | I3 profit | supply/load |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 20.552 | 1,466.92 | 72,432.93 | 109,698.31 | 100.0000% |
| 5 | 0.000 | 4,274.32 | 65,157.52 | 62,000.90 | 103.5038% |
| 25 | 0.000 | 7,366.11 | 71,860.02 | 64,070.76 | 100.7548% |
| 100 | 0.000 | 7,503.70 | 72,114.00 | 64,525.44 | 100.1890% |
| 500 | 0.000 | 7,503.78 | 72,114.04 | 64,525.08 | 100.0378% |
| 5,000 | 0.000 | 7,504.66 | 72,114.48 | 64,521.04 | 100.0038% |
| 20,000 | 0.000 | 7,507.63 | 72,115.93 | 64,507.33 | 100.0009% |

Every positive rho selects the *congested* branch — the one the optimistic MPEC
wanted. Relative to the `rho = 0` clearing this is worth **+6 037 EUR/day to I1**
and **−45 173 EUR/day to I3**, and it is stable to three significant figures for
rho >= 100.

**The `rho → ∞` limit is not the `rho = 0` answer.** At rho = 20 000 the elastic
term moves 0.0009 % of load and still returns 0.000, not 20.552. So the standard
defence — "choose rho large enough that the distortion is negligible" — does not
hold at a degenerate point: the *quantity* distortion vanishes while the *price
and profit* effect stays at full size. The two are different branches of the
same degenerate optimum, not a limit and its approximation.

**Interpretation.** This arguably makes positive rho the more principled
selection, not the less: its answer is the limit of a sequence of well-posed
elastic markets, whereas the `rho = 0` value of 20.552 is whichever point on the
optimal face IPOPT's central path happened to reach. But it is a *selection*,
worth 45 000 EUR/day to I3, and must be reported with that number attached.

Not yet measured: whether profit is *continuous* in capacity under positive rho.
Uniqueness makes the price a function of capacity rather than a correspondence;
it does not by itself remove the steep transition, and equilibrium existence
depends on that.

## 11. Recommended next step

Do not run a symmetric multi-sweep trajectory yet. It will walk into the
threshold and return a convention-determined number.

The choice is now between two defensible market models, and it should be made
explicitly rather than by tie-break:

1. **Inelastic demand plus a stated selection rule.** Preserves `rho = 0`
   economics but leaves profit discontinuous at the threshold; the rule decides
   the equilibrium and must be defended as a market design assumption.
2. **A small, reported demand elasticity or strictly convex offer curve.**
   Makes the dual unique, removes the discontinuity, and turns the threshold
   into a steep-but-single-valued transition. Costs an explicit economic
   assumption and changes clearing at the threshold.

Before choosing, measure the profit discontinuity across the threshold under
option 2 at several elasticities: if a small elasticity leaves a near-vertical
but single-valued profit curve, the equilibrium becomes well-posed without
materially changing the economics, and that is the strongest available outcome.
