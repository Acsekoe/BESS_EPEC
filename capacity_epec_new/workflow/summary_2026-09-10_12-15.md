# The solution space at the threshold: a four-step staircase, and what rho buys

**Written:** 2026-09-10 12:15 CEST (UTC+02:00)
**Project:** `capacity_epec`
**Reference data:** `model/input/market_data_smoothed.json`
**Reference market setting:** energy-only, path through I1's MPEC corner
**Reference MPEC relaxation:** not used — every number here is an exact market clear

## Executive status

Positive rho makes investor profit **continuous** in capacity. That is now
measured, not assumed, by bracket refinement rather than by inspecting a grid.

It is **necessary but not sufficient**. The payoff through the threshold is a
staircase of four distinct congestion-relief events compressed into about 7 MW,
across which I1 and I3 move in exactly opposite directions. Continuity does not
make that quasi-concave, so a pure-strategy equilibrium is still not guaranteed,
and best responses near the staircase are effectively bang-bang.

rho buys continuity at an exactly fixed exchange rate: both the smoothing window
and the market distortion scale as `1/rho`, so their ratio is a constant of the
model. There is no sweet spot to find — only a point on a line to choose.

No equilibrium was searched for or certified. No model formulation was changed.

## 1. Method: bracket refinement, not a grid

A finite grid cannot separate a steep slope from a jump — each looks like the
other. `model/scan_threshold.py` therefore scans a capacity path, finds the
adjacent pair carrying the largest profit change, then bisects that bracket
repeatedly, keeping the half that carries the change. Each level halves the
bracket, so:

- a **continuous** payoff halves the profit change with it (decay ~0.5);
- a **jump** keeps the change whatever the bracket width (decay ~1.0).

Only the decay is diagnostic. The absolute size of the change says nothing: a
steep enough slope beats any fixed threshold, which is exactly the mistake the
first version of this script made.

## 2. rho = 0 is a genuine discontinuity

Refining the I3 bracket at rho = 0, the profit change held at **exactly
54,372.04 EUR/day** through 17 bisections while the bracket shrank 65,536x to
**0.000078 MW**. Decay ratio 1.000. The pocket LMP jumped 0.000 -> 18.581 with
no intermediate value at any refinement level.

This is a true discontinuity in the payoff, not a steep transition.

## 3. Every positive rho is continuous, and the scaling is exact

| rho | verdict | decay | transition width | local slope EUR/MW/day |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | JUMP | 1.000 | 0 | infinite |
| 100 | continuous | 0.500 | 0.159066 MW | 57,020 |
| 500 | continuous | 0.500 | 0.039767 MW | 285,098 |
| 2,500 | continuous | 0.500 | 0.009942 MW | 1,425,494 |
| 5,000 | continuous | 0.500 | 0.004971 MW | 2,850,580 |
| 20,000 | continuous | 0.500 | 0.001243 MW | 11,404,304 |

Decay is 0.500 to three decimals at every positive rho. The scaling is exact
across a 200x range:

```text
transition width  ~ 24.9 / rho   MW
local slope       ~ 570 * rho    EUR/MW/day
```

and the earlier conjured-supply measurement scales the same way
(`0.189 % at rho 100`, `0.0009 % at rho 20 000`, i.e. `~18.9 / rho`).

**The consequence is a fixed exchange rate.** Market distortion and smoothing
window both go as `1/rho`, so their ratio — roughly 1.3 MW of window per 1 % of
conjured supply — is invariant. Choosing rho does not trade a small distortion
for a disproportionate numerical gain; it slides along a straight line. A larger
rho is more faithful *and* proportionally harder to resolve, in exactly the
place resolution is needed.

This reverses the presumption behind the archived `rho = 5000` choice. That
value was picked to minimise conjured supply, which it does; but it also
compresses the feature the solver must resolve to 0.005 MW with a gradient of
2.85 million EUR/MW/day. A smaller rho is numerically far kinder for a
proportional and still small distortion.

## 4. rho is neutral everywhere except at the degenerate point

Scanning past the corner, `rho = 0` and `rho = 5000` agree **to the cent** from
x = 1.05 outward (I1 939.94, I2 72,263.86, I3 113,341.63 at x = 1.05, and
identical to x = 1.40). They also agree at and below x = 0.95.

They differ at exactly one point, x = 1.000 — the degenerate corner. Combined
with the earlier uniform-profile control, the picture is complete: **rho changes
nothing except where the rho-zero price was undefined.**

## 5. The solution space is a four-step staircase

Scanning x from 0.96 to 1.12 at rho = 5000 (0.005 in x, about 0.64 MW):

| x | total MW | pocket LMP | I1 profit | I3 profit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| <= 0.995 | <= 236.29 | 0.000 | rising to **9,450.74** | 55,326.27 |
| 1.000 | 236.80 | 2.892 | 7,504.66 | 64,521.04 |
| 1.005-1.035 | 237.4-241.2 | 18.581 | ~1,480 | 109,698.34 |
| 1.040-1.050 | 241.9-243.1 | 19.827 | ~936 | 113,341.63 |
| >= 1.055 | >= 243.8 | 23.000 | falling from **-1,052** | 123,001.02 |

This is not one threshold. It is **four distinct congestion-relief events
compressed into about 7 MW** (236.3 to 243.8), each a different line-and-hour
combination becoming unbinding, each contributing its own step.

The interests are exactly opposed across it:

- **I1 peaks at 9,450.74 EUR/day at x = 0.995, immediately before the first
  step, then collapses to -1,052.** Its optimum sits on the left edge of the
  cliff.
- **I3 climbs monotonically from 55,326 to 123,001 across the staircase.** Its
  optimum is past the right edge.

## 6. This explains the MPEC's behaviour completely

I1's best response is to sit at the last capacity before congestion relief. At
rho = 0 that point is degenerate, and the degeneracy let the MPEC claim to hold
x = 1.000 of capacity while being *priced* as if it were at x < 1.000 — pocket
LMP 0.000, embedded profit 9,492.41, against a recleared 1,466.92.

The MPEC did not stumble into degeneracy. It optimised toward the left edge of
a cliff, and the cliff edge is exactly where the price is undefined. The
8,025.48 EUR/day gap is the height of the first step, claimed by standing on the
cheap side of it.

This also explains the archived observations that a damped Jacobi iteration
"parks inside the step" and that earlier runs suggested a continuum near
215 MW: an iteration has no fixed point to find inside a staircase of opposed
interests, so it cycles.

## 7. Why continuity is not enough

A pure-strategy Nash equilibrium needs more than continuous payoffs. It needs
quasi-concavity in each player's own strategy, and section 5 shows I1's payoff
is emphatically not quasi-concave: it rises to a sharp interior peak and then
collapses across four risers within 7 MW.

At rho = 5000 those risers are continuous but nearly vertical — 2.85 million
EUR/MW/day. Against an audit tolerance of 10 EUR/day on the profit gap, a
0.0000035 MW capacity error is already a tolerance-sized profit error. So:

- best responses near the staircase are effectively bang-bang;
- the regret criterion is satisfiable only if an iterate lands within a window
  narrower than the solver's own capacity resolution;
- existence in pure strategies is an open question for this instance, not a
  formality restored by making rho positive.

## 9. Recommended next step

1. **Do not treat positive rho as having solved the game.** It removes the price
   ambiguity and restores continuity, both real gains, and it is neutral away
   from the degenerate set. It does not restore quasi-concavity.
2. **Re-choose rho for conditioning, not only fidelity.** Given the fixed
   exchange rate, prefer the smallest rho whose conjured supply is defensible.
   `rho = 100` gives a 0.16 MW window at 0.19 % conjured supply; `rho = 5000`
   gives 0.005 MW at 0.0038 %. Report the choice with both numbers.
3. **Test existence directly before running long iterations.** On a 2-D slice —
   I1 total against I3 total, with I2 fixed — map both best-response
   correspondences by exact reclear and look for a crossing. A staircase of
   opposed interests may have none, and that is far cheaper to discover on a
   grid than through a 400-sweep Jacobi run that cannot converge.
4. If no pure-strategy equilibrium exists on that slice, the honest options are
   a mixed-strategy or epsilon-equilibrium report, or a market refinement that
   removes the staircase itself. Say which, rather than reporting a parked
   iterate as an equilibrium.
