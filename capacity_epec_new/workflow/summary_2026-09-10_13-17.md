# A candidate structure, not an equilibrium: I1 exits, I3 supports its own PV price

**Written:** 2026-09-10 13:17 CEST (UTC+02:00)
**Project:** `capacity_epec`
**Reference data:** `model/input/market_data_smoothed.json`
**Reference market setting:** energy-only, `rho = 100`
**Method:** exact market clears throughout; one unrestricted multistart MPEC audit at the end

## Executive status

**These are not equilibrium capacities, and this stage did not determine any.**
I2 was pinned at 5 MW/node as a construction choice for a two-player slice and
was never optimised at any point; I1 and I3 were restricted to a single
investment shape. The unrestricted audit at the resulting point returns
`passed: False`, with I2 wanting +1,376.74 EUR/day on an 8.463 MW move and I3
+928.33 on 27.454 MW.

What the stage does establish is a **candidate structure**. On the slice, both
best responses are parallel lines of slope -1 that never meet in the interior;
they meet only at the `q1 >= 0` floor, where **I1 exits entirely and I3 holds the
pocket**. An unrestricted multistart audit confirms **I1's exit is an exact best
response** — profitable deviation 0.00 EUR/day, 0.000 MW — which is the one
claim here that survives an off-shape search.

Determining the actual capacities requires a three-player iteration with I2
free. That is the next stage, not this one.

Separately and importantly: at `rho = 100` the embedded-versus-recleared gaps at
this profile are **0.00 / 6.33 / 30.27 EUR/day**, against 8,025.48 at `rho = 0`.
The price-selection pathology is effectively gone.

## 1. The slice

`model/probe_br_slice.py` holds I2 at a uniform base and gives I1 and I3 a
common "pocket" shape — I1's own MPEC allocation, normalised to
`N3 0.1229, N6 0.4783, N8 0.2761, N9 0.1227` — scaled by one number each:

```text
I1 = shape * q1
I2 = 5 MW/node                      (45 MW)
I3 = 5 MW/node + shape * q3
```

At `q1 = 146.80, q3 = 0` this reproduces the MPEC corner exactly, so the slice
passes through the point where the rho-zero degeneracy was found. Every payoff
is an exact clear and settlement — no MPEC, no relaxation, no warm start.
1,939 market clears in 1,675.7 s.

## 2. Both best responses are slope -1 lines targeting different totals

**I1 targets a total of 230.41 MW** — the last capacity before congestion
relief. `BR1(q3) = 140.41 - q3` exactly, for q3 = 0 to 120, with pocket LMP
1.911 at every point:

| q3 | BR1 | total MW | pocket | I1 profit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 140.41 | 230.41 | 1.911 | 8,767.11 |
| 40 | 100.41 | 230.41 | 1.911 | 6,269.60 |
| 80 | 60.41 | 230.41 | 1.911 | 3,772.08 |
| 120 | 20.41 | 230.41 | 1.911 | 1,274.56 |
| 140 | 24.79 | 254.79 | 19.827 | 126.77 |
| 160 | 4.83 | 254.83 | 19.827 | 24.72 |
| >= 180 | 0.00 | — | 23.000 | 0.00 |

I1 spends its entire strategy on holding aggregate capacity on the cheap side of
the cliff. Its profit erodes as I3 grows *at an unchanged price* — same
arbitrage, smaller share. At q3 = 140 it can no longer hold 230.41 (that would
need 0.41 MW) and switches to a larger position at a worse price: 24.79 MW at
total 254.79 for 126.77 EUR/day. A bigger position on the far side beats a
sliver on the near side.

**I3 targets a total of 255.12 MW** — just past the *last* step.
`BR3(q1) = 165.12 - q1` exactly, for q1 = 0 to 160, pocket 23.000 throughout,
profit 121,846 to 122,965. Above q1 = 180 it holds nothing: I1 alone has already
pushed the total past the step, so I3 free-rides.

## 3. No interior fixed point, by construction

In the region where both are interior:

```text
BR1: q1 = 164.83 - q3      (target total 254.83, just below the last step)
BR3: q3 = 165.12 - q1      (target total 255.12, just above it)
```

Composing gives a **constant residual of +0.29** — the two lines are parallel
and never cross. The whole game is a 0.29 MW standoff over the last riser, and
they chase each other up the staircase to get there.

Measured `g(q3) = BR3(BR1(q3)) - q3`: `+24.71` constant while I1 still targets
230.41; then `+0.33` and `+0.25` once both are contesting the last step; then
`-14.88` at q3 = 180, once I1 is on its floor. Sign change bracketed in
[160, 180].

## 4. The fixed point is at the boundary

The only crossing is where I1's reply hits `q1 >= 0`. At `q3 = BR3(0) = 165.12`
I1's desired position is `164.83 - 165.12 = -0.29`, clamped to zero.

Verified directly rather than inferred:

- **I1**: 0.00 at q1 = 0, and strictly negative at every positive capacity
  (-0.35 at 0.05 MW, -0.70 at 0.1, -6.99 at 1, -82.38 at 10, -2,821 at 140),
  monotone. BR1(165.12) = 0 exactly.
- **I3**: 165.12 is a true interior optimum — 114,159.74 at q3 = 160,
  114,180.19 at 164, 119,984.74 at 165.00, **121,846.55 at 165.12**, then
  121,840.40 at 166, 121,812.43 at 170, 117,704.31 at 200.

Equilibrium profits: **I1 0.00, I2 71,114.25, I3 121,846.55.**

## 5. What the equilibrium means economically

I3 at `q3 = 0` earns 55,533.68. At `q3 = 165.12` it earns 121,846.55. So
**price support is worth 66,312.87 EUR/day net of the capital cost of 165 MW of
storage.** I3 does not buy storage to arbitrage; it buys storage to relieve the
congestion that is suppressing the price on its own 4,500 MWh/day of PV.

The merchant is the casualty. I1's arbitrage lives on the very price spread that
I3 is paying to destroy, so once I3 is large enough the merchant's best response
is to hold nothing at all. This is the mechanism behind the ~68k/day price
support already noted for I3; it is now located, priced, and shown to be an
equilibrium outcome rather than an audit artefact.

`audit_equilibrium` at the candidate profile, `rho = 100`, multistart,

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I1 | -0.00 | -0.00 | **0.00** | **0.000 MW** | 0.00 |
| I2 | 71,114.25 | 72,490.99 | 1,376.74 | 8.463 MW | 6.33 |
| I3 | 121,846.55 | 122,774.88 | 928.33 | 27.454 MW | 30.27 |

**I1's exit survives an unrestricted, off-shape search exactly** — zero
deviation, zero megawatts. That is the strongest single result here.

It does not pass overall, and should not: I2 was pinned at 5 MW/node by the
slice construction and never optimised, and I3 was restricted to one shape. Both
deviations are small relative to their profits (1.9 % and 0.8 %) and to the
66,313 EUR/day at stake in the price support, so the *structure* is right even
though the point is not the equilibrium.

## 7. rho has effectively closed the price-selection gap

Embedded-versus-recleared gaps at this profile are 0.00, 6.33 and 30.27 EUR/day,
against **8,025.48** at the rho-zero corner. Only I3 still exceeds the 10.0
tolerance, and it does so at a profile where it is 27 MW away from its own best
response, so the residual is a search artefact rather than a pricing one.

The pathology that motivated this whole investigation is, for practical
purposes, resolved by a positive rho.

## 9. Recommended next step

1. **Run a genuine three-player search warm-started from this point.** The
   structure is now known and the warm start is close: `I1 = 0`,
   `I3 = 5 MW/node + shape*165.12`, I2 free. Use `regret_guided_search.py` with
   `rho = 100` and `--initial-capacities`. It has a real chance of converging,
   which no run from the symmetric start did.
2. **Watch for I1 re-entering.** A cleaner equilibrium may have I1 holding a
   small position; the audit says zero here, but I2's 8.5 MW deviation will move
   the total and could reopen the door.
3. **Report the price-support mechanism as a finding, not a nuisance.** The
   66,313 EUR/day figure and the merchant's exit are the economically
   interesting content of this model, and they are now measured rather than
   inferred.
4. Re-run the slice at `rho = 0` for contrast if a thesis section needs it: the
   same map should show the corner sitting exactly on the discontinuity.
