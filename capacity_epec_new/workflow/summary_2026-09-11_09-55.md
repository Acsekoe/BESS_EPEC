## Executive status

**No full-game equilibrium was found, and neither existence nor nonexistence of a pure equilibrium has been proved.** There is substantial new evidence about why the search is difficult, and a new inexpensive way to certify I1's exit conditional on rivals' capacities.

- The planner fleet split equally among investors is a defensible initialization. Preserve its nodal power AND energy allocation; do not redistribute the fleet uniformly over all nine nodes.
- At the planner profile, the reference clearing exhibits a profit jump. Across a bracket only 8.3361e-7 MW wide in maximum nodal power, I3's profit changes by EUR 34,348.75 at rho = 0. At rho = 100, the corresponding refined transition changes by only EUR 0.01576.
- A convex incremental-expansion LP gives a global upper bound on a generation-free merchant's entry profit. It is zero, within LP tolerances, at the planner-owned-by-I3 profile, the earlier exit profile revalued at rho = 0, and the final new coordinate-search profile.
- A planner-shape-only search reaches I1 = I2 = 0, I3 = 186.971374 MW. This restricted candidate fails location/duration deviation checks: I2 and I3 both have profitable moves.
- The final unrestricted-coordinate candidate is 227.007691 MW / 805.891610 MWh, with I1 = 0. It is demonstrably NOT an equilibrium: adding 0.001 MW / 0.003 MWh at N5 gains I2 EUR 626.60/day.
- Equal LMPs at N3 and N9 are confirmed at the tested migration profiles. The abandoned node does not acquire a larger spread than the occupied node. Transfers nevertheless can change global price selection or battery flexibility, particularly when durations differ.
- An explicit, independently recleared three-point counterexample shows I2's payoff is not quasiconcave on one feasible unilateral line at rho = 0. Standard concavity-based pure-equilibrium existence results therefore cannot simply be invoked.

## 1. Defensible initialization and market convention

The inherited planner fleet is 186.971371 MW / 561.904611 MWh. Give each investor one third at EACH planner location: approximately 62.323790 MW / 187.301537 MWh per investor. Retain each node's own duration; the fleet's average duration of 3.0053 hours is not a uniform nodal duration.

The existing planner_equal_split_capacities.csv already does this. Its ownership split is a convention, while the nodal fleet is the planner result.

The benchmark does not remove price-selection ambiguity. New HiGHS versus fresh IPOPT comparisons at the planner split give:

| Measurement | HiGHS minus IPOPT, or maximum absolute difference |
|---|---:|
| ISO operating cost | -7.72e-8 EUR/day |
| Maximum LMP difference | 1.44750 EUR/MWh |
| I1 profit | +107.99 EUR/day |

At the older Jacobi stall and the earlier exit profile, both evaluated at rho = 0, the same comparison agrees in prices to below 1e-8 EUR/MWh and profits to below 4e-6 EUR/day.

Reported profitable deviations and counterexamples were checked by fresh IPOPT clearing and settlement. A precise economic price-selection convention is still needed for a defensible full-game existence statement at degenerate profiles.

## 2. Smoothness at the planner point

The path changes only I3's nodal power and energy, from the equal planner split toward its first new single-start MPEC response. I1 and I2 stay fixed. Both endpoints are feasible, so convex interpolation is feasible.

There were 17 initial samples and 18 bracket halvings per rho, 35 fresh IPOPT clears per case. The largest-change bracket was selected adaptively. At rho = 0 it stays attached to the planner endpoint; at rho = 100 it moves into the interior of the transition.

| rho | Final maximum nodal MW bracket width | I3 profit change | Maximum LMP change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 8.33608e-7 | 34,348.7460 EUR/day | 22.376824 EUR/MWh |
| 100 | 8.33608e-7 | 0.015760 EUR/day | 7.22687e-6 EUR/MWh |

This is strong numerical evidence of a jump in the reference clearing and a steep continuous transition in the positive-rho sensitivity along this path. It is not a proof of global discontinuity locations, global smoothness, or uniqueness of all QP prices.

At the earlier exit profile, separate unilateral scaling/re-entry rays also show persistent rho-zero jumps. Positive rho reduces those changes with bracket width, but the transition can still be very steep. For example, a 0.00014525 MW maximum nodal interval on I3's scaling ray changes its profit by EUR 36.46/day at rho = 100.

## 3. A global merchant-entry bound without an MPEC

This section is an analytical derivation applied to this model, not a claim inferred solely from sampled deviations.

Fix the rivals' capacities. Let V(x) be the exact ISO operating cost when a merchant enters with nodal capacity vector x = (power, energy). Let k(x) be its daily capital cost, and S(x) its operating surplus from LMP settlement after degradation.

The merchant's operational constraints are homogeneous apart from capacity right-hand sides. At an exact ISO primal-dual optimum, its surplus is the sum of the associated capacity shadow rents. Reusing that dual solution after setting the merchant's capacities to zero gives, by weak duality,

    S(x) <= V(0) - V(x).

Consequently,

    merchant profit(x) = S(x) - k(x)
                       <= V(0) - [V(x) + k(x)].

Therefore a valid upper bound over ALL feasible merchant capacities is

    B = V(0) - min_x { V(x) + k(x) }.

The minimization is a convex incremental-expansion problem: keep the rivals' capacities fixed, free I1's nodal power and energy, preserve all ISO operating constraints, impose the 2–8 hour duration limits and residual shared node limits, and minimize operating cost plus I1 capex. At rho = 0 this is an LP.

If B = 0, zero entry achieves zero profit and is a global merchant best response at those fixed rivals. The argument covers every exact dual settlement of each market, rather than choosing the merchant's preferred degenerate prices. It does not cover generation owners, whose generation-rent changes are absent from the bound.

This applies the weak-duality and sensitivity framework described in [Boyd and Vandenberghe's duality notes](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/duality.pdf).

Measured LP results, all at rho = 0:

| Fixed rivals | Merchant profit upper bound | Optimal incremental entry |
|---|---:|---:|
| I3 holds the planner-shape candidate, I2 zero | 0.0 EUR/day | 0 MW / 0 MWh |
| Earlier gs_from_corner_rho100 final capacities, revalued at rho = 0 | 2.91e-11 EUR/day | 0 MW / 0 MWh |
| Intermediate new coordinate profile | 0.0, raw -2.91e-11 EUR/day | 0 MW / 0 MWh |
| Final 227.007691 MW coordinate profile | 0.0, raw -2.91e-11 EUR/day | 0 MW / 0 MWh |

These are numerical LP certificates subject to solver tolerances, not exact rational-arithmetic certificates. The proof is conditional on the stated fixed rivals and model assumptions. Recompute the LP whenever their capacities change; this does not justify fixing I1 at zero everywhere in the game.

The automated test also checks a positive-control case: with no rival storage, the expansion bound is greater than EUR 10,000/day, so the routine does not return zero indiscriminately.

## 4. What the planner-based searches found

### 4.1 Restricted planner shape

Each investor was allowed to scale the planner's full nodal power/energy shape by its own total MW. Every scalar response first screened the entire feasible scale interval, then refined sampled local maxima. The shared connection limits imply a finite scale interval.

Sequential updates from the equal split moved ownership toward I3. At the final restricted candidate:

| Investor | MW |
|---|---:|
| I1 | 0 |
| I2 | 0 |
| I3 | 186.97137394 |

The largest sampled scalar deviation gain was approximately EUR 9e-6/day under HiGHS clearing. This is a restricted finite-search candidate, not a global scalar certificate and not a full-game equilibrium.

A subsequent screen tested 343 candidate patterns per investor, including exit, portfolio scaling, and single-node power/duration choices. Fresh IPOPT checks confirmed:

- I2 gains EUR 1,358.82/day by adding 0.5 MW / 1 MWh at N5.
- I3 gains EUR 1,248.02/day by replacing its N6 position with 60 MW / 360 MWh.
- No tested merchant entry was profitable; the LP bound in Section 3 establishes its exit more strongly.

### 4.2 Single-start Jacobi sensitivity

Four Jacobi sweeps at damping 0.25 were completed from the planner equal split at rho = 100, epsilon = 1e-8, and MPEC tolerance = 1e-8. Each sweep used a single MPEC start per investor, plus the incumbent clearing screen.

The resulting fleet was 187.159583 MW / 563.136358 MWh. The final sweep's maximum reported deviation gain was EUR 25,296.53/day. I3's embedded/recleared profit discrepancy was still about EUR 53.04/day. Tightening epsilon alone did not eliminate the difficulty.

The driver then entered its automatic multistart audit. That process was stopped following the user's no-multistart instruction. No completed final audit exists or is used.

### 4.3 Nodal and duration changes

A deterministic coordinate search then screened nodal capacity patterns at rho = 0. Every accepted move was confirmed with fresh IPOPT clearing. No MPEC multistarts were used.

The final saved candidate is:

| Investor | MW | MWh |
|---|---:|---:|
| I1 | 0 | 0 |
| I2 | 38.602193 | 114.949591 |
| I3 | 188.405499 | 690.942019 |
| Total | 227.007691 | 805.891610 |

Its exact-cleared profits are approximately I1 = 0, I2 = EUR 71,357.25/day, I3 = EUR 123,471.49/day.

**It is not an equilibrium.** At this final profile, increasing only I2's N5 capacity by 0.001 MW / 0.003 MWh gains EUR 626.598/day; increasing it by 1 MW / 3 MWh gains EUR 747.242/day. Those are fresh IPOPT clear-and-settle comparisons.

The coordinate search is useful for finding counterexamples and locating active directions. Zero gain in such a finite screen would not be a full best-response certificate.

## 5. I2/I3 response geometry and a verified non-quasiconcavity witness

The next restricted map frees I2's N5 MW at 3 hours and I3's N6 MW at its current duration, approximately 3.79834 hours. All other entries are fixed at the saved first corrected coordinate-sweep profile.

The broad scalar scans cover the feasible interval and include small one-sided deviations around the incumbent. This matters: an initial coarse version missed profitable narrow branches and suggested an earlier switch near 45 MW. The final one-sided version supersedes that result.

After 12 bracket refinements:

| Side | I2 N5 MW | I3 sampled reply at N6 MW | I2 selected response MW | I2 gain, verified by IPOPT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Left | 45.13671875 | 51.92654867 | 45.13672148 | 562.9532 EUR/day |
| Right | 45.14160156 | 51.94219740 | 0 | 2,508.3682 EUR/day |

The left response residual is only 2.73e-6 MW, despite a EUR 563/day improvement. The right reply jumps to exit. A sign change of this sampled composed response is therefore not a certificate of a fixed point inside the bracket.

The stronger payoff finding is an explicit feasible three-point witness. Hold I3's N6 capacity at 51.92654867 MW and all other entries fixed. I2 changes only N5 capacity, with energy always three times power:

| I2 N5 MW | I2 profit, fresh IPOPT |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 71,621.1524 EUR/day |
| 45.13671975 | 71,305.5722 EUR/day |
| 45.13672148 | 71,868.5231 EUR/day |

The middle strategy is a convex combination of the endpoints, yet earns EUR 315.58/day less than the worse endpoint. Thus I2's payoff is not quasiconcave on this line, to the demonstrated numerical accuracy. A coarser earlier verified witness had a EUR 314.59/day violation.

Standard GNE existence results require conditions on payoffs and feasible-set correspondences; continuity alone is insufficient. See [Facchinei and Kanzow, Section 4.1](https://www.mathematik.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/10040700/paper/Facchinei_Kanzow_AORP.pdf). Failure of a sufficient condition is **not** proof of nonexistence. Discontinuous games can have equilibria under additional assumptions; see [Reny (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00069).

The diagnostic text in probe_br_slice.py and scan_threshold.py was corrected to avoid treating finite-grid sign changes, absent sign changes, continuity, or quasiconcavity as necessary-and-sufficient existence tests.

## 6. The user's node-migration hypothesis

Transfers moved power AND energy together between two nodes while holding investor totals, ownership and rivals fixed. Seven transfers were cleared per investor/pair/rho, including complete relocation in each direction.

### N3 and N9

At the tested planner and earlier-exit profiles, their hourly LMPs are equal to numerical precision, including after one location is emptied. The tests do not show an abandoned N3 or N9 acquiring a greater arbitrage spread than the occupied node.

However, equal current prices do not imply complete strategic equivalence:

- At the planner split, transfers leave ISO cost unchanged within about 2e-9 EUR/day but change IPOPT-selected prices. I3's own profit varies by up to EUR 227.62/day along the tested transfer. This is consistent with price-selection sensitivity on an optimal face.
- At the earlier exit profile, I3's N3 battery has approximately 5.616 hours and N9 approximately 2.844 hours. Moving capacity also changes how power and energy are combined at each location. At rho = 0, some transfers lower ISO cost by EUR 1.7488/day, while large transfers reduce I3 profit by about EUR 679.66/day and change some prices by EUR 5/MWh.
- At rho = 100, the tested N3/N9 transfers change I3 profit by up to EUR 915.42/day, despite the two nodes still having equal contemporaneous prices.

The interpretation involving changed battery flexibility follows from the capacity model and differing durations; the transfer experiment alone is not a full decomposition of every operating effect.

### N6 and N8, and the saved iteration history

N6/N8 transfers have much larger economic effects. At the earlier exit profile they change I3 profit by up to EUR 44,178/day at rho = 0 and EUR 44,145/day at rho = 100. These are magnitudes across sampled transfers, not automatically profitable gains.

The earlier saved trajectory also does not show simple N3/N9 hopping. After its initial move, I3's N3 capacity stays near 11.57515 MW. Larger changes occur among N9, N6 and N8. From sweep 4 to 5, roughly 11.90 MW moves toward N9 while N6/N8 decrease by about 4.43/7.47 MW.

So the user's migration mechanism is worth monitoring, but it has not been established as the main cause here. True equal-payoff reallocation, price-selection sensitivity, heterogeneous battery durations, and congestion-changing moves need separate diagnostics.

## 7. A more defensible method from here

1. Use the saved planner equal split as the documented default initialization. Preserve nodal MW/MWh and record the effective rho.
2. Define the exact market-price selection convention. The optimistic MPEC price choice and the procedural IPOPT reclear must represent the same game before claiming equilibrium in the rho-zero case.
3. At an I1-exit candidate, use the incremental-expansion LP bound. If its upper bound is within tolerance, I1's exit is verified globally at those rivals without an MPEC search.
4. Concentrate computation on I2 and I3. Track active congestion changes, use coarse scans to locate branches, then evaluate both sides of the boundaries at shrinking step sizes. A smooth scalar optimizer or a coarse grid alone can miss profitable narrow branches.
5. Test changes in power, energy, location and ownership response separately. The planner ray is useful for initialization and diagnosis, but it conceals profitable off-shape changes.
6. Measure gains after exact reclearing at the SAME fixed-rival profile. A tiny capacity residual, stable profits between sweeps, or an incumbent winning a failed search is insufficient.
7. Where multiple responses have genuinely equal profit under a consistent clearing rule, an explicit closest-to-incumbent selection can avoid gratuitous relocation. Do not add an economic penalty merely to hide profitable moves, and do not merge N3/N9 without checking feasible operating sets and duration effects.
8. A final full-game claim needs controlled best-response errors for the generation owners. The current finite scans provide lower bounds on regret, not global upper bounds. No additional multistart run is proposed.

## 8. What was not established

- No full-game equilibrium, global uniqueness, or nonexistence proof.
- No complete characterization of the planner's optimal face.
- No global smoothness or price-uniqueness theorem for positive rho.
- No proof that I1 exits at every possible rival profile, or under the user's unspecified older configuration.
- No completed multistart audit of the new candidates.
- No claim that the coordinate-search endpoint is close to equilibrium: its explicit profitable deviation is material.
- No change to the maintained ISO economics, investor costs, ownership, or production best-response algorithm.
