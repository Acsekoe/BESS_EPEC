# Profit cliffs traced to generation rents; demand response is the strongest tested remedy

**Written:** 2026-09-11 11:10 CEST (UTC+02:00)
**Project:** capacity_epec
**Reference:** market_data_smoothed.json; energy-only; inelastic demand (rho = 0).

**Constraint:** no multistart searches; none performed.

## Status

No full-game equilibrium is established, and neither existence nor multiplicity has been proved. This was a targeted investigation of the proposed remedies, not a new unrestricted equilibrium search. Multiple response branches do not establish multiple equilibria.

The saved jumps reproduce under fresh IPOPT clearing. The strongest result is an exact decomposition: price changes on existing renewable portfolios dominate the gains, while physical output and operating-cost changes are tiny.

The N5 move changes the marginal value of L46 in hours 12–16. A physical line can already be at its limit while its congestion multiplier changes from zero to positive. A simple binding-line flag can miss this mechanism.

## Remedy findings

**A consistent minimum-norm LMP rule does not eliminate the cliffs.** Residual-audited selections retain an I3 planner gain of about EUR 33,397.73/day and the I2 N5 gain of EUR 626.60/day. The rule is a fixed-profile numerical prototype, not an additional selection layer implemented inside the production MPEC. An optimistic MPEC and an external price rule would still need to be made consistent.

**Demand response strongly suppresses the original tiny-step gains.** At rho = 100, the identical planner perturbation gains EUR 0.000337/day and the identical N5 expansion loses EUR 0.015604/day. At the original profiles, adjustment is approximately 0.195–0.204% of daily load, reaching 1.265% in the most affected node-hour. Positive rho is an economic model change, not a neutral tie-break. Larger positive rho reduces adjustment but steepens transitions; rho = 0 is a separate inelastic switch.

The old I2 quasiconcavity witness disappears at rho = 25, 100 and 500. An 83-point screen of the feasible I2 N5 scalar interval finds no replacement violation for these rho cases and selects exit as the best sampled point. This is one unilateral line, not a global shape or equilibrium certificate.

**Operating curvature is not a general cure.** Generation marginal-cost increments of EUR 1 and EUR 5/MWh, storage operating curvature, and their combination all retain a large planner jump. Combined curvature leaves EUR 8,966.53/day of the original jump. It also yields a new fresh-IPOPT quasiconcavity violation: I2 profits at N5 capacities 0, 5 and 12.5 MW are EUR 72,160.66580, EUR 72,150.68607 and EUR 72,259.29719/day. The middle point violates quasiconcavity by EUR 9.97973/day. The report explains analytically why strict primal curvature does not guarantee continuous marginal prices and why smooth investment costs cannot remove a revenue jump.

**Scenario averaging spreads thresholds but finite grids retain them.** The completed study uses 36,380 LP screens across demand stresses of ±0.1%, ±1%, ±5% and wind/solar stresses of ±1%, with 9, 33 and 65 scenarios. Under the explicitly stated HiGHS convention, the planner tiny-step gain with ±1% demand uncertainty is EUR 1,079.76/day at 33 scenarios and EUR 548.19/day at 65. The central scenario contributes a jump with weight 1/N.

An additional 1,804-screen parity check finds almost zero gain at 32/64/128 scenarios, which omit the central threshold, versus EUR 1,079.76/548.19/276.22 at 33/65/129 scenarios, which include it. This is a sampling trap, not proof of continuity or equilibrium. Expected deviation gains need quadrature and scenario-weight refinement around thresholds.

## Numerical qualifications

- Main before/after tables use fresh reference IPOPT settlement. Screening prices are not silently substituted. The central scenario planner jump is EUR 35,632.18/day under HiGHS, versus EUR 34,348.75/day under reference IPOPT.
- A direct minimum-norm dual QP sometimes returned `Solved` with unacceptable raw residuals. Those results are rejected; accepted selections use a reduced optimal face with explicit residual and cutoff checks.
- A loose 1e-6 MW active cutoff can admit the wrong branch. The accepted 1e-8 and 1e-10 cutoffs agree at the important selections.
- Extremely small bracket results reach numerical limits. Rho = 100 shows a millieuro-scale floor in one refinement; this is not a proof of a residual economic jump. Curvature cases retain much larger sensitivity, including in the original, larger brackets.
- No global price uniqueness, smoothness, quasiconcavity, best-response bound or equilibrium certificate is inferred from a finite screen.

## Next step

Use the inelastic case as a benchmark and investigate a calibrated, bounded demand-response model near the rho = 100 sensitivity, combined with threshold-sensitive integration over operating uncertainty. Evaluate best responses through the same complete clearing convention, audit unrestricted unilateral deviation gains, and recompute the merchant bound whenever rivals or the market specification change. Damping, quadratic investment penalties, or a small fixed scenario count should not be treated as having removed the price mechanism.
