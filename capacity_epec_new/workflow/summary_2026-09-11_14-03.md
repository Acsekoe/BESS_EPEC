# Stable capacity totals mask a steep payoff boundary

**Written:** 2026-09-11 14:03 CEST (UTC+02:00)  
**Project:** `BESS_EPEC/capacity_epec_new`  
**Run:** `model/output/init5mw15mwh_jacobi_rho100_d025_s100`  
**Configuration:** capacity-only relaxed-KKT, energy-only market, symmetric 5 MW / 15 MWh initialization, Jacobi updates, damping `0.25`, demand-adjustment penalty `rho = 100`, complementarity epsilon `1e-3`, 100 sweeps, no multistart during ordinary sweeps.

## Executive assessment

The endpoint is not a near-equilibrium that merely needs a few more sweeps. The iteration has approached a steep payoff/price boundary: aggregate capacities and, at times, raw capacity deviations become small while profitable unilateral deviations remain economically large. Extending the same single-start trajectory without first characterizing that boundary is therefore not well motivated.

The final profile is rejected as a Nash candidate. This is not caused only by the final multistart search. The incumbent-start local response already finds most of the reported regret, especially for I3. The multistart audit discovers an additional, more distant I3 duration/location branch, but it is not the primary reason the candidate fails.

No pure-strategy equilibrium has been found. The evidence does not prove nonexistence.

## Capacity and regret trajectory

The final aggregate fleet is 187.339618 MW / 562.672552 MWh:

| Investor | Power (MW) | Energy (MWh) | Duration (h) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| I1 | 59.955313 | 170.357073 | 2.8414 |
| I2 | 54.755695 | 167.815945 | 3.0648 |
| I3 | 72.628610 | 224.499534 | 3.0911 |

Aggregate stability is misleading. At sweep 44 the maximum raw response residual was already only 0.384394 MW / 0.832305 MWh, inside the configured capacity tolerances, yet the maximum measured unilateral gain was still EUR 23,143.23/day. The trajectory subsequently left that neighborhood and produced new response spikes. At sweep 100 the raw residual was 1.013802 MW / 3.825837 MWh and the single-start regret monitor still reported EUR 32,654.14/day.

Thus a small capacity residual, stable total fleet size, or slowly moving investor totals does not imply a small Nash gap in this game.

## Final audit and the role of multistart

| Investor | Current profit (EUR/day) | Selected deviation profit (EUR/day) | Gain (EUR/day) | Max nodal change (MW / MWh) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I1 | 1,920.92 | 3,572.68 | 1,651.76 | 0.197 / 0.350 |
| I2 | 73,564.63 | 75,764.69 | 2,200.07 | 7.033 / 31.736 |
| I3 | 90,099.79 | 125,941.73 | 35,841.94 | 16.439 / 105.934 |

The incumbent-start refinement at the same final profile already gives I3 approximately EUR 122,773.61/day, about EUR 32,674/day above the incumbent payoff, with a response close to the final single-start sweep response. The distant `duration_8h` branch selected by multistart raises this to EUR 125,941.73/day. Multistart therefore adds roughly EUR 3,168/day to the discovered I3 deviation, but most of the rejection is already visible on the incumbent branch.

For I1, refinements from the incumbent and from several other starts converge to essentially the same local response. That response changes nodal capacity by at most 0.197 MW / 0.350 MWh while increasing profit by EUR 1,651.76/day. This is direct evidence of an extremely steep local payoff transition rather than merely a missed distant start.

## What `rho = 100` did and did not fix

Demand adjustment is active in the final market. Total absolute adjustment is approximately 25.7916 MWh, or 0.1956% of total daily load, and the largest node-hour adjustment is 0.47 MW.

The positive-rho model softened the previously studied exact price cliff along specific paths, but it did not make the current strategic payoff surface benign. A high penalty permits little physical demand movement and maps a small demand adjustment change into a large price change. At the final profile, sub-MW capacity changes can still move some LMPs by more than EUR 10/MWh and produce four-digit daily profit gains.

This may be a very steep continuous transition rather than a literal discontinuity. That distinction must be established with shrinking-step path scans at the current endpoint; it cannot be inferred from the Jacobi history alone.

## Embedded-MPEC versus exact-reclear consistency

The final audit reports embedded/recleared deviation-profit gaps of:

| Investor | Absolute gap (EUR/day) | Audit tolerance (EUR/day) |
|---|---:|---:|
| I1 | 8.94 | 10 |
| I2 | 26.09 | 10 |
| I3 | 224.76 | 10 |

I2 and I3 therefore fail the configured consistency check in addition to failing the regret and capacity-response checks. All three deviation solves terminate optimally, bound violations are negligible, and the lower-level primal-dual gaps are about EUR 2/day. Nevertheless, small lower-level price inaccuracies are magnified by the large owned-renewable portfolios.

Candidate selection compares exact recleared profits across returned candidates, but each refinement is optimized using the embedded MPEC objective. If embedded prices and procedural reclear prices disagree, the local NLP is not optimizing exactly the payoff ultimately used by the audit. Tightening epsilon and solver tolerances should be tested, but earlier tight-tolerance evidence still showed a material I3 gap, so tolerance tightening alone must not be assumed to solve the selection issue.

## Other qualifications

- Shared connection limits are not driving the endpoint: the highest nodal utilization is only about 26.6% of the 200 MW limit.
- All incumbent profits are positive, so individual rationality is not the problem.
- This run used the symmetric 5 MW / 15 MWh start, not the saved planner-equal-split initialization. It represents one trajectory and one basin only.
- Flat incumbent profits would not itself identify a Nash region. A trajectory can sit in a locally flat or numerically selected market regime while a profitable deviation exists elsewhere.

## Recommended next investigation

Before another long Jacobi run:

1. Reconstruct the exact feasible investment region from the maintained code, including nonnegativity, 2--8 hour power/energy coupling, shared nodal power limits, and every artificial or data-derived bound.
2. Characterize the final profile's local payoff geometry with exact reclearing. Scan shrinking steps from the incumbent toward the incumbent-start I1 and I3 responses, and separately toward the selected distant I3 branch.
3. Repeat those local scans for a small documented set of demand-response penalties, including `rho = 25`, `50`, and `100`. Separate storage operating surplus, owned-generation rent, capex, active congestion, LMP changes, and dispatch changes.
4. Distinguish three concepts: stable profits between iterations, a local payoff plateau under all feasible directions in a stated neighborhood, and a low-regret Nash region. Only the third answers the equilibrium question.
5. Test tighter relaxed-KKT settings and the maintained strong-duality alternative, if supported, until embedded and exact-reclear profits agree to a defensible tolerance. Do not loosen the audit tolerance to hide the discrepancy.
6. Only after the payoff rule is consistent, compare the symmetric and planner-equal-split initializations and introduce periodic multistart or seed the iteration with audited deviation branches.

The immediate priority is a boundary-aware feasible-region and payoff-regime analysis, not a blind extension from 100 to 200 single-start sweeps.
