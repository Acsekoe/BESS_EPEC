# No low-regret region near the rho = 100 endpoint: a steep, continuous, adversarial congestion boundary

**Written:** 2026-09-11 16:49 CEST (UTC+02:00)
**Project:** `capacity_epec_new`
**Run analysed:** `model/output/init5mw15mwh_jacobi_rho100_d025_s100` (final profile = state after sweep 100)
**Reference data:** `model/input/market_data_smoothed.json` (sha256 `b9837000...d402`, identical to the run)
**Reference market setting:** energy-only, demand-adjustment penalty `rho = 100`; sensitivities at `rho = 50` and `25`
**Reference MPEC relaxation:** relaxed KKT, complementarity epsilon `1e-3`, IPOPT tolerance `1e-4` (the run's settings); strong duality and tighter settings tested
**Diagnostics:** scripts in `diagnostics/regret_region/`, all outputs in `diagnostics/regret_region/output/`
**Maintained model files:** economic equations and defaults unchanged. On request, one opt-in lower-level formulation (`relaxed-strong-duality`) was added to `model/mpec.py` and `model/run_model.py`. No multistart Jacobi run was started.

## Executive status

**Conclusion: no defensible stable or low-regret region exists near this endpoint, and none was found along the explored feasible directions.** Profits and deviation gains do not stop moving:
- **Final profile:** exact-reclear unilateral gains stay at the thousands-to-tens-of-thousands EUR/day level.
- **Payoff sensitivity:** random joint perturbations of 0.01 MW already change I3's profit by a median 2,692 EUR/day.
- **Other candidates:** every candidate constructed around the identified market boundary (five profiles, 2,191 feasible probes plus MPEC refinements) leaves at least two investors with gains above 2,000 EUR/day.
- **Undamped best responses:** they cycle between a congested and a decongested midday corridor regime instead of settling. After three rounds I1 and I2 are at, or certifiably within 179 EUR/day of, their best responses, but I3 still gains 43,563 EUR/day by decongesting.

- **The final profile is not a Nash equilibrium, and the regret is not a numerical artifact.** With exact lower-level optimality (strong duality) and exact reclear, the best found unilateral gains are I1 **1,660.6 EUR/day (86.4 % of its profit)**, I2 **2,225.8 EUR/day (3.0 %)**, I3 **36,063.0 EUR/day (40.0 %)**. I1's regret is also bounded from above: the merchant-entry bound gives **at most 1,680.5 EUR/day**.
- **The payoff surface is continuous but extremely steep across one identified boundary**: the midday export congestion of the solar corridors L46 (N4-N6) and L78 (N7-N8), hours 11-16. Secant slopes reach about 37,000 EUR/day per MW on I3's local path and 237,000 EUR/day per MW on its duration-8h branch. Shrinking brackets down to widths of 1e-9 in the path parameter find no material discontinuity: the largest residual is 1.22 EUR/day, and 0.43 EUR/day when re-solved with MA27.
- **The incentives on that boundary are opposed.** I3 gains by relieving the midday congestion, which lifts solar-node prices from about 12-14 to about 23 EUR/MWh (+35.9k PV rent). I1 and I2 gain by deepening it, which lowers midday prices to about 5-11 EUR/MWh; each such move costs I3 14.5k-22.9k EUR/day. In sweeps 51-100 the Jacobi iterate alternates across the L46 hour-14 congestion switch while I1 and I3 trade ownership at N3 and N8 at nearly constant node totals.
- **The embedded/reclear mismatch is a price-selection effect of epsilon = 1e-3, not a reclear or tolerance defect.** The audit gaps (I1 8.9, I2 26.1, I3 224.8 EUR/day) decompose almost entirely into more favourable embedded LMPs at the investor's own nodes (0.07-0.26 EUR/MWh). They shrink to 0.0-0.1 EUR/day under strong duality. Tightening only the IPOPT tolerance does not reduce them. An aggregate relaxation primal - dual <= delta behaves the same way: the distortion grows with delta (about 170 x delta for I1) and is acceptable only at delta <= 1e-3 EUR/day.
- **The ISO optimal face is a singleton for settlements at every tested profile.** Dual- and primal-face LP ranges vanish with the optimality band, so no price or dispatch selection freedom exists at rho > 0 here.
- **The maintained reclear accepts inexact optima near sub-milliwatt "ghost" capacities** (primal-dual gaps up to 30.8 EUR/day). Re-solving with MA27 bounds the resulting profit error by 0.87 EUR/day on all 2,475 path samples, so no conclusion depends on it.
- **Lower rho does not produce a low-regret region.** It raises physical demand adjustment from 0.20 % to 0.76 % of load and flattens local slopes roughly in proportion to rho. The run-setting gains are I3 47-51k and I2 2.3k at rho = 50, and I3 52-55k and I2 3.0k at rho = 25.
- **No pure equilibrium was found; nonexistence is not proved.**

## 1. The feasible investment region

Full derivation with code references: `diagnostics/regret_region/feasible_region.md`. Measured faces: `output/feasible_region/`.

For investor i and node n the maintained code imposes exactly:

| | Constraint | Enforced in |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | P_in >= 0, E_in >= 0 | MPEC variable domains, exact screen, clipping of responses |
| C2 | 2 P_in <= E_in <= 8 P_in | MPEC `energy_ratio_min/max`, screen (tolerance 1e-7) |
| C3 | sum_i P_in <= 200 MW (shared, per node) | MPEC `node_connection_limit`, `_check_profile_node_limits` before every clear |

There is no budget, maximum size, integrality, or energy coupling. At rho > 0 the ISO QP is feasible for every capacity vector, so the market imposes no implicit restriction. Artificial or algorithmic bounds exist only in the solver layer: MPEC price bounds of +/-500 EUR/MWh, other duals within +/-1e4, the epsilon relaxation, the 1e-6 MW iterate cleanup (not applied to best responses) and the 1e-8 MW sparse-rival omission.

**Geometry.** With rivals fixed, C2 implies C1. Investor i's set is the product of nine triangles, conv{(0,0), (R_n, 2R_n), (R_n, 8R_n)}, where R_n is the residual shared cap. It is polyhedral, convex, compact and 18-dimensional, with 3^9 vertices. The joint set is a 54-dimensional compact polytope that factorises by node; investors are coupled only through the nine shared power inequalities.

**Faces at the final profile.**
- Shared cap: slack everywhere (highest utilisation 26.6 %, slack >= 146.8 MW for every investor).
- 8 h face: nothing within 2 %.
- 2 h face: I2 at N3 (2.002 h), I1 at N9 (2.029 h), I3 at N8 (2.030 h).
- Material positions: N3, N6, N8, N9 (I3 has only a ghost at N9).
- Origin: every other coordinate is at the origin vertex or a ghost of 1e-6 to 1e-3 MW, where only entry directions are feasible.

The effective local dimension is 8 coordinates for I1 and I2 and 6 for I3. Because the shared cap is far from binding, the local game is an ordinary Nash problem; the generalized (shared-constraint) structure matters only globally.

**Why the complete model is a generalized Nash complementarity problem anyway.**
- C3 makes strategy sets depend on rivals.
- Payoffs are defined through the optimal primal-dual solution of a parametric convex QP. They are nonsmooth and nonconcave across ISO active-set boundaries, and depend on prices at owned generators.
- Each best response is an MPEC with complementarity constraints that violate constraint qualifications.
- The three MPECs share one market, so the equilibrium conditions are a nonconvex two-level complementarity system.

Compactness and convexity of the capacity polytope therefore do not deliver existence.

## 2. What "profits do not move" has to mean

Three different statements are easily conflated. For investor i with payoff pi_i:

1. **Iterate stability:** |pi_i(x^{k+1}) - pi_i(x^k)| is small between successive Jacobi iterates.
2. **Local flatness:** sup over feasible directions d in the tangent cone of K with ||d|| <= r of |pi_i(x_i + d, x_-i) - pi_i(x)| is small. This is payoff sensitivity, in both directions.
3. **Low-regret Nash region:** for all x in a stated neighbourhood U and all i,
   R_i(x) = sup over y_i in K_i(x_-i) of pi_i(y_i, x_-i) - pi_i(x) <= tau_abs, and R_i(x) / max(1, |pi_i(x)|) <= tau_rel,
   with pi_i evaluated by exact reclear.

Only statement 3 supports an equilibrium claim, and it must hold per investor in both absolute and relative terms. The run's tolerance is tau_rel = 0.1 %.

The first two are demonstrably misleading here:
- **Iterate stability versus regret.** In sweeps 28-37 of the run the largest incumbent profit change between sweeps was only 1.3-19.1 EUR/day, while single-start regret stayed at 22,376-22,385 EUR/day. At sweep 44 the raw response residual was 0.38 MW, inside tolerance, with 23.1k regret (`output/figures/fig6_history_regret_vs_residual.png`).
- **Local flatness versus regret.** Along the segment between I2's two audited responses, every investor's profit changes by at most 0.2 EUR/day although positions move by up to 6.4 MW / 29.2 MWh. That is a genuinely flat set for I2, yet I2's exact regret at the final profile is 2,226 EUR/day.
- **Relative versus absolute.** I1's absolute regret is the smallest of the three, but its relative regret (86 %) is by far the largest, and its gain is certified by an upper bound. Scaling by the renewable owners' profits would hide it.

**Flatness at the final profile.** The final profile is not even locally flat. Sixteen random feasible joint perturbations per radius, with all three investors moving together (`output/region/joint_perturbations_final.csv`, `output/figures/fig7_joint_perturbation_flatness.png`), gave:

| Radius | I3 own-profit change | I1 own-profit change | I2 own-profit change | Max LMP change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 MW | median 2,692, max 3,856 EUR/day (4.3 %) | median 199, max 286 (14.9 %) | median 158, max 321 | 2.0 EUR/MWh |
| 0.1 MW | up to 32,697 (36 %) | up to 2,327 (121 %) | up to 2,525 | 16.2 EUR/MWh |

Finite-difference gradients point in opposite directions:
- **I3:** +31.7k EUR/day per MWh at N3, +21.1k at N6, +36.4k at N8.
- **I1:** negative in every coordinate (for example -6.9k per MW at N6, -2.4k per MWh at N8).
- **I2:** negative in every coordinate except E_N8 (+158) and P_N9 (+20), e.g. -8.9k per MW at N6 (`output/region/fd_gradients.csv`).

Stable totals, stable iterate profits and the flat I2 bridge thus coexist with a payoff surface that moves by thousands of EUR/day per 0.01 MW.

## 3. Accuracy of the exact reclear and uniqueness of settlements

**Reproducibility.** Every audited profit reproduces with difference 0.000 EUR/day. Repeated identical clears give identical LMPs and profits.

**Exactness.** The ISO KKT residuals were evaluated from the returned duals:
- **Incumbent:** primal-dual gap 1.7e-8 EUR/day, complementarity 6.5e-9.
- **Seven of twelve audited refinement profiles:** the maintained reclear reports `optimal` with gaps of 2.1-30.0 EUR/day and complementarity up to 0.17. The cause is idle ghost batteries (4e-7 to 6e-7 MW) whose power-bound multipliers reach -2.3e4 to -4.2e5 EUR/MW. IPOPT accepts them on its scaled error (7.5e-12 scaled vs 4.8e-7 unscaled dual infeasibility).
- **The same QPs re-solved:** with MA27 or bound-push options they reach gaps of about 4e-9. LMPs change by at most 3e-4 EUR/MWh and profits by at most 0.12 EUR/day at the audited profiles.
- **Path samples:** 1,061 of 2,475 reference clears were flagged; the worst verified profit error is 0.87 EUR/day.
- HiGHS via Pyomo appsi cannot solve the quadratic ISO objective.

## 5. Active-set regions and their boundaries

**Regime segments** (price-forming congestion set constant, `output/paths/regime_segments.csv`). At rho = 100:
- **I1 and I2 paths:** three segments; L46 hour 15 becomes priced near s = 0.0075-0.01, and L46 hour 14 near s = 0.01-0.02.
- **I3 incumbent-start path:** one congestion set holds for s in [0, 0.92]; L46 hour 15 then flips on and off three times before s = 1, and L78 hours 12-13 are released at s = 0.998.
- **I3 duration_8h path:** four segments.
  - s = 0.006-0.0075: L78 hours 12-13 released.
  - s = 0.021: L46 hours 11-13, 16 and L78 hour 15 released.
  - s = 0.925-0.95: L78 hour 14 released.

**Inside each segment the profit is smooth and nearly linear in s; the steep changes occur at and between these switches.** Generator-marginality changes (mostly G_N1_a switching between off and marginal in hours 11 and 16, and PV units between capacity and marginal) occur far more often. They create slope changes but no jumps.

**Bracket classification.** 61 brackets were traced by bisection to widths of 3e-10 to 1.5e-9 in s (`output/paths/bracket_classification.csv`, `output/figures/fig3_bracket_scaling_rho100.png`):

| Classification | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| change shrinks to zero with width (log-log slope about 1) | 32 | continuous |
| steep continuous | 2 | continuous, slopes 5.7e4 / 2.1e5 EUR/day per MW (I3 duration_8h, rho 25/50) |
| residual below reclear resolution | 25 | residual 0.0003-0.16 EUR/day stops shrinking; all below the 0.87 EUR/day verified reclear error |
| jump-like residual above resolution | 1 | I3 duration_8h, rho 100: 1.22 EUR/day across 1.5e-9 where L46/L78 hour 14 switch and G_N2_a leaves its cap; the exact MA27 settlements differ by only 0.43 EUR/day |
| reclear error too large to classify | 1 | I2 incumbent-start, rho 100 |

**Classification of the apparent cliffs:**
- **Reproducible discontinuity:** none of material size at rho >= 25. The largest residual is 1.22 EUR/day under the reference solve and 0.43 EUR/day exactly.
- **Steep continuous transition:** all large profit changes. They are Lipschitz in capacity with slopes of 10^3-10^5 EUR/day per MW, rising roughly in proportion to rho.
- **Change of selected price or dispatch:** excluded, because face ranges are below 1e-5 EUR/day at every bracket endpoint.
- **Numerical or relaxation artifact:** the reference reclear's ghost-capacity inexactness is real but bounded (<= 0.87 EUR/day). The MPEC's epsilon relaxation is an artifact of the embedded payoff only (section 7).

The loaded nodes are N5, N6 and N8; at rho > 0 their LMPs equal rho times the demand adjustment. Halving rho doubles the physical adjustment and roughly halves the local price and profit slopes, because the same capacity perturbation moves prices by about rho times the induced adjustment. Two things do **not** fall with rho:
- the size of I3's congestion-relief prize, since the uncongested price is set by the 23 EUR/MWh thermal step and not by rho;
- the investor-relative regret, which rises.

Lower rho makes the payoff surface easier to resolve numerically but not more stable strategically, and it is a real model change: at rho = 25 a node-hour's demand moves by up to 5 %. The rho value should be chosen by calibrating demand flexibility, not by convergence.

**Decomposition.** The symmetric price/quantity split is exact to its residual. At the run settings:
- I1: all 8.94 is a storage price effect.
- I2: 8.11 storage price effect + 18.00 wind-rent price effect.
- I3 selected start: +247.45 PV-rent price effect, -22.67 storage price effect.

Quantity effects are at most 0.01 EUR/day. The embedded LMPs differ from the exact ones by at most 0.066 (I1), 0.172 (I2) and 0.259 EUR/MWh (I3).

**Interpretation.** With epsilon = 1e-3 every complementarity product may be up to 1e-3, so the embedded lower level is only an epsilon-optimal market. The optimistic MPEC uses that slack to choose LMPs slightly more favourable at the investor's own nodes. Owned PV output of several GWh/day amplifies 0.26 EUR/MWh into about 225 EUR/day. Tightening the IPOPT tolerance does not remove the slack (the gap even grows), while tightening epsilon or imposing strong duality does.

**Consequence for search quality.** Better lower-level consistency also finds better responses:
- I1: 3,572.68 -> 3,581.49
- I2: 75,764.69 -> 75,790.47
- I3: 125,941.73 -> 126,162.80

Strong duality started from the incumbent finds I3's high branch, which the run's incumbent-start relaxation missed (122,773.61). The relaxed NLP at epsilon = 1e-3 therefore optimises a materially different payoff for I2 and I3. Exact-profit selection among its candidates does not repair that, because the refinement direction itself is biased and it converges to different local optima.

Very tight relaxations are fragile: at epsilon <= 1e-5, 8 of 36 solves ended in IPOPT errors and 10 at the iteration limit, although several of the latter still returned accurate points. Strong duality warm-started from a capacity profile solved 22 of 24 cases cleanly (one IPOPT error, one iteration limit with an accurate point).

**The best responses and the NLP are close enough to the settlement game only under strong duality, epsilon <= 1e-5 finished with strong duality, or relaxed strong duality at delta <= 1e-3 EUR/day (below).**

**Relaxed strong duality** (added on request as opt-in `lower_level = "relaxed-strong-duality"`). The equality is relaxed to primal - dual <= delta (EUR/day). Because the gap equals the sum of all complementarity products, delta bounds the total slack.

The test ran 30 solves at the final profile: 3 investors x 2 starts x delta in {10, 1, 0.1, 0.01, 0.001}, IPOPT tol 1e-6 (`relaxed_sd_test.py`, `output/relaxed_strong_duality/relaxed_sd_variants.csv`). All were `optimal` and took 4-64 s each, against 48-297 s for strong duality.

|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 772.4 / 2,819.9 | 86.5 / 75,775.1 | 376.4 / 126,160.8 |
| 1 | 174.9 / 3,407.8 | 22.4 / 75,775.3 | 42.9 / 126,164.5 |
| 0.1 | 17.4 / 3,564.2 | 15.9 / 75,775.3 | 7.5 / 126,162.5 |

Findings:
- **The optimiser always spends the whole allowance** (gap = delta) and concentrates it in a handful of complementarity pairs. The largest single product is 0.5 delta for I1 and essentially all of delta for I2 and I3.
- **I1's valuation error grows about 170 x delta.** One EUR/day of cost slack moves its node LMPs by 3.1 EUR/MWh.
- **I3's valuation error is 40-75 x delta**, through the PV-rent price effect.
- **I2 keeps a floor of about 15 EUR/day** down to delta = 0.01, from a 0.17 EUR/MWh wind-node price shift. Joint primal-dual near-optimality is therefore more permissive than the one-sided face tests of section 3.
- **Large delta also misleads the search.** At delta = 10, I1's response is 761 EUR/day worse by exact reclear than under strong duality.
- **At delta = 0.001 it is practically strong duality.** Gaps are <= 4 EUR/day (9.9 for I3 from the duration-8h start), I3 finds its best response so far (126,165.8), solves are 2-10 x faster, and none failed.

**Use it as a continuation path (delta 0.1 -> 0.01 -> 0.001 -> strong duality) or at delta <= 1e-3 with exact-reclear acceptance, never at delta >= 0.01 as an endpoint.**

**I1 upper bound.** With I2 and I3 fixed, B = V(0) - min_x [V(x) + k_I1(x)] = 219,688.7101 - 216,087.2958 = **3,601.41 EUR/day**; MA57 and MA27 agree to 2e-10. B bounds I1's profit over *all* feasible capacities because the ISO value function is convex in capacity at rho >= 0. I1's global best-response profit therefore lies in [3,581.49, 3,601.41], and its regret at the final profile in [1,660.6, 1,680.5] EUR/day (relative 86.4-87.5 %). The minimising expansion is N3 13.6 MW / 38.4 MWh, N6 12.4 / 49.8, N8 20.4 / 44.8, N9 13.6 / 38.4.

| Family | Moves |
|---|---|
| Duration-preserving scaling | down to node exit |
| Power-only changes | inside $E/8\le P\le E/2$ |
| Energy-only changes | inside $2P\le E\le 8P$ |
| Transfers | fractions of node n's (P, E) moved to every other node |
| Entry | at every empty node, at 2, 4 or 8 h |

Every generated vector is retracted node by node onto {0 <= P <= residual cap, 2P <= E <= 8P} and re-checked with the maintained guard. The basis therefore covers expansion, contraction, relocation, duration and exit. Along audited paths, feasibility holds by convexity (section 4).

The dynamics therefore end in a profile where the two investors who prefer congestion are at, or within a certified 179 EUR/day of, their best responses, while I3 is 43.6k EUR/day away. **The obstacle to equilibrium is I3's congestion-relief incentive, not I1 or I2.**

A pure equilibrium would need one of two things:
- a profile from which I3 cannot profitably decongest, or
- a decongested profile from which I1 and I2 cannot profitably re-congest.

Neither was found in the candidates, slices or dynamics examined.

This is an explicit **best-response cycle across the midday congestion boundary**:
- I3 relieves congestion, by concentrating long-duration storage at N3 upstream of the solar corridors.
- That pushes I1 into a loss.
- I1 and I2 relocate so that their charging re-congests the corridors: I1 to N9, I2 to the solar nodes.
- I3's price support collapses (from about 126k to 79-85k EUR/day), and I3 decongests again with more storage (72.6 -> 93.5 MW over three rounds).

Every step gave the mover a gain of at least 846 EUR/day, and every I3 step at least 36k. The iteration does not approach a low-regret set; each full best response moves the market to the other regime.

**What prevents stability.** One moving active-set boundary: the midday congestion multipliers of L46 hours 11-16 and L78 hours 12-16.
- On the congested side, I3's PV-rent incentive to cross it is 30-46k EUR/day.
- On the decongested side, I1's merchant arbitrage cannot cover its capex. I1's and I2's best responses re-cross the boundary, worth 1.2-3.9k EUR/day.

Damped Jacobi hides this as slow ownership exchange; undamped best responses expose it as a cycle.

## 10. How to handle the geometry: ranked recommendation

The incentive conflict lives on a small number of congestion switches (L46 hours 11-16, L78 hours 12-16). The payoff rule becomes consistent once the lower level is solved exactly. The methods below are ranked by how directly they address that geometry without changing the economics.

**Algorithmic methods (the economic model is unchanged)**

1. **Make the best-response payoff consistent before anything else.**
   - Use strong duality, relaxed strong duality with delta <= 1e-3 EUR/day, or a continuation (epsilon 1e-3 -> 1e-5, or delta 0.1 -> 0.001) finished with strong duality, and accept candidates only by exact reclear.
   - Add a residual check to the reclear: primal-dual gap and complementarity in original units, with an MA27 re-solve when either exceeds 1e-3 EUR/day.
   - Clean ghost capacities below 1e-6 MW from best responses, as the iterate cleanup already does.

   Without these steps the epsilon = 1e-3 NLP optimises a payoff up to 225 EUR/day different from settlement and misses I3's high branch from the incumbent start.
2. **Market-regime (active-set) enumeration around the midday corridor switches.** Partition capacity space by the priced congestion set and the marginal-generator pattern. Inside a regime the ISO solution is affine in capacities and each payoff is a smooth quadratic, so regime-restricted best responses are low-dimensional and can be solved and bounded exactly. The equilibrium question then becomes a finite combinatorial one: is there a regime in which no investor gains by moving itself, or the market, into another regime? Section 5 shows only about 3-6 congestion regimes are visited per path. Together with the merchant bound this is the most informative next computation.
3. **Undamped Gauss-Seidel or continuation from audited deviation solutions**, with exact-reclear selection over structured exit, entry, transfer and duration starts plus strong-duality refinements. This either finds a mutually consistent regime (for example I3 decongesting with I1 contracting) or exhibits a best-response cycle with explicit profiles. The undamped run in section 9 already shows a best-response cycle across the congestion switch. The next use of this method is therefore to follow the cycle with regime labels, not to wait for convergence.
4. **Trust-region best responses with exact-reclear acceptance.** Limit the step in capacity space and accept only exact profit improvements. This controls crossings of the steep boundary without changing the payoff. It is preferable to a proximal penalty in the objective, which biases the regret and is correctly forbidden for regret-based stops; the final audit must still be unrestricted.
5. **Periodically multistarted Jacobi.** Useful only as a regret monitor. Damped simultaneous steps structurally trail an adversarial boundary, as sweeps 51-100 show. Use a regret-certified stop, never a capacity-residual stop.
6. **Epsilon homotopy as a warm-start device only.** Its endpoints must not be used as payoffs: epsilon slack is exactly what creates the optimistic price selection.
7. **An explicit ISO price/dispatch selection rule.** Not needed at rho > 0 in this data, where the faces are singletons. It becomes necessary if the inelastic benchmark (rho = 0) is revisited.

**Economic model changes (to be justified economically, not by convergence)**

8. **Homotopy or choice of rho.** It changes demand elasticity: 0.20 % -> 0.76 % of load adjusted, up to 5 % in a node-hour. It scales local slopes roughly in proportion to rho but leaves I3's congestion-relief prize intact and raises relative regret. Use a calibrated, bounded demand response, not rho as a convergence device.
9. **A different equilibrium concept if no pure equilibrium exists.** The options are:
   - mixed or correlated equilibria of a finite regime or capacity menu, computed with exact payoffs, e.g. by support enumeration or regret matching;
   - leader-follower timing (the PV owner as a Stackelberg leader);
   - a sequential-entry game;
   - an epsilon-Nash statement with explicitly reported epsilon per investor.

   Each changes the economic question and must be stated as such.

Quadratic investment costs, stronger damping and looser convergence tolerances are **not** recommended: they suppress visible movement without removing the congestion-relief incentive (section 6 and the 11:10 summary).

## 11. What was and was not proved

**Established, to the stated numerical accuracy:**
- **The feasible region's exact structure** (section 1), derived from the maintained code.
- **The final profile is not a Nash equilibrium.** Explicit feasible unilateral deviations with verified exact-reclear gains exist for every investor: I1 1,660.6, I2 2,225.8, I3 36,063.0 EUR/day. These are measured points, not bounds, and they exceed every plausible numerical error by three to four orders of magnitude.
- **I1's regret at the final profile is at most 1,680.5 EUR/day.** This upper bound holds for every feasible I1 capacity. It relies on convexity of the ISO QP in capacity and on the accuracy of two tight optimal values (MA57/MA27 spread 2e-10 EUR/day).
- **ISO settlements are unique** at every tested profile: the face ranges vanish with delta.
- **Continuity along the tested paths.** Profit changes are continuous down to the resolution of the reference reclear (0.87 EUR/day). The largest residual is 1.22 EUR/day under the reference solve and 0.43 EUR/day when re-solved with MA27.
- **Embedded/reclear gaps at epsilon = 1e-3 are price-selection slack**, and they vanish under strong duality at the tested profiles.
- **Explicit feasible deviations with verified gains at every candidate and dynamics step.** At each of the five candidate profiles at least two investors had verified deviations above 2,000 EUR/day, and at every dynamics step the mover gained at least 846 EUR/day. None of these profiles is an equilibrium. At each candidate I1's regret is bracketed by its best found response and the merchant bound.
- **An explicit best-response cycle** across the L46/L78 midday congestion switch, with exact-reclear payoffs (section 9).
- **I1's regret at the last dynamics profile lies in [76.2, 178.9] EUR/day**, bracketed by its best found response and the merchant bound. I3 has a verified 43,562.8 EUR/day deviation there.

**Not established:**
- **No global best responses for I2 or I3.** Their regrets are finite-search lower bounds; no upper bound exists because owned-generation rent breaks the merchant argument.
- **No existence or nonexistence proof for a pure equilibrium, and no certificate that a low-regret region exists or is absent elsewhere** in the 54-dimensional region. Continuity was established only along the sampled paths and slice, not over the whole region.
- **No calibrated demand response.** The rho values are sensitivities.
- **Only one trajectory.** The symmetric 5 MW start was analysed; the planner-equal-split start was not re-run.

**What further evidence would support a nonexistence argument.**
1. Enumerate the market regimes reachable within the relevant region, using the ISO active sets.
2. In each regime, compute exact regime-restricted best responses for all investors together with *upper* bounds on I2's and I3's gains, e.g. by bounding owned-generation rent through regime-wise price bounds. I1 already has one.
3. Show a positive lower bound on the maximum regret over a covering of the region, i.e. that the best-response correspondence has no fixed point there. Unbounded exits and entries must be closed by the merchant bound and by a price-cap argument.
4. Confirm the result under the exact lower level from several starts, including the planner equal split, and under a finer scenario representation if uncertainty is added.

Alternatively, compute equilibria of a finite capacity or regime approximation. Showing that all of them are mixed would suggest pure nonexistence, but the discontinuous-game approximation results (Reny) would still be needed to transfer the conclusion.

## Recommended next step

1. **Make the best-response payoff consistent** (section 10, item 1):
   - strong duality or epsilon-continuation ending in strong duality;
   - exact-reclear acceptance with a residual check and MA27 fallback;
   - ghost-capacity cleanup in responses.

   These are algorithmic changes to the search layer, not to the economic equations.
2. **Enumerate the midday corridor regimes reachable from the cycle profiles.** Start from the congested and decongested regimes seen in section 9 and the priced sets of `output/history/late_window_regimes.csv`. Per regime, compute exact regime-restricted best responses for all three investors, I1's merchant bound, and an upper bound on I2's and I3's owned-rent gains. This either finds a regime-consistent profile with certified low regret or turns the observed cycle into a documented regime-level best-response cycle, which is the evidence needed before arguing nonexistence of a pure equilibrium.
3. **Only then** decide whether the economic specification should change:
   - a calibrated, bounded demand response instead of rho = 100;
   - leader-follower timing for the PV owner;
   - or a mixed or epsilon-equilibrium statement.

   Each must be justified economically and not by convergence.
