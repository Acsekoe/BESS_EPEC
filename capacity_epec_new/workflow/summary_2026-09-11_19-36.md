## Executive result

I continued the earlier active-set/regime investigation by turning the observed investment portfolios into an exact finite normal-form game, auditing its pure equilibria against continuous deviations, adding the defeating deviations to the menus, and repeating the calculation twice.

The main result is:

> **Every restricted pure equilibrium found in the three finite menus is rejected by an explicit feasible unilateral deviation when searched in the continuous capacity space and re-cleared through the maintained ISO procedure.**

The menu equilibria also migrate when the newly found deviations are added:

| Menu | Strategies I1 / I2 / I3 | Exact cross-profiles | Congestion regimes | Restricted pure equilibria |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Base observed menu | 6 / 6 / 6 | 216 | 17 | profiles 33, 75, 129 |
| Oracle round 1 | 8 / 8 / 9 | 576 | 36 | profiles 195, 402 |
| Oracle round 2 | 9 / 10 / 11 | 990 | 45 | profile 406 |

All 1,782 cross-profiles were feasible and successfully cleared. A congestion regime in this table means a distinct set of priced line-hours.

This is stronger evidence than the earlier single trajectory that the apparent fixed points are missing-strategy artifacts. It is **not** a proof that the continuous game has no pure equilibrium: the strategy menus remain finite, and no global upper bounds exist for I2's or I3's regret.

No candidate found here satisfies a defensible low-regret criterion. The most structurally promising point is still oracle-round-1 profile 402, where I1 exits and its regret is bounded above by only 4.09 EUR/day. However, I2 and I3 have verified gains of 1,288.89 and 1,819.70 EUR/day there, so it is not an equilibrium or a low-regret region.

## 1. What was added

### Exact finite regime-menu game

`diagnostics/regret_region/regime_menu_game.py` constructs one finite strategy menu per investor from:

- the sweep-100 portfolio;
- that investor's portfolio after each of its three undamped response moves;
- the final-audit selected portfolio;
- exit, with zero power and energy at every node;
- in oracle rounds, continuous deviations found when auditing equilibria from all earlier rounds.

It removes duplicate capacity vectors, crosses the three menus, applies the shared node caps, and re-clears every feasible combination. It then computes each investor's exact best response **within that finite menu**, restricted-menu regret, restricted pure equilibria, priced-line regimes, and best-response regime transitions.

The script now supports repeatable double-oracle enrichment with `--oracle-round N`. Round `N` reads the continuous audit responses from the base menu and completed rounds `1,...,N-1`, preserving the source round in every strategy label.

### Continuous equilibrium audit

`diagnostics/regret_region/regime_menu_audit.py` reconstructs a selected menu profile and searches the original continuous feasible set using:

- structured feasible exit, entry, scale, transfer, duration, power and portfolio probes;
- strong-duality MPEC refinements;
- relaxed-KKT refinements where useful;
- exact procedural re-clearing for response selection;
- the merchant upper bound for I1.

The script supports any menu folder, selected profile IDs and investors, and resumable output. For I2 and I3, the resulting regret remains a lower bound because no global response upper bound is available.

## 2. Base menu: three finite equilibria, all spurious in continuous space

The base menu has six distinct strategies per investor and therefore 216 cross-profiles. All 216 cleared, producing 17 priced-line congestion regimes and three restricted pure equilibria.

| Profile | I1 / I2 / I3 strategies | I1 / I2 / I3 profits (EUR/day) | Continuous audit |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 33 | sweep100 / exit / step7 | 2,916.26 / 71,731.13 / 89,944.02 | gains 1,147.13 / 662.88 / 37,478.00 |
| 75 | step5 / sweep100 / step7 | 608.27 / 73,380.61 / 107,264.74 | gains 117.95 / 1,804.97 / 18,578.58 |
| 129 | step8 / step9 / step7 | 791.59 / 75,514.83 / 82,268.80 | I3 gains 43,562.78; the earlier final-profile audit also covers I1 and I2 |

Profiles 33 and 75 are rejected for all three investors. Profile 129 is the earlier last dynamics profile and is decisively rejected by I3 alone; a single profitable investor deviation is sufficient to disprove equilibrium.

The important observation is that these portfolios are mutual best responses only relative to the six available choices. Once the continuous response portfolios are admitted, all three equilibria disappear.

## 3. Oracle round 1: the original equilibria disappear, two new ones appear

Adding the distinct continuous deviations from the base audits expands the menus to 8 / 8 / 9 strategies and 576 exact cross-profiles. The number of priced-line regimes rises from 17 to 36. None of profiles 33, 75 or 129 remains a restricted equilibrium.

Two new restricted equilibria appear:

| Profile | I1 / I2 / I3 strategies | I1 / I2 / I3 profits (EUR/day) | Priced line-hours |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 195 | step5 / exit / oracle response to p33 | 1,049.61 / 71,952.62 / 98,889.34 | L46 h11-15; L78 h13-14 |
| 402 | exit / audit-selected / oracle response to p33 | approximately 0 / 71,903.28 / 125,641.24 | L46 h14 |

Continuous audits reject both:

| Profile | I1 gain | I2 gain | I3 gain | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 195 | 162.41 (15.47%) | 291.36 (0.405%) | 28,238.52 (28.56%) | all three deviate; I2 enters from exit |
| 402 | none found; regret upper bound 4.09 | 1,288.89 (1.79%) | 1,819.70 (1.45%) | I1 exit is nearly certified, but both active investors deviate |

For profile 195, the I2 relaxed-KKT response is supported by a directly re-cleared entry probe gaining 248.80 EUR/day, and its alternative-clear verification differs by only 0.0054 EUR/day. Thus the rejection does not rely on the complementarity relaxation.

Profile 402 is useful despite failing. It is the first examined profile where exit is essentially optimal for I1: no profitable entry was found and the merchant relaxation limits any missed I1 profit to 4.0855 EUR/day. The remaining problem is consistency between I2 and I3, not I1 entry.

## 4. Oracle round 2: one further finite equilibrium, again defeated

Adding the distinct responses from profiles 195 and 402 produces 9 / 10 / 11 strategies, or 990 cross-profiles. All 990 clear, spanning 45 congestion regimes. Profiles 195 and 402 disappear as restricted equilibria.

One new restricted equilibrium appears:

| Profile | I1 strategy | I2 strategy | I3 strategy | Profits I1 / I2 / I3 (EUR/day) | Priced line-hours |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 406 | step8 | base-audit response to p33 | round-1 response to p195 | 342.03 / 72,323.66 / 115,646.28 | L46 h13-14; L78 h14 |

Its continuous audit finds:

| Investor | Current profit | Best found | Regret lower bound | Relative regret | Response evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| I1 | 342.03 | 494.36 | 152.33 | 44.54% | strong duality; merchant regret upper bound 321.17 |
| I2 | 72,323.66 | 72,484.61 | 160.94 | 0.223% | strong duality |
| I3 | 115,646.28 | 127,080.83 | 11,434.56 | 9.89% | strong duality from `scale_N3_+0.1` |

The maintained reference clear flags the I1 and I3 response profiles as inexact and invokes the alternative verification clear. The largest reported profit difference is 2.31 EUR/day for I3, versus an 11,434.56 EUR/day gain; I1's difference is 0.15 EUR/day versus a 152.33 EUR/day gain. The equilibrium rejection is therefore robust to those clearing discrepancies.

Profile 406 fails for all three investors. As in the previous rounds, adding its response portfolios would remove at least this exact strategy combination, but another restricted equilibrium could appear elsewhere in a larger menu. That is why this continuation stops short of a nonexistence claim.

## 5. What this changes in the interpretation

### The regime conflict is not an ordering artifact

The earlier Gauss-Seidel trajectory alternated across L46/L78 midday congestion patterns. The finite games now allow all observed strategies to be combined in every order. Restricted equilibria do exist in each finite game, but they move or disappear whenever the missing continuous deviations are added. The congestion conflict is therefore not explained solely by the sequential order of the earlier trajectory.

### “Best-response cycle” was too strong

The previous report called the three-round trajectory an “explicit best-response cycle.” That wording overstates what was certified:

- the response searches are multistart and exact-reclear selected, but no global response upper bounds exist for I2 or I3;
- the sequence shows repeated regime alternation, not a return to an identical capacity state;
- at the last dynamics profile I2 had no better response **found**, but its best-response optimality was not certified.

The defensible description is:

> **a best-found improving-response regime alternation across the midday L46/L78 congestion boundary.**

The menu experiments strengthen the evidence for persistent regime conflict, but they still do not establish a mathematical best-response cycle.

### I3 remains the largest observed instability, but not the only one

I3 supplies the largest defeating deviation at profiles 33, 75, 129, 195, 402 and 406. However, the broader menu search shows that I1 and I2 can also be pivotal: both profitably reject profile 406, and I2 rejects the otherwise promising I1-exit profile 402. Therefore “the obstacle is I3, not I1 or I2” is accurate only for the last earlier trajectory profile, not as a global statement about the game.

## 6. What is and is not established

**Established to the stated numerical accuracy:**

- 1,782 finite-menu profiles were feasible and successfully re-cleared.
- The base, round-1 and round-2 menus contain 17, 36 and 45 distinct priced-line congestion regimes.
- Their restricted pure equilibria are respectively `{33, 75, 129}`, `{195, 402}` and `{406}`.
- Every one of those restricted equilibria is rejected by at least one explicit feasible continuous deviation. Profiles 33, 75, 195 and 406 are rejected for all three investors.
- I1's regret at profile 406 lies in `[152.33, 321.17]` EUR/day under the best-found response and merchant upper bound.
- I1's regret at profile 402 lies in `[0, 4.09]` EUR/day; nevertheless profile 402 is rejected by I2 and I3.
- Restricted equilibria are highly menu-sensitive: every equilibrium from one round disappears in the next enriched round.

**Not established:**

- no global best response or regret upper bound for I2 or I3;
- convergence of the double-oracle procedure;
- existence or nonexistence of a pure equilibrium in the continuous 54-dimensional game;
- a positive lower bound on maximum regret over the complete feasible region;
- a mathematical best-response cycle;
- a stable low-regret region elsewhere in the feasible polytope.

## Recommended next step

Continue the adaptive menu procedure, but make certification—not merely more iterations—the stopping target:

1. Add profile 406's three deviations and solve oracle round 3. If no restricted pure equilibrium remains, report absence only for that enriched finite game.
2. If another restricted equilibrium appears, audit it first with inexpensive structured probes, then use strong-duality MPEC refinements only for survivors.
3. Develop regime-wise upper bounds for I2 and I3 by bounding their owned-generation rents from the affine price solution within each priced-line/marginal-generator regime. Together with the existing I1 merchant bound, this is the missing ingredient for a certified low-regret or nonexistence statement.
4. Prioritise the neighbourhood of profile 402: I1 is effectively settled at exit there, reducing the economically relevant search to the I2/I3 interaction around the L46 hour-14 boundary.
5. Use the phrase “best-found improving-response regime alternation” unless and until all responses on a closed repeated state sequence are globally certified.

At present the evidence supports a clear practical conclusion: **the tested low-regret candidates do not survive continuous unilateral deviations, and finite-menu fixed points should not be treated as equilibria without an oracle audit.**
