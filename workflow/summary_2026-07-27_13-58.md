## Current auction experiment

The diagnostic has two investors competing for the 100 MW access limit at N8.
The active investor is the leader, while the access auction and fixed-demand
spot market are the two embedded followers.

The mathematical block in `single_investor_auction_mpec.py` contains:

- continuous bid quantity and storage-energy decisions;
- auction primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and strong duality;
- awarded auction MW as installed BESS power;
- fixed-demand spot-market primal feasibility;
- spot dual feasibility/stationarity and strong duality;
- investor spot revenue, optional generation rent, degradation cost, annualized
  CAPEX, pay-as-bid access payment, and profit maximization.

Ipopt returns local optimistic MPEC candidates, not global Stackelberg proofs.

## Mechanism decisions

- Access settlement is now pay-as-bid only.
- The old uniform-dual settlement and minimum-winning-bid refinement were
  removed.
- The maintained auction diagnostic uses a 0.01 EUR/MW/day bid-price grid.
- A 0.001 EUR/MW/day deterministic priority offset resolves equal raw bids.
  With the current investor ordering, I1 wins a raw-price tie against I2.
- Grid mode currently supports exactly one active node and requires damping=1
  in Gauss-Seidel so updated prices remain on the grid.
- Bid quantity and energy remain continuous inside every fixed-price MPEC.
- Candidate results are accepted only if termination is optimal, auction and
  spot strong-duality gaps are small, pay-as-bid payment reconciles, and the
  embedded award matches an independent auction reclear.
- The explicit zero-quantity outside option remains a separate MPEC in the
  current implementation.
- The main auction MPEC uses Ipopt `tol=1e-4` and
  `acceptable_tol=1e-4`. Independent auction reclears use tighter tolerances.

## Main findings

### Continuous-price MPEC is not reliable

A one-iteration continuous-price Gauss-Seidel run returned `optimal` NLP
terminations but both embedded best responses failed the independent award
reclear by about 100 MW. The active investors exploited near-tied continuous
prices and optimistic auction allocations. Those results are not economically
usable.

Diagnostic output:

`model/auction/output/gauss_seidel/two_investor_n8_i2_100mw_30_iter1/`

### Tick-price single-investor response

The single-investor tick runner evaluates the minimum grid price, each relevant
rival price, one tick above each rival, and a forced-zero outside option. In the
I1-versus-I2 case, the candidates are 0, 30, and 30.01 EUR/MW/day.

A validated candidate at 30.01 returned approximately:

- I1 award: 100 MW;
- energy: 471.126 MWh;
- profit: 16,482 EUR/day;
- independent award difference below `1e-6` MW;
- auction and spot strong-duality gaps below the configured thresholds.

An exact 30 EUR/MW/day bid can also be optimal for I1 because the declared
priority rule lets I1 win a tie. Therefore rival-plus-one-tick is not imposed as
an artificial rule; it depends on the auction tie priority.

### Tick-price Gauss-Seidel iteration

The same tick candidate generator and validation function are now reused by
`gauss_seidel.py`. One complete I1-to-I2 sweep with a 0.01 tick produced:

- I1 selected 100 MW at 30.00 EUR/MW/day;
  - profit about 16,485.41 EUR/day;
  - embedded award about 100 MW;
  - independent award difference `9.31e-7` MW.
- I2 then selected 100 MW at 30.01 EUR/MW/day;
  - profit about 15,702.33 EUR/day;
  - embedded award about 100 MW;
  - independent award difference `2.36e-7` MW.
- Final independent clearing after both updates:
  - I1 award approximately zero;
  - I2 award approximately 100 MW.
- The run is not converged because only one sweep was requested. In a later
  sweep I1 would likely consider 30.01 and 30.02, so bid leapfrogging or cycling
  remains a central issue to test.

## Computational issue and pending modeling choice

The current tick Gauss-Seidel loop solves four MPECs per investor in this case:

1. price 0;
2. rival price;
3. rival price plus one tick;
4. forced-zero outside option.

With two investors this is eight MPECs per sweep, plus small independent auction
LP reclears. The user wants only one MPEC per investor, meaning two MPECs per
sweep.

An exact free discrete-price best response cannot be optimized by one Ipopt NLP
without either candidate enumeration or integer/disjunctive variables. A single
MINLP/MPEC would require a different solver and could be more difficult than the
current enumeration.

For the restricted present case - two investors, one node, and a rival able to
fill the entire 100 MW limit - the price choice can be reduced analytically:

- if the active investor wins an equal-price tie, solve one MPEC with price
  fixed at the rival price;
- if it loses the tie, solve one MPEC with price fixed at rival price plus
  0.01;
- leave bid quantity free, including zero, so non-participation remains
  feasible inside that MPEC;
- perform only the small independent auction reclear afterward.

This reduction is defensible in the restricted case because higher winning
prices are dominated under pay-as-bid and lower prices receive no capacity.
It is not generally exact with multiple rivals, residual access capacity, or
multiple active nodes. This one-MPEC-per-investor mode has been proposed but is
not implemented yet.

## Recommended next step

Decide explicitly whether the auction experiment will remain restricted to the
current two-investor, one-node, fully subscribed case. If yes, implement the
analytically reduced one-MPEC-per-investor threshold-price response and retain
the independent award reclear as a safeguard. If the mechanism must support
multiple rivals, residual capacity, or multiple nodes, candidate enumeration
or a mixed-integer reformulation is still required to preserve a genuinely free
discrete bid-price decision.
