## Current objective

Develop a reliable starting point for the normal four-investor Gauss–Seidel EPEC by solving one common-snapshot Jacobi best-response sweep, proportionally projecting nodal overloads, and resuming from the resulting checkpoint. The maintained lower-level reformulation is primal feasibility + dual feasibility + strong duality; it has no explicit complementarity constraints.

## Maintained base-model decisions

- Active data: `model/data/processed/market_data_IEEE_9Bus_congestion.json`.
- Fixed nodal demand; no load-shedding primal/dual block.
- Zero dispatch regularization.
- Ipopt `tol=acceptable_tol=1e-4`; sparse MUMPS linear solver.
- Every investor sees each rival as a distinct nodal battery with that rival's MW and MWh, never an aggregate rival fleet.
- The active investor is represented at all nodes. A rival storage-node block is created only for nonzero rival MW or MWh.
- Zero-capacity generator-hours are omitted from the embedded primal-dual model.
- Current electricity-price bounds: `lam` and `lam_sys` use `[-500, 500]` EUR/MWh. All other embedded lower-level duals use `[-10,000, 10,000]`.
- Capacity pairs are normalized to exact zero between iterations when both MW and MWh are `<=1e-4`. This is below the solve tolerance and preserves sparse rival representations.

## Interpretation and caveats

- Model-size reduction is a clear computational success: the previously failing I3 Jacobi response now solves.
- Raising the bound from 10,000 to 50,000 worsened runtime and proved that I2/I3 optimistic dual prices and profits are not economically robust. Strong duality verifies lower-level optimality but does not select a unique or neutral dual price.
- I2/I3 discharge at the artificial price ceiling in hour 20. Their optimistic profits must not be interpreted as market outcomes.
- The v3 checkpoint is feasible and useful as an algorithmic starting heuristic, but it is not an equilibrium and its I2/I3 raw best responses reflect optimistic bound selection.
- After the sensitivity, the maintained code split the bounds: ±500 applies only to `lam`/`lam_sys`, while all internal lower-level duals retain ±10,000. The split-bound Jacobi result is documented below.
