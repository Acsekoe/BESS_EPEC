# Handoff summary - 2026-07-28 09:02: uniform clearing-price access auction

## Current objective

Replace the failed continuous pay-as-bid access-bidding mechanism in the
experimental auction EPEC (`model/auction/`) with a mechanism that can produce
meaningful quantity/price bid pairs. This session implemented and verified that
replacement: a **uniform nodal clearing-price payment rule combined with a
strictly concave quadratic auction regularization**. Pay-as-bid remains
available only as a legacy diagnostic mode.

Read `project_context.md` first; its auction paragraph was updated this session.

## Why the old mechanism could not work (diagnosis)

The 2026-07-27 experiments (see `workflow/summary_2026-07-27_22-58.md`) failed
for structural, not algorithmic, reasons. No update rule (Jacobi/Seidel),
information assumption, ordering, or seeding can fix them:

1. **Pay-as-bid destroys price meaning in both regimes.** An investor pays its
   own bid, so with guaranteed residual capacity the best response drives the
   price to zero (micro-euro bids from zero-like starts). Under contest, the
   best response is "rival cutoff + epsilon" with no smallest epsilon, so no
   best response exists (1e-8 price spreads, rotating winners). This is the
   classic Bertrand/pay-as-bid nonexistence result. A tick grid restores a
   smallest overbid but typically yields Edgeworth cycles, not a fixed point.
2. **Pay-as-bid competition drives the system exactly into the tie region where
   the auction LP is set-valued.** At a tie the optimistic embedded MPEC awards
   the full node to the leader, so the (correctly) strengthened
   embedded-versus-common award convergence test is structurally unattainable:
   every losing investor's MPEC keeps claiming ~100 MW.

## The implemented mechanism

### Auction follower: strictly concave QP

At each node with bids `(b_i, q_i)` and limit `K`:

    max  sum_i b_i x_i - (eps/2) sum_i x_i^2
    s.t. 0 <= x_i <= q_i,  sum_i x_i <= K   (dual mu >= 0)

- `eps` = `--auction-quadratic-epsilon`, default `1e-3` EUR/MW^2/day. With
  awards up to 100 MW the allocation perturbation is ~0.1 EUR/MW/day: above
  solver tolerance, far below the ~33 EUR/MW/day price scale.
- The allocation is **unique and continuous in the bids**; exact price ties
  split symmetrically among uncapped bidders, subject to their bid-quantity
  limits. This *is* the tie-break in continuous mode; sub-tick merit offsets
  are now a pay-as-bid tick-mode-only device.
- Embedded formulation: primal feasibility + dual feasibility
  `mu_n + eta_i >= b_i - eps*x_i` + strong duality with the quadratic term on
  both sides (`sum b x - eps*sum x^2 = mu*K + sum eta*q`). For a convex QP this
  equality enforces exactly all complementarity conditions, i.e. it is the
  exact KKT system - same embedding pattern as before, no new constraint types.

### Payment rule: uniform clearing price (the essential change)

    access_payment_i = mu_n * x_i     (was: b_i * x_i)

`mu_n` is the auction capacity dual = nodal clearing price. Consequences:

- The bid price no longer sets the payment; it only decides marginal award.
  No zero-price collapse when uncontested (mu = 0 automatically when the limit
  is slack - no scarcity, no scarcity rent) and no epsilon-overbidding race
  when contested (raising your price above the cutoff does not change what you
  pay). Bidding ~true marginal value of access becomes approximately optimal.
- The remaining strategic margin is **demand reduction** (bid a smaller
  quantity to depress mu) - a real, interpretable effect of uniform-price
  multi-unit auctions and a legitimate thesis result.
- Known caveat: the payment depends on a follower dual, so the optimistic
  dual-selection concern (as for spot lambda) applies to mu. Mitigations:
  the eps-QP pins mu uniquely whenever any award is interior; auction duals are
  bounded by the bid cap (100 EUR/MW/day) instead of 10,000; and convergence
  now requires embedded mu to match the common re-clear mu.

### Deterministic reference clearing price

`uniform_clearing_prices()` in `nodal_access_auction_primal.py` computes mu
from a solved auction: 0 if the limit is slack; pinned to
`b_i - eps*x_i` (averaged) if any award is strictly interior; otherwise the
**highest-rejected-bid convention** (lower end of the feasible KKT interval),
clipped so fully-awarded bids stay consistent. Because the optimistic leader
also prefers low mu where it pays, this convention minimizes false
nonconvergence.

### Convergence test (now six conditions)

bid quantity, raw price, duration, common award, unilateral embedded award,
and (uniform rule only) **max |embedded mu_n - common re-clear mu_n| over each
investor's payment-relevant nodes** `<= --clearing-price-tol` (default 0.05
EUR/MW/day). Payment-relevant = the investor has positive embedded or common
award at the node.

## Interpretation of the first sweep (important for the next runs)

Prices escalated above the 35.48 seed to a 38.90 cutoff. Under uniform pricing
this is expected and meaningful: losers pay nothing, so each can costlessly bid
up to its true value of access; escalation should stop near the marginal
loser's genuine willingness to pay - exactly the price the mechanism is meant
to discover. The equilibrium clearing price may legitimately exceed the
33-35.5 EUR/MW/day headroom multipliers, because those were marginal values at
~70 MW, not the value of sole 100 MW ownership. Watch for quantity shading
(demand reduction) as the now-relevant strategic margin. Do not report any
snapshot as an equilibrium price unless the run converges under all six
conditions and is order-robust.
