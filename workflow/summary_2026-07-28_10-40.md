## Automatic Jacobi initializer added to the maintained EPEC

Fresh Seidel runs now automatically perform the following initialization:

1. Every investor solves one MPEC against the same economic rival-capacity
   snapshot (default: zero MW at every investor-node).
2. Ipopt still receives the independent positive numerical starting guess
   controlled by `--seed-power-mw` (default: 10 MW). The 10 MW value is not an
   economic capacity assumption and does not enter rivals' constraints.
3. The four desired responses are combined simultaneously.
4. Only overloaded nodes are proportionally projected onto the shared nodal
   limit, with each investor-node E/P ratio preserved.
5. The projected fleet is saved as iteration 0 and the normal Gauss-Seidel loop
   starts at iteration 1.

Checkpoint resumes bypass the initializer. Jacobi runs and
`--skip-jacobi-initializer` retain the legacy uniform-seed behavior. Initializer
metadata is exported to the checkpoint, run configuration, and final summary.
The initializer is explicitly a feasible starting heuristic, not an equilibrium
or access-allocation mechanism.

The allocation is therefore strongly solve-order dependent. Each investor
receives substantially more total power when it is placed first, most
dramatically I4 (146.5 MW first versus 36.4-72.2 MW in the other positions).
This is an algorithmic equilibrium-selection/path-dependence result for a
shared-constraint generalized Nash problem, not evidence of an economic access
payment or a literal first-mover game.

The Jacobi initializer itself is common-snapshot and symmetric with respect to
solve order; the divergence arises during sequential Seidel updates, not from
different economic initializer snapshots.

## Numerical verification and caveats

- All four summaries report convergence and shared-limit feasibility.
- No shared-limit projection events occurred during any Seidel loop.
- The I1-, I3-, and I4-first runs had only optimal MPEC responses.
- The I2-first run had two intermediate responses terminate at
  `maxIterations`: I4 in iteration 7 and I2 in iteration 14. Their previous
  capacities were retained for those updates. Later responses, including the
  entire final sweep at iteration 20, were optimal and the convergence test was
  satisfied. Replicate this run before treating it as equally clean evidence.
- Final maximum relative MW/MWh changes were below 0.02 in all runs. The largest
  final undamped nodal MW move was about 0.025 MW for I1-first, 0.006 MW for
  I2-first, 0.015 MW for I3-first, and 0.930 MW for I4-first. The I4-first run
  passes the relative test but has the least tight final absolute residual.
- Lower-level strong-duality gaps are numerically small relative to the
  hundreds-of-thousands-EUR objective scale. The I3-first history contains the
  largest successful recorded gap, about 0.027 EUR/day.
- Price selection remains nonunique. Across the order runs, maximum
  investor-MPEC versus joint-settlement LMP differences reach about 37.48
  EUR/MWh, and the largest optimistic-minus-settled profit gap is about 55.45
  kEUR/day. Treat each result as an optimistic EPEC candidate, not a
  unique-price equilibrium.

## Interpretation

The central physical conclusion is robust across these four runs: total power
is approximately 238.924 MW, N8 is filled to 100 MW, and the remainder is at
N3/N9. Fine siting, energy duration, ownership, prices, and rents are not robust
to the Gauss-Seidel ordering.

This strengthens the case for reporting aggregate capacity separately from
ownership/rent allocation. A single arbitrary Seidel order should not be
presented as uniquely identifying who invests. Since the maintained model has
no access payments, sequential private-headroom constraints effectively let the
algorithm select among different shared-capacity allocations.
