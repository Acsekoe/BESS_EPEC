# Recent findings and next actions

## Current objective

Reduce update-order dependence in the capacity-only BESS EPEC and determine whether the instability concerns physical siting, investor ownership, or only the diagonalization algorithm.

## Findings

- All four 200 MW sensitivity runs converged within 9--14 iterations.
- In the old 100 MW-node-limit runs, total capacity was about 195.45 MW. N8 was always full at 100 MW, while about 95.44 MW moved almost entirely between N3 and N9 with the update order.
- Raising the node limit to 200 MW reduced equilibrium investment to about 176.80 MW and concentrated roughly 152--166 MW at N8. This indicates that much of the old N3/N9 behavior was overflow caused by the binding N8 limit.
- With node limit 200 MW and L93 reduced from 400 to 200 MW, both tested orders produced essentially the same physical result: about 166.25 MW at N8, 10.54 MW at N6, and negligible capacity elsewhere; total energy was about 594.05 MWh.
- Investor ownership remains order-dependent even when physical siting is stable. In the combined case, about 33.42 MW was reassigned among investors between the two orders.
- L93 at 200 MW is close to, but not binding at, the final solutions. N3 and N9 have identical hourly settled LMPs, so this change did not create a genuine final-price distinction between the nodes.
- No excessive price spike occurred: maximum settled LMPs were about 74.3 EUR/MWh, below the approximately 85 EUR/MWh maxima in the original runs. Large differences in selected minimum LMPs despite nearly identical physical solutions still indicate lower-level dual non-uniqueness.

## Next experiment

Run the capacity-only EPEC with Gauss--Jacobi updates on the revised two-PV/one-wind data using:

- node limit: 200 MW;
- L93 limit: 400 MW;
- damping: 0.25, meaning 25% new best response and 75% previous iterate;
- convergence tolerance: 0.02;
- maximum iterations: 100;
- the same generator calibration as the order-sensitivity runs;
- both `I1-I4-I3-I2` and `I4-I2-I1-I3` investor listings;
- outputs under `model/output/jacobi_renewable_geography_2026-07-31/damping025/`.

Because Jacobi investors respond to one common snapshot, the two investor listings should produce the same result apart from numerical noise. If they do not, the remaining sensitivity is attributable to solver/best-response selection rather than Gauss--Seidel sequencing. Repeated projection near convergence would mean that the projection rule, rather than a clean generalized Nash fixed point, is selecting the outcome.
