# Current-data EPEC runs and electricity-price selection

## Current objective

Compare the capacity-only and strategic-operation four-investor EPECs on the
current distributed-congestion IEEE-9 data with a 200 MW nodal connection
limit, and determine how lower-level electricity-price nonuniqueness affects
investment and reported profits.

## Main runs

Combined output root:
`model/output/combined_current_data_200mw_2026-08-01_13-18/`.

Both runs used fixed demand, a `1e-4 EUR/MW^2h` ISO dispatch regularizer, and
solver tolerance `1e-4`. The active data add 20 MW to each conventional
generator and a 200 MW peaker at N5 with marginal cost 95 EUR/MWh.

### Capacity-only EPEC

- Gauss--Seidel with damping 0.25 converged at iteration 36.
- Final fleet: 176.690 MW / 594.111 MWh. N8 receives 164.350 MW (93.0%); N6
  receives 9.481 MW and N9 2.850 MW.
- Joint-settlement profits are positive for all investors: approximately
  EUR 583/day (I1), EUR 588/day (I2), EUR 136,963/day (I3), and
  EUR 187,326/day (I4).
- Joint LMP range: 0.039--74.331 EUR/MWh. There was no shared-limit projection
  and simultaneous charging/discharging was negligible.
- This is a credible local capacity-only equilibrium candidate, subject to the
  maintained optimistic dual-price convention inside each best response.

### Strategic-operation EPEC

- Gauss--Jacobi with four parallel workers and damping 0.25 used two-sided bid
  prices and quantity offers. The price epsilon was `1e-4`. The proximal
  coefficient was zero for iterations 1--10, 1 for iterations 11--15, and 2
  thereafter.
- The staged run stopped as converged at iteration 18 after three qualifying
  damped sweeps. Final fleet: 193.690 MW / 593.853 MWh, with 180.762 MW at N8
  and 10.040 MW at N6.
- Quantity offers were almost completely available (about 99.6--99.9%), and
  weighted bid prices were close to zero. Thus the added strategic bid and
  offer variables did not produce economically important withholding in this
  run.
- The final damped steps met the stopping tolerances, but undamped residuals
  remained as large as 1.152 MW-equivalent for capacity and 1.361 EUR/MWh for
  prices. The result is therefore a damped/proximal selection candidate, not a
  verified fixed point of the unregularized best-response map.
- The realized proximal and price-epsilon penalties were only about
  1--2 EUR/day, all MPEC solves terminated optimally, strong-duality gaps were
  below about `1.7e-3`, no projection occurred, and simultaneous operation was
  negligible.

## Price nonuniqueness and merchant profitability

- The strategic MPECs select optimistic investor-specific dual prices, whereas
  the final joint market has one common price vector. The maximum MPEC versus
  joint LMP differences were about 42.3 EUR/MWh for I1/I2 and 15.8 EUR/MWh for
  I3/I4.
- At the common joint prices, merchant investors lose approximately
  EUR 511/day (I1) and EUR 682/day (I2), despite optimistic MPEC values of
  roughly EUR 4,759/day and EUR 4,401/day. Their positive investment is
  therefore not validated under the common settlement prices.
- This is lower-level dual nonuniqueness, not evidence that bid prices caused
  profitable physical withholding. A unique ex-post settlement price alone
  does not repair investment incentives; investors must anticipate the chosen
  price rule in every best response.

## LMP-bound diagnostic

Five additional Jacobi sweeps were run from the same iteration-18 checkpoint
with proximal coefficient 2:

- Narrow bound `[-10,100]`: iteration-23 fleet 187.892 MW / 592.876 MWh;
  joint LMP range 43.176--71.407 EUR/MWh.
- Matched control `[-500,500]`: iteration-23 fleet 188.454 MW / 593.042 MWh;
  joint LMP range 38.533--74.556 EUR/MWh.

Neither diagnostic converged, by design, within five sweeps. Narrowing the
price box changed bid prices, common prices, and settled rents, but changed
total capacity by only 0.561 MW relative to the matched control and did not
make the merchant investors exit. Bounds restrict the dual face; they do not
define a unique price within it. The maintained bounds should therefore remain
`[-500,500]` EUR/MWh.

## Minimum-norm price-selector test

The proposed rule is lexicographic rather than a single added constraint:

1. minimize the ISO economic-dispatch objective and obtain its optimal value;
2. over primal/dual feasible solutions satisfying strong duality at that
   optimum, minimize the squared norm of nodal and system prices.

An ex-post two-stage test was performed on the iteration-23 `[-500,500]`
control strategy using the same `1e-4` dispatch regularizer. The independent
joint re-clear produced 38.5333--74.5556 EUR/MWh, while the minimum-norm
selector produced 38.5333--74.5307 EUR/MWh. The maximum difference was only
0.0905 EUR/MWh and the selector's strong-duality residual was about
0.0017 EUR/day. The ordinary joint re-clear was therefore already extremely
close to the minimum-norm selection; the material discrepancy is between
common prices and optimistic prices inside the investor MPECs.

The test was ex post only and made no permanent selector implementation. To
obtain a true minimum-norm-price EPEC, the secondary selector must be embedded
so that every investor anticipates it. This adds another optimality layer; it
cannot be represented by simply adding a small price penalty to the investor
objective.

## Model and repository status

- The MPEC implementation uses primal feasibility, dual
  feasibility/stationarity, and strong duality. It does not explicitly embed
  complementarity products. Ipopt returns local stationary/local optimum
  candidates; diagonalization convergence is an additional, separate test.
- `Overleaf/model_extension.tex` now distinguishes the core physical market,
  capacity-only EPEC, strategic-operation extension, primal--dual lower-level
  optimality system, and optional numerical penalties/regularizers.
- The strategic CLI now accepts asymmetric LMP bounds while retaining
  `[-500,500]` as the default. Modified code remains uncommitted.

## Next step

Implement the minimum-norm selector first as a reusable joint-settlement
diagnostic and report its prices and profits beside the solver-dual settlement.
Only then decide whether the additional selector optimality layer is tractable
inside every capacity-only and strategic best-response MPEC. Until that is
done, label current MPEC profits as optimistic and the strategic iteration-18
state as a regularized/damped selection candidate.
