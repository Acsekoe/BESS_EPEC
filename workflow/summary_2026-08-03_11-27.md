# Capacity MPEC recalibration and electricity-price selection experiments

## Current objective

Develop a tractable four-investor capacity-only EPEC on the distributed IEEE-9
system with two meaningful BESS congestion-relief locations, and eliminate the
investor-specific optimistic selection of nonunique lower-level electricity
prices. Strategic-operation runs were explicitly stopped because they are too
heavy for this laptop; the current focus is capacity investment only.

## Active IEEE-9 calibration

The active data file is
`model/data/processed/market_data_IEEE_9Bus_distributed_congestion.json`.

- Restored the three synchronous machines at N1, N2, and N3. The N2 unit is
  `G_IEEE2`, 300 MW at 52.20 EUR/MWh. New default runs do not add the former
  artificial N5 peaker or the 20 MW conventional-capacity adder.
- Renewable generators remain wind at N1 and PV at N6 and N8.
- Set L46/L69 to 180 MW and L98/L78 to 210 MW. Topology and PTDFs did not
  change.
- With a 200 MW nodal BESS limit, the planner installs about 187.18 MW /
  547.20 MWh: 53.39 MW at N6, 65.63 MW at N8, and about 34.08 MW at each of
  N3/N9. It eliminates renewable curtailment. Without storage, PV curtailment
  is about 278.35 MWh at N6 and 306.26 MWh at N8.
- Wind-tilt sensitivities reduced the two-node PV-congestion signal, so the
  current wind/PV mix was retained as the main case.

## Demand-curve experiment

A configurable quadratic demand curve was added to the capacity and strategic
drivers. Three capacity-only Jacobi sweeps used alpha=100 EUR/MWh and beta=0.1.
Curtailment was only solver noise, but prices did not pin at 100 EUR/MWh:
embedded maxima were about 74--85 EUR/MWh and investor-specific optimistic
price selection remained. A one-sided curtailment curve at zero curtailment
caps price but does not identify it. Output:
`model/output/demand_curve_alpha100_beta0p1_2026-08-03/`.

## Three-machine strong-duality baseline

Two fixed-demand Jacobi sweeps on the recalibrated system are in
`model/output/three_sync_balanced_2026-08-03/capacity_only_jacobi_2/`.
The run was intentionally nonconverged. All four embedded maximum prices agreed
near 60.089 EUR/MWh, so the network/generator calibration successfully pins the
top price. Lower congested-hour prices remained nonunique. At the iteration-2
best-response snapshots, same-capacity LMP gaps were approximately 12.18,
12.08, 7.36, and 6.96 EUR/MWh for I1--I4.

A matched one-sweep strong-duality control, later used for formulation
comparisons, produced gaps of 1.737, 1.729, 28.190, and 7.229 EUR/MWh. The
difference from iteration 2 confirms local price branches and capacity
snapshots matter; comparisons must use matched snapshots and independent
same-capacity re-clears.

## Dual-bound and leader-penalty tests

Capacity-scaled bounds were tested on the active investor's charge, discharge,
and SOC-capacity duals with coefficient 500. Several bounds became active. In a
matched one-sweep comparison, LMP gaps changed from
`[1.737, 1.729, 28.190, 7.229]` to
`[5.705, 0.452, 28.088, 7.284]` EUR/MWh. The result was mixed and changed the
investment problem rather than harmlessly stabilizing duals. The scaled bounds
were removed; all non-price duals again use the maintained +/-10,000 bounds.

An optional leader-side objective penalty
`profit - epsilon * sum(lambda**2)` was implemented and tested at epsilon=1e-3.
It cost about 675--699 EUR/day per investor but changed the matched LMP gaps to
`[8.246, 8.152, 28.239, 7.254]` EUR/MWh. It selects prices closer to zero, not
the ISO/reference price, and does not defeat portfolio rent incentives. The
feature remains optional (`--lambda-l2-penalty`) and defaults to zero. Output:
`model/output/three_sync_balanced_lambda_penalty_1e-3_2026-08-03/`.

## Explicit relaxed-KKT formulation

Created `model/single_investor_mpec_relaxed_kkt.py`. It deactivates primary
strong duality and imposes individual Scholtes products
`nonnegative_slack * nonnegative_dual <= epsilon_comp` without binaries or
Big-M. It supports the separate rival batteries used by every Jacobi best
response. Select it with
`--lower-level-optimality relaxed-kkt --complementarity-epsilon ...`.

- At epsilon=1e-3, each model had 5,936 products. Maximum products respected
  the bound, but their sum/primal-dual gap was 2.81--2.98 EUR/day. Same-capacity
  LMP gaps were `[33.040, 29.821, 20.533, 20.017]` EUR/MWh.
- At epsilon=1e-4, the summed gap fell to 0.267--0.271 EUR/day and LMP gaps to
  `[32.628, 18.725, 13.591, 12.985]` EUR/MWh. Some products were slightly
  negative (down to about -1.52e-4) because epsilon equaled Ipopt's 1e-4
  feasibility tolerance.
- Tightening helped relative to epsilon=1e-3 but did not consistently beat
  exact strong duality. Positive per-pair relaxation increases the leader's
  admissible price-selection freedom.

Outputs:

- `model/output/three_sync_balanced_relaxed_kkt_eps1e-3_2026-08-03/`
- `model/output/three_sync_balanced_relaxed_kkt_eps1e-4_2026-08-03/`

## Embedded ISO minimum-norm price rule

Created `model/single_investor_mpec_min_norm_prices.py`, which implements the
first formulation that materially resolves optimistic price selection:

1. Primary ISO dispatch remains exact through primal feasibility, primary dual
   feasibility, and primary strong duality.
2. Over the full primary dual-optimal set, an ISO-owned convex secondary QP
   minimizes `0.5 * sum(lambda[n,t]**2)`.
3. The KKT conditions of that secondary QP are embedded in every investor MPEC,
   so min-norm selection is a common market rule rather than a leader penalty.
4. A fixed-capacity ISO min-norm QP warm start imports primary duals, secondary
   constraint multipliers, and bound multipliers before solving the full NLP.

Select it with `--lower-level-optimality iso-min-norm-dual`; leader lambda
penalty must be zero. Each common-snapshot investor model has about 18,927
variables and 11,645 constraints.

One four-investor Jacobi sweep is in
`model/output/three_sync_balanced_iso_min_norm_dual_2026-08-03/capacity_only_jacobi_1/`.
It took about 17.4 minutes; I1 and I4 required retries, but all final attempts
terminated optimal. The run is intentionally nonconverged after one sweep.

- Primary strong-duality gaps were about 3.7e-8 to 2.5e-5.
- ISO-secondary stationarity residuals were below 8.7e-7 and secondary
  complementarity sums were within roughly +/-9.8e-5.
- Against independent same-capacity ISO min-norm re-clears, maximum LMP gaps
  were only 0.0675, 0.0336, 0.0357, and 0.1626 EUR/MWh for I1--I4.
- Corresponding economic-profit discrepancies were only 0.83, 0.41, 2.85,
  and 76.41 EUR/day. All maximum prices remained near 60.089 EUR/MWh.
- A tighter independent reference attempt at solver tolerance 1e-6 was
  infeasible; the currently validated tolerance is 1e-4.

`model/epec_results.py` now applies the same min-norm rule to final joint
settlement in this mode. The completed output was generated just before that
settlement patch; its run configuration and summary labels were corrected, and
an in-memory corrected joint settlement reproduced the same 0.02338--60.08936
EUR/MWh range and essentially the same reported profits.

## Proposed but not implemented: KKT "double-lock"

The latest proposal from a separate ChatGPT discussion is to avoid the
secondary ISO layer by combining:

- a gamma term directly linking a primal equality/injection equation to lambda;
- equality smoothing `(slack + 1e-8) * dual == epsilon`, with epsilon shrunk
  iteratively.

This proposal has not been implemented or fully derived. It should not yet be
treated as equivalent to the lexicographic ISO rule. Important issues to assess
first are whether the gamma equation changes physical nodal balance, whether
positive equality smoothing forces artificial interior dispatch/capacity, and
whether zero slack implies dual magnitudes of `epsilon / 1e-8` that conflict
with the 10,000 dual bounds. The existing min-norm ISO formulation is the
current evidence-backed price-selection candidate.
