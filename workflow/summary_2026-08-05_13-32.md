# Optimistic-versus-settled gap explained: discontinuous payoffs, not dual nonuniqueness

## Current objective

Explain the persistent gap between optimistic MPEC profit and joint-settlement
profit in the Tikhonov capacity EPEC, and decide whether it can be removed by
convexifying the lower level. The investigation started from the hypothesis that
the gap is caused by lower-level dual nonuniqueness and could be smoothed away
by a convex (quadratic) storage degradation cost.

**That hypothesis is wrong.** The gap is a genuine payoff discontinuity in the
capacity game, and the run previously recorded as converged is not a Nash
equilibrium.

## Headline result

At the converged fleet of
`model/output/jacobi_tikhonov_runs/tikhonov_capacity_gamma1e-4_20iters_d025/`,
each merchant investor has a profitable unilateral deviation of about
5,200 EUR/day, obtained by shrinking its own N8 power by roughly 0.05 MW.
The deviation is evaluated in the **exact unregularized market** with rivals
fixed, so it is not a `gamma` artifact and not a dual-selection convention.

| Investor | profit at converged fleet | after shrinking 0.05 MW | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| I1 merchant, 8% WACC | -328.4 | **+4,869.7** | **+5,198** |
| I2 merchant, 12% WACC | -574.8 | **+4,623.8** | **+5,199** |
| I3 wind portfolio | 124,707.7 | 107,276.0 | -17,432 |
| I4 solar portfolio | 177,583.4 | 99,133.4 | -78,450 |

All values EUR/day. The reconstruction reproduces `summary.json` at the
unperturbed fleet (I1 -328.4 versus -327.7 reported; I3 rent 125,021.0 versus
125,020.95; I4 rent 177,912.0 versus 177,912.04), so the profit accounting is
verified against the run's own exports.

I1's reported `last_optimistic_mpec_profit_eur_per_day` is 4,873.45. The
measured deviation payoff is 4,869.7. These agree to about 4 EUR/day.
**The MPEC was not selecting a favorable price off a dual face. It was
correctly finding this deviation and reporting its true value.** The damped
Jacobi update pushed the fleet back across the threshold each sweep, and the
joint settlement re-cleared on the other side. The 5,201 EUR/day
"optimistic minus settled" figure is the payoff discontinuity itself.

## Mechanism

Midday hours 12-16, behind the binding export lines L78 (-210 MW) and
L46 (-180 MW):

- when PV at N8/N6 is curtailed by any positive amount, free PV is marginal and
  `lambda_N8 = 0` exactly;
- when curtailment is zero, storage charging is marginal and
  `lambda_N8 = 38.495` EUR/MWh, which equals the storage indifference price
  `eta^2*60 - 7.5*(1 + eta^2)` at `eta = 0.936` and an evening price of 60;
- nothing in the nodal merit order prices between 0 and 38.495.

The transition sits between -0.05 MW and -0.01 MW of I1's N8 power. Storage
absorption exactly equals the trapped PV surplus at the solution, so the
degeneracy is not incidental: strategic entry drives the fleet onto that
boundary.

Merchants and portfolio investors want **opposite branches**. Merchants want
curtailment so they charge for free; I3/I4 lose 17k-78k EUR/day if the midday
price collapses, because it destroys their PV inframarginal rent. Merchant
profit peaks near -0.05 MW and decays slowly beyond it (4,869.7 at -0.05,
4,865.4 at -0.10, 4,848.4 at -0.30), so each merchant wants to sit just below
the threshold, and they cannot all be the marginal one. Combined with the
incentive to re-expand once charging is free, this is a cycle. It is plausibly
the same mechanism behind the documented N3/N9 cycling in the
strategic-operation runs, though that has not been separately verified.

## Rejected remediation: convex degradation

Screened primal-only on the fixed converged fleet, sweeping the share `theta`
of marginal degradation coming from a quadratic term (reference power 25 MW,
marginal degradation held at 7.5 EUR/MWh per leg at that reference).

| theta | linear part EUR/MWh | price jump at the threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.00 (current model) | 15.00 | 38.50 |
| 0.10 | 13.50 | 38.75 |
| 0.25 | 11.25 | 39.14 |
| 0.50 | 7.50 | 39.79 |
| 1.00 (pure quadratic) | 0.00 | 33.01 |

The jump does not shrink. Convexifying storage slopes the upper branch only
(visible at `theta=1`, where the branch runs 33.0 to 40.1 continuously) but
cannot pull storage willingness-to-pay down to zero. Spanning the full gap
needs a quadratic coefficient near 0.6, implying about 53 EUR/MWh marginal
degradation at full power, roughly 3.5 times the calibrated value. This is the
same accuracy-versus-smoothing trade-off as `gamma`, in economic clothing.

A convex PV curtailment cost was also tested and also fails: it pushes the low
branch **negative** (-0.99, -3.97, -15.86 EUR/MWh at k = 0.5, 2, 8) rather than
raising it, because costly curtailment makes the system pay to absorb. The jump
at the threshold persists and widens slightly to 39.2.

Conclusion: the price discontinuity is structural economics, being the
merit-order hole between curtailed free solar and storage arbitrage value
behind a binding export line. It is not removable by convexifying either side
at defensible parameter values. **No repository code was changed for this
experiment.**

## Why the algorithm reported convergence

`tol_rel = 0.02`. The deviation is about 0.1% of I1's fleet. The convergence
test is more than an order of magnitude too coarse to detect a capacity move
that flips the sign of the payoff. The run satisfied its stated criteria; the
criteria cannot certify equilibrium in a game with discontinuous payoffs.

## Status of the gamma calibration

`gamma` is not the cause of the gap and tightening it further does not help.
The earlier finding still stands on its own terms: at the converged fleet the
soft market matches the exact hard market to within 0.26 EUR/MWh at
`gamma=3e-5` and 0.26 at `1e-5`, versus 18.98 EUR/MWh at `1e-4`, so
`gamma <= 3e-5` remains the right choice for price accuracy. It buys accuracy,
not equilibrium existence.

## Verification notes and caveats

- All perturbation results come from the exact hard market
  (`build_primal_llp` plus Ipopt at `tol=1e-8`), not the soft market.
- LMP is `dual[nodal_balance[n,t]]` alone. Adding `dual[system_balance[t]]` is
  wrong and inflated an earlier diagnostic by about 60 EUR/MWh. Nodes without
  generators or storage (N4, N5, N7) carry a flipped dual sign; N3/N6/N8/N9 are
  sign-consistent.
- Only N8 power was perturbed, with energy scaled proportionally and all other
  nodes held fixed. A true best response optimizes all nodes jointly, so
  **+5,200 EUR/day is a lower bound** on the deviation gain. This strengthens
  rather than weakens the non-equilibrium conclusion.
- Scripts for all four experiments were temporary and live in the session
  scratchpad, not in the repository.

## Consequences for the thesis framing

The dual-nonuniqueness framing in `project_context.md` does not explain this
gap. The lower-level price is unique on each branch; the problem is that the
upper-level game has discontinuous payoffs and the reported point is not an
equilibrium. Statements that should be revised once this is accepted:

- the ~38.5 and ~44.57 EUR/MWh "MPEC versus joint settlement" price differences
  should be described as branch differences across a capacity threshold, not as
  optimistic dual selection;
- the `tikhonov_capacity_gamma1e-4_20iters_d025` run must not be reported as a
  converged equilibrium;
- the dual-face interval figure remains a legitimate separate exercise, but it
  is not the explanation for the merchant profit gap.

## Next steps

1. Add a permanent post-hoc equilibrium verification: after any EPEC run, sweep
   each investor's capacity around its solution in the exact market and confirm
   no profitable deviation. This is a genuine recurring workflow need and is the
   single highest-value addition.
2. Re-examine whether a pure-strategy equilibrium exists at all in this
   calibration. If the merchant undercut incentive and the portfolio owners'
   opposite preference are both robust, non-existence is a reportable result.
3. Only after 1 and 2, decide between mixed strategies, a coarse discrete
   capacity grid that removes sub-MW threshold games, or reporting
   non-existence.
4. Do not pursue convex degradation or convex curtailment cost as smoothing
   devices; both are tested and rejected above.
