# N8 profit landscape: the capacity payoff is a step function, and BESS is viable only as a curtailment absorber

Follow-up to `summary_2026-08-05_13-32.md`, which established that the
`tikhonov_capacity_gamma1e-4_20iters_d025` run is not a Nash equilibrium
because the capacity payoff is discontinuous. This document answers the
next question: can the discontinuity be removed by changing the network
calibration (costly PV curtailment, line limits, PV size)?

## Current objective

Decide whether a defensible recalibration of the IEEE-9 congestion case
removes the capacity threshold at N8, and if not, what the honest fallback is.

## Method

Primal-only, exact unregularized hard market (`build_primal_llp` plus Ipopt at
`tol=1e-8`). N3/N6/N9 storage held at the converged gamma=1e-4 fleet. All four
investors' N8 MW and MWh scaled proportionally so total N8 power sweeps
2 to 200 MW. At each point, I1's N8 unit margin is

> (spot revenue - degradation) / MW - annualized unit CAPEX

with unit CAPEX = 17.080 EUR/MW/day at I1's converged N8 duration of 2.487 h
and 8% WACC. Free entry stops where this crosses zero.

Five configurations, 18 capacity points each, 90 solves, 249 s.
**No repository code was changed.** The script was temporary and lives in the
session scratchpad.

## Headline result: there is no interior free-entry point

Base calibration:

| Total N8 fleet MW | 2 - 80 | 87.77 | 95 - 200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| midday lambda_N8 EUR/MWh | 0.000 | 38.495 | 38.495 |
| PV_N8 curtailment MWh/day | 237.6 -> 21.5 | 0 | 0 |
| I1 unit margin EUR/MW/day | **+85.21** | **-17.08** | **-17.08** |

The margin is *exactly* +85.21 at every point from 2 to 80 MW, then drops
102.3 EUR/MW/day and is *exactly* -17.08 at every point from 87.77 to 200 MW.
Zero slope on both branches. **No capacity satisfies the marginal
cost-equals-marginal-benefit condition.** Capacity is pinned entirely by the
curtailment-absorption threshold.

## Two structural consequences

### Storage earns exactly zero operating margin when it is marginal

The upper-branch margin -17.08 equals minus the unit CAPEX exactly. That is
not a numerical coincidence. With charging `c` and discharging `eta^2 * c`:

> revenue - degradation = c * [60*eta^2 - 38.495 - 7.5*(1 + eta^2)] = 0

because 38.495 is by construction the storage indifference price
`eta^2*60 - 7.5*(1 + eta^2)` at `eta = 0.936`. Whenever storage is the
price-setting unit in its own charging hours, it prices itself to zero gross
margin and can never recover CAPEX.

**BESS at N8 is economically viable only as a curtailment absorber. The moment
it eliminates curtailment it becomes marginal and its return collapses to
zero.** All of its rent comes from inframarginal hours in which curtailed free
PV sets `lambda = 0`.

### Equilibrium capacity is a physical quantity, not an economic one

The threshold is where storage absorption equals trapped PV surplus.
Curtailment falls linearly at 2.771 MWh per MW of N8 power from 237.6 MWh,
reaching zero at 87.8 MW, which reproduces the converged fleet (87.773 MW) to
three digits.

Because CAPEX (17.08) is far below the low-branch margin (85.21), the threshold
location is insensitive to cost parameters: BESS CAPEX could rise roughly
fivefold before the answer changes. **The reported capacity is determined by
trapped-surplus volume, not by the calibrated technology cost, WACC, or
degradation.** This is reportable as a result, but it also means capacity
numbers should not be presented as cost-driven.

## Configuration sweep

| Configuration | Payoff shape | Entry stops at | Jump at entry |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| base | single step | 87.8 MW | **-102.3** |
| lines x1.20 | 4-step staircase | ~55 MW | **-38.5** |
| lines x0.80 | step, margin stays positive | 200 MW (nodal cap) | **none** |
| PV x1.25 | flat +85.21 throughout | 200 MW (nodal cap) | **none** |
| PV x0.80 | flat -17.08 throughout | **0 MW** | none |

### Looser lines partially smooth the payoff

Correcting an earlier assessment in this investigation: relaxing the export
limits does **not** merely relocate the cliff. At `lines x1.20` the midday
`lambda_N8` takes the values 9.624, 19.248, 28.871, 38.495, which are exactly
one, two, three and four quarters of 38.495. The four midday hours cross the
threshold at *different* capacities, so the payoff becomes a staircase:

| Total N8 MW | 2 - 10 | 20 | 30 - 50 | 60 - 200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I1 unit margin | +85.21 | +59.91 | +21.42 | -17.08 |

The terminal jump falls from 102.3 to 38.5 EUR/MW/day. This is the same
"many crossing points" smoothing mechanism previously proposed for multiple
representative days, obtained inside a single day. With tight lines the
constraint binds hard enough that all midday hours share one regime; with slack
lines the hourly PV shape separates them.

It reduces the deviation incentive by about 2.7x, does not remove it, and
weakens the congestion signal the calibration exists to produce.

### PV resizing does not help

It translates the threshold without changing anything structural, and does so
violently. The active calibration sits between two configurations differing by
plus or minus 20% in PV that give **0 MW** and **at least 200 MW** of N8
investment respectively. At `PV x0.80` curtailment is already zero at 2 MW, so
no BESS is built at N8 at all. This is an independent robustness concern for
the reported capacity.

### Costly PV curtailment does not help

Already tested and rejected in `summary_2026-08-05_13-32.md`: a convex
curtailment cost pushes the low branch negative (-0.99, -3.97, -15.86 EUR/MWh
at k = 0.5, 2, 8) instead of raising it, and the jump widens slightly to 39.2.

## The configuration that does remove the cliff

`lines x0.80`. The margin stays **+62.40 flat from 105 MW to 200 MW**. Crossing
N8's own threshold at about 102 MW only lifts `lambda_N8` to 8.586, because N6
remains saturated with free PV and holds the price down. Entry runs into the
200 MW nodal access cap and stops there.

Result: a clean corner solution, no interior cliff, no sub-MW undercut
deviation, and the post-hoc deviation check would pass.

The cost of this choice is that the capacity game becomes trivial. Every
investor wants the cap, so the entire question moves to who receives the
access. That is the rationing problem `model/auction/` already exists for, and
it puts the nodal access limit at the centre of the analysis, which matches the
stated research question. It is a well-posed problem in place of a non-existent
equilibrium, not a free lunch.

## Caveats

- Single-node sweep. N3/N6/N9 were held fixed. Under `lines x0.80` the nodes
  are visibly coupled: N8 storage pulls N6 curtailment from 396 to 133 MWh as
  N8 grows from 105 to 200 MW. A joint N6+N8 sweep is required before
  committing to a recalibration.
- I1 only, storage margin only. I1 owns no generation, so the figure is exact
  for it. I3 and I4 face the opposite incentive because a midday price collapse
  destroys their PV inframarginal rent.
- E/P ratios held at converged values; duration was not re-optimized.
- The base-case threshold coinciding with the converged fleet is a
  cross-validation of the diagnosis, not an independent result.

## Next steps

1. Run the joint N6+N8 sweep at `lines x0.80` to check the corner survives when
   both congested nodes move together. This is the gating test before any
   recalibration decision.
2. If it survives, decide explicitly between two thesis framings:
   (a) recalibrate to the cap-binding corner and make nodal access allocation
   the research object, or (b) keep the current calibration and report
   equilibrium non-existence as the finding.
3. Add the permanent post-hoc deviation check from
   `summary_2026-08-05_13-32.md` regardless of which framing is chosen.
4. Revise `project_context.md`. Beyond the dual-nonuniqueness correction already
   flagged, the claim that capacity is jointly determined by CAPEX, WACC and
   degradation does not hold in this calibration.
5. Do not pursue convex degradation, convex curtailment cost, or PV resizing as
   smoothing devices. All three are tested and rejected.
