## Objective

Starting from the four large Tikhonov Jacobi runs, attempt to recover a
capacity-only equilibrium and a strategic-operation equilibrium, diagnose the
source of cycling, test undamped and sequential updates, and fix misleading
runner/convergence behavior where possible.

## Bottom line

- **Capacity only:** a robust aggregate/set-valued candidate exists near
  **187.26 MW / 547.57 MWh**, but no unique nodal pure-strategy fixed point was
  recovered. Raw best responses preserve aggregate and investor totals while
  selecting different points on a flat siting/duration face.
- **Full strategic price and quantity bidding:** no unregularized pure-strategy
  equilibrium was recovered. Undamped Jacobi diverges, Seidel removes the
  simultaneous overshoot but leaves large price/offer changes, and explicit
  price regularization changes the game without stabilizing the full strategy.
- **Quantity-only strategic operation:** this is the strongest strategic
  candidate. Merchants exit, total investment stabilizes at
  **187.336 MW / 547.932 MWh**, and quantity offers become nearly fixed. Nodal
  capacity allocation still rotates, so it is an aggregate/set-valued
  candidate rather than a formally converged nodal equilibrium.

## Capacity-only findings

The no-penalty run's last 20 sweeps have aggregate MW between 187.2596 and
187.2663 MW (standard deviation 0.0018 MW). Individual totals drift slowly and
nodal positions move by several MW. N3 and N9 settlement LMPs are identical in
all 24 hours to a maximum difference of about `3.5e-12 EUR/MWh`, confirming one
exact nodal indifference direction. N6 and N8 are not fully price-equivalent,
so the remaining movement is a broader flat/local best-response face rather
than only an N3/N9 label swap.

Correct fixed-demand, fully undamped, unpenalized one-sweep audits were run from
both iteration-100 capacity checkpoints:

- total fleet change was below `0.005 MW` and `0.02 MWh`;
- each investor changed total capacity by only about `±0.077 MW` and
  `±0.36 MWh`;
- raw nodal MW/MWh-equivalent movements were still `1.48–8.60 MW-eq` because
  the solver selected a different siting/duration point;
- the newly solved unpenalized objectives were not higher than the saved
  proximal best-response values, so no profitable aggregate deviation was
  found, but Ipopt local optimality does not prove global regret is zero.

A corrected fixed-demand continuation with fixed `rho=18` reached iteration
110 without the relative residual crossing the 2% threshold. A one-sweep audit
with newly exported absolute metrics showed only `0.0211 MW-eq` applied and
`0.0843 MW-eq` raw movement. The failure is therefore dominated by relative
changes at small nodal positions, not material aggregate capacity cycling.

Interpretation: report **187.26 MW / 547.57 MWh as an aggregate or set-valued
capacity equilibrium candidate**, not as a formally converged nodal Nash
equilibrium. A rigorous unique point would require an equilibrium-preserving
lexicographic tie-break over profit-optimal capacities; a finite weighted
proximal penalty is not sufficient proof.

## Strategic-operation findings

### Undamped Jacobi

From the unpenalized iteration-100 checkpoint, five sweeps with `alpha=1` grew
to 335.21 MW / 1009.02 MWh. The final raw changes were `28.14 MW-eq`,
`5.26 MW` of offers, and `473.84 EUR/MWh` of prices. Projection was required.
This is simultaneous best-response overshoot, not convergence hidden by
damping.

From the proximal iteration-56 checkpoint, the first unpenalized undamped
sweep sent both merchants from 85.0/66.5 MW to zero. After three sweeps the raw
changes remained `13.77 MW-eq`, `7.80 MW`, and `294.45 EUR/MWh`. This decisively
rejects iteration 56 as an equilibrium of the original unpenalized game.

### Undamped Seidel

Five full-bid Seidel sweeps from iteration 56 kept both merchants at zero and
reduced the capacity residual from about 95 to 4.89 MW-eq, but the final offer
and price changes were still 7.76 MW and 308.04 EUR/MWh. Sequential updates
remove the Jacobi overshoot but do not cure continuous bid cycling.

### Quantity-only isolation

With prices fixed to truthful degradation, ten undamped Seidel sweeps and then
15 sweeps at damping 0.5 gave:

- I1 = 0 MW, I2 = 0 MW;
- I3 = 27.203 MW, I4 = 160.134 MW;
- total = 187.336 MW / 547.932 MWh;
- final raw offer residual = 0.0668 MW;
- final raw capacity residual = 2.1115 MW-eq, with intermittent values from
  0.343 to 6.829 MW-eq;
- investor totals remained fixed to three decimals; the residual is nodal
  relocation.

This shows that bid-price variables are the main operational-strategy cycling
source. Once removed, offers and totals stabilize, leaving the same capacity
siting nonuniqueness seen in the capacity-only model.

### Direct price selector

A full-bid Seidel run with damping 0.5 and
`strategic_epsilon_penalty=1e-4` reduced the canonical raw price residual from
541.02 to 1.38 EUR/MWh over ten sweeps. It did not converge: the final raw
capacity and offer residuals jumped to 26.85 MW-eq and 4.21 MW. The selector
changes the strategic objective and does not recover the unregularized game.

## Causes of cycling

1. Exact or near-exact nodal/ownership indifference creates a set-valued
   capacity best-response map. N3/N9 are exactly price-equivalent in the saved
   settlement; broader N6/N8/frontier reallocations have nearly flat values.
2. The PV-curtailment/congestion-relief frontier produces discontinuous
   capacity incentives, as established by the earlier 221-case screen.
3. Continuous two-sided bid prices create threshold/undercutting responses.
   Prices on inactive offers are economically unidentified unless explicitly
   canonicalized.
4. Jacobi evaluates all investors against a stale common snapshot, amplifying
   regime switches. Seidel mitigates this but does not remove the set-valued
   response or continuous-price problem.
5. The MPECs are nonconvex and Ipopt returns local optima. Checkpoint resumes
   restore economic strategies but not full Ipopt iterates, so alternative
   local best responses can be selected after restart.
