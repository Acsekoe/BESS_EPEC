## Research direction (stated by the user)

How do different investors split limited BESS capacity? Vision: nodal limits or
nodal access costs reveal a willingness to pay per investor. Expected trouble:
Gauss-Seidel may show strong first-mover advantages, and Gauss-Jacobi may cycle.
**Before any of that, market prices must be made unique** (see the last section).

## Decisions

- The MPEC has the user's structure in named blocks in `model/mpec.py`:
  investor objective, investor constraints, LLP primal feasibility, LLP
  stationarity, Big-M complementarity, variable bounds, and a profit identity cut.
- The default objective is the investor's direct nodal settlement
  `price*(discharge-charge) - degradation + owned generation margin - capex`,
  which is bilinear. `--objective linear` maximizes the equivalent
  strong-duality form `profit_linear`.
- **Gurobi is the only solver** (13.0.3 with an academic licence;
  `gurobipy` in the project venv). It solves MPECs, market LPs,
  reclears, and ranges. HiGHS is removed: it had not solved the all-nodes I1
  linear MPEC after 25 min; Gurobi needs 2 s.
- Gurobi options: `NonConvex=2`, `IntFeasTol=1e-9`, `MIPGap` from `--mip-gap`.
  `FeasibilityTol` stays at Gurobi's default 1e-6 (user decision; see limitations).

## How the linear objective and the identity cut work

- **Linear objective.** The market LP's strong duality says: consumer payments =
  production cost + scarcity rents of generators, lines, rival storage, and the
  active investor. Every term except the active investor's rent is
  *fixed number x one variable* (demand x price, cost x quantity, capacity x mu),
  so its rent is linear as the residual. KKT of its own dispatch makes the
  settlement equal to that rent. This needs no extra assumption, only that:
  (1) the lower level is an LP cleared at its optimum; (2) the investor's
  decisions are right-hand sides of its own constraints (capacities); (3) all
  other capacities, costs, and demand are fixed in this MPEC; (4) profit is the
  settlement of those assets. It breaks with strategic bids or with several
  investors' capacities as variables in one problem. It holds for
  one-investor-at-a-time diagonalization and with fixed elastic demand bids.
- **Identity cut `profit == profit_linear`.** Off KKT, settlement profit minus
  `profit_linear` equals the weighted sum of slack*multiplier products of all
  other assets and lines. Checked numerically with complementarity disabled:
  489 838 on both sides, while the full primal-dual gap was 619 730. At every
  feasible MPEC point the cut therefore removes nothing. It stops the McCormick
  relaxation of price*quantity from inventing profit: the bilinear N6 case
  without the cut had an upper bound of 3e8 against a true value of 9e3 after
  15 min; with it, it is proven optimal in 0.44 s.
- Literature (cited from memory; check details before citing): Ruiz & Conejo
  2009 (IEEE TPWRS, LMP-endogenous producer strategy); Kazempour, Conejo & Ruiz
  2011 (IEEE TPWRS, strategic generation investment); Nasrolahpour et al. 2016
  (IEEE TSTE, strategic storage sizing); Gabriel et al. 2013 (Complementarity
  Modeling in Energy Markets); Fortuny-Amat & McCarl 1981 (Big-M).

## Unique prices: options and proposal

Sources of non-uniqueness: supply-curve steps; six nodes without demand (only
N5, N6, N8 have demand), whose prices are pinned only through congestion; and
the MPEC actively choosing capacities at steps.

| Option | Unique? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A. Quadratic generator costs | partly | jumps at generator capacity limits remain |
| B. Elastic demand | only at N5, N6, N8 | economically meaningful, zero-demand nodes stay free |
| **C. Soft nodal balance** | **every node and hour** | penalty `s^2/(2 eps)` on imbalance `s`, so `price = s/eps` |
| D. Lexicographic price rule | yes | exact but needs another optimization level; C is its smooth version |
| E. Pessimistic MPEC | no | hard to solve, avoids steps without making prices unique |

**Proposal C:** a tiny price-responsive balancing resource at each node, with a
slope of eps MW per EUR/MWh. Prices become unique and continuous in capacity,
and steps become steep ramps. As eps -> 0, prices tend to the minimum-norm
optimal prices (Tikhonov). The KKT stays linear (`price = s/eps`). The linear
profit gains `-eps * sum(price^2)`, giving a convex MIQP. The balancing volume
is eps*price (1 MW at VOLL for eps = 1e-4), so eps must be chosen with the
price scale. Expected optimum: just below the old step, near 18 800.

**Caution:** an earlier project note says eps = 1e-3 price regularization
"still selected prices optimistically" (details unknown). The prototype must
test this. Unique prices do not make the best response unique (split across
nodes), which also matters for Gauss-Seidel and Gauss-Jacobi.

**Prototype plan (awaiting go-ahead):**
1. Add soft balance as parameter `eps` to `primal_llp.py`, `dual_llp.py`,
   `mpec.py`; `eps = 0` reproduces the current model.
2. Verify zero-width price ranges at the MPEC solution.
3. Sweep eps in {1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4} for I1 at all nodes: capacity, profit vs
   18 819 / 18 800, balancing volumes.
4. Profit vs capacity scale around the old step: it must be continuous.

Later: a shared nodal limit `p_n >= 0 _|_ L_n - sum_i X_i,n >= 0` gives a common
nodal access price (willingness to pay), an equilibrium alternative to the
ordering effects of Gauss-Seidel and Gauss-Jacobi.

## Verification and limitations

- All 10 tests pass with Gurobi. The no-storage market cost equals the HiGHS
  regression value (394 587.85188). Toy MPEC: 140 EUR, profit range [-10, 140].
  Ranges: 220 ranges in 16 s.
- With the default `FeasibilityTol` 1e-6, the all-nodes bilinear run fails the
  strict checks narrowly: multipliers of about 1.5e-6 that should be 0, times
  slacks of 50-110 MW, give products of 1.5e-4 against a limit of 1e-4.
  `FeasibilityTol=1e-9` passes but takes 213 s instead of 17 s. The linear run passes.
- Global optimality is conditional on `dual_m = 1e5`, the strategy set
  (nodes, node limit, durations), and optimistic price selection.
- Some N6 output folders predate the identity cut and `IntFeasTol`; the
  current reference results are `n6_i1_bilinear_gurobi_cut`,
  `all_i1_bilinear_gurobi`, and `all_i1_linear_gurobi`.
