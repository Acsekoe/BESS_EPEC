## Decisions

- **Second MPEC version, `model/mpec_relaxed.py`:** the same blocks as
  `mpec.py`, but Big-M complementarity is replaced by `0 <= slack * mu <= epsilon`
  (Scholtes), solved by Ipopt with epsilon continuation 1 -> 1e-4 from an exact
  market start (`initialize_from_market`, default 50 MW per node). It is a local
  method; the result depends on the start.
- **Unique prices, `--balancing-eps eps`:** a nodal balancing injection
  `b = eps * price` with cost `b^2/(2 eps)` in the market. Every price and every
  investor profit is unique and continuous in capacity. Default 0 is the LP.
  Reclears with eps > 0 take `price = b/eps` from the primal QP (Ipopt, tol
  1e-12); the dual QP with ordinary tolerances is unreliable.
- **Investment nodes:** N1, N2, N4, N5 and N7 never get storage. Use
  `--nodes N3 N6 N8 N9` (the PV pocket) to save compute.
- **Stay with the 1-day model** (user decision). A 3-day test only made the
  cliffs smaller relative to total profit; the optimum stayed on a step.
- **Quadratic thermal costs were tried and removed again** (user decision).

## Findings

### The step

- The PV pocket N3/N6/N8/N9 can export only over L46 (180 MW) and L78
  (210 MW). Without storage, 12:00-16:00, both lines are full and 584.6 MWh of
  PV is curtailed (peak 186.8 MW at 14:00). Free curtailed PV then sets the
  pocket price at 0 (outside: 52.20).
- Once the storage can absorb all of it, i.e. 584.6 * 0.936 = **547.2 MWh**
  (about 187.18 MW), PV no longer sets the price. The battery does, at its
  break-even value `60 * 0.936^2 - 7.5 * (1 + 0.936^2) = 38.50 EUR/MWh`.
  In the exact LP, any price in [0, 38.50] is optimal exactly at the step.
- Both sides of the step are flat (PV cost 0, constant break-even value),
  so the price jumps. A rising thermal cost only moves the 38.50 level.

| Profile (eps = 1e-4 prices) | 0.1% below | at the step | 0.1% above |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 pocket, 187.182 MW | 18 800 | **18 811** | -3 689 |
| I4 all nodes, 187.198 MW | 113 058 | **183 768** | 183 402 |
| I4 eps = 0 selection, 187.182 MW | 113 056 | 131 435 (price 23.22) | 183 402 |

- **I1 (merchant)** builds up to the step but not past it: one more 0.2 MW
  makes all 584.6 MWh of charging cost 38.50 (-22.5k EUR/day).
- **I4 (80% of the pocket PV)** goes just past it: its battery loses about
  3.7k EUR/day, but its midday PV now sells at 38.50 (+93k EUR/day). The money
  comes mostly from the grid operator's congestion rent (about 75k) and from
  pocket consumers (about 18.5k).
- **Optimistic selection:** the exact (eps = 0) I4 optimum sits exactly on the
  step and counts on 38.50; at that capacity the unique price is only 23.22.
  eps = 1e-4 removes this tie-break, but the investors still size to within
  about 0.02 MW of the step. The cliff is market power exercised through
  investment, not a solver artefact.
- No-storage profits: I4 94 419 EUR/day (unique).

### Thermal cost slope (removed)

Common slope d on all three thermal units, 1 day, pocket nodes, eps = 1e-4:
I1 always at 187.18 MW (d = 0.02: 20 095; d = 0.05: 22 259; d = 0.1:
25 958 EUR/day), I4 at 187.20 MW (d = 0.02: 193 215; d = 0.05: 208 645;
d = 0.1: Ipopt failed). It did not move any optimum off the step. The
implementation was never committed, so these numbers cannot be rerun from `main`.

### Capex (uncertain)

Just below the step, an extra MW earns about 125 EUR/day net against about
22 EUR/day capex (2.11 EUR/MW/day + 6.01 EUR/MWh/day). An interior optimum
would need about 6x higher capex. The inherited costs (6 600 EUR/MW,
18 800 EUR/MWh) look roughly an order of magnitude below current turnkey BESS
prices. This is not verified; check before relying on them.

## Equilibrium and allocation (design discussion, nothing implemented)

- **Expected loop behaviour at the step** (reasoning, not computed):
  - With eps = 0 and optimistic best responses, Gauss-Seidel can stop after one
    round at a false equilibrium: I1 at the step believes price 0, while I4
    builds nothing and believes 38.50.
  - With eps > 0 it ratchets instead, about 0.04 MW per round.
  - Likely pure equilibrium: **I1 = 0, a PV owner just above the step**.
    Equilibria among the PV owners I3/I4 are not unique, because each gains
    if the other pays for the storage.
- **Remedies:**
  - always eps > 0;
  - continuation from a large eps (0.1-1) down to small;
  - stop when no investor's deviation gain exceeds about 1 EUR/day, checked
    with independent best responses;
  - several investor orders and starting points;
  - cross-check on a grid of pocket totals.
- **Splitting limited capacity (the core research question):** with linear
  values and a fixed room (connection limit or the step), the market does not
  determine the split; an explicit allocation rule must be chosen. Candidates:
  1. central planner benchmark;
  2. first come, first served (queue order; one MPEC per investor, no loop);
  3. fixed quotas;
  4. uniform-price connection auction;
  5. the earlier pay-as-bid access auction.
  The shared nodal limit (thesis eq:headroom) does not bind at present: the
  code default is 1000 MW, and the data's 200 MW per node is unused.
- **Planned step 2: uniform-price connection auction**
  (variational equilibrium, Rosen 1965):
  - Each investor maximises `profit - sum_n rho_n * X_power[i,n]` without the
    headroom constraint; clearing `0 <= rho_n _|_ Xbar_n - sum_j X_power[j,n] >= 0`.
  - Code: `connection_price[n]` Param (default 0) in `prepare_input.py`, added
    in `investment_cost_rule`; a new `model/auction.py` with bisection on rho
    (demand jumps between full and zero) around an inner eps > 0 Nash loop.
    Start with one pocket-wide limit L.
  - Expected: if L is above about 187.2 MW, rho = 0 and the plain equilibrium
    holds. If L is below, the price stays 0, I2 (12% WACC) drops out, and
    I1/I3/I4 (all 8%) tie exactly. A tie rule is then still needed: equal
    shares, or a small cost per MW that rises with the investor's own size.
