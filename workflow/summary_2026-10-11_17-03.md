## Decisions

- **The inherited capex has no source.** 6 600 EUR/MW and 18 800 EUR/MWh were
  in the first commit; together that is about 21 EUR/kWh all-in, 5-7x too
  low. The default `capacities.json` is unchanged.
- **New cost split.** The power cost `c^P` (EUR/MW) is the grid-access fee.
  The energy cost `c^E` (EUR/MWh) is the BESS system, including power
  conversion.
- **Two cost cases** (`model/input/capacities_capex120.json`, `..._capex155.json`):
  - `c^E` = 120 000 EUR/MWh: global all-in installed cost of a 4-hour BESS,
    USD 140/kWh, about EUR 124/kWh;
  - `c^E` = 155 000 EUR/MWh: European turnkey price, USD 172-181/kWh, about
    EUR 152-160/kWh, excluding EPC and grid connection;
  - `c^P` = 1 EUR/MW: a placeholder, not sourced.
- **Sources:** IRENA (2026), *Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2025*, ch. 9
  (from BNEF data, 2025 USD per kWh usable). Converted at the ECB 2025 average,
  1.1300 USD/EUR. Inverters are 13% of installed cost (17% in Germany); they
  stay in `c^E`.
- **Daily cost** = CRF / 365.25, 15 years. At 8% WACC (I1, I3, I4):
  38.38 / 49.58 EUR/MWh/day for the two cases. At 12% (I2): 48.24 / 62.31.
  Checked against the model's `cost_energy_daily`.
- **Documented** in `Overleaf/model_extension.tex`, subsection
  "Investment-cost data", with citations in the new `Overleaf/bibliography.bib`.
- **Always run with `--balancing-eps 1e-4`.** With eps = 0, the I1 profit at
  its chosen capacity ranges from -21 003 to 1 501, and the MPEC reports the
  favourable end.
- **Loose tolerances** (`--mip-gap 0.2`, 300 s) are acceptable for now
  (user decision): only the direction matters.

## Results (pocket nodes N3 N6 N8 N9, eps = 1e-4, rivals at zero, 1 day)

Capex scaling with the old cost split (I1, exact, eps = 0):
- x1 and x3: I1 builds exactly to the step;
- x6: below the step, profit 640-808 EUR/day;
- x10: builds nothing.

| Investor | 120 EUR/kWh | 155 EUR/kWh | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 merchant | step, 187.18 MW / 547.2 MWh, **1 500** EUR/day | **0** | exact Gurobi, 20% gap |
| I4, 80% of pocket PV | just past the step, 187.20 MW, **166 429** (no storage: 94 419) | step battery **160 303** | relaxed Ipopt + reclear; the relaxed run at 155 stopped in a local optimum (6.4 MW, 109 128) |
| I3, 80% wind + 20% PV | step battery 105 784 (no storage: 103 599); bound 112 025 | step battery 99 658; bound 109 129 | Gurobi found no feasible solution in 300 s; I4's step battery evaluated for I3 |

- **Only the PV owner builds for sure.** At realistic capex, I4 builds to the
  step for price support. The merchant is marginal at 120 and out at 155.
- **For I4 it is all or nothing.** Below the step every MW only costs money,
  because the pocket price stays at 0. At the step the price jumps to 38.50,
  worth about +70k EUR/day.
- **Critical PV share.** The step pays off only for an owner of more than about
  18% (120) or 23% (155) of the pocket PV. This is a rough linear estimate
  between I3 and I4. I3, with 20%, flips between the two cases.

### Negative PV offers (fixed-capacity reclears along I4's step battery, 155 EUR/kWh)

| PV offer | I4: none / 99.9% / step | I1: 99.9% / step |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 94 419 / 89 635 / 160 303 | -4 621 / -26 885 |
| -20 | 121 056 / 127 955 / 235 173 | +7 061 / -26 885 |
| -50 | 161 013 / 185 434 / 347 479 | +24 585 / -26 885 |

- **I4 gets no trade-off; its jump grows** to about (38.50 + c) x its PV volume.
- **The merchant becomes viable below the step,** since it is now paid to charge.
- **Merchant and PV owner now want opposite sides of the step.** A PV owner
  can tip the price over the step with a sliver of capacity.
- **Interpretation (agreed):**
  - congestion traps PV in the pocket;
  - the price goes negative only if curtailment is costly for the owner
    (lost per-MWh subsidy, a plant that can't be switched off, contracts
    paying per MWh produced), so `C_PV = -c`;
  - whether c is a lost subsidy or a curtailment cost changes profits only by
    a constant (c x available PV), not the decisions.
- **Caveat for the thesis:** AT/DE are zonal markets. Congestion inside a zone
  is handled by redispatch, not prices, so local negative prices behind a
  bottleneck are a nodal-market (US-style) feature.
