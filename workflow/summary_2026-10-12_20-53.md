### 6. Solver settings checked

- **`NonConvex` has no effect on the MPEC.** V6 I1 runs identically with 2, 0,
  and -1 (2022 nodes, 1.162 work units). The MPEC is convex apart from the
  SOS1 sets; only `ranges` (`extrema`) needs `NonConvex=2`. The nonconvex
  formulations tried before lost (10-08 bilinear objective; 10-12 products,
  see `complementarity_and_degradation_2026-10-12.md`).
- **Bounding the duals does not help.** With every price and multiplier bounded
  at +-2e4 or +-1.1e4, V6 I3's root stays at 129 942.6 and the 60 s bound is no
  better. The owned `mu_gen_upper` enters `profit_linear` with
  -(1 - share) * capacity, so the bound cannot run away.
- **Threads.** Gurobi already uses all 16 threads. More threads would not close
  a bound that is weak in the relaxation itself.

## Update 21:01 (2026-10-12, Europe/Vienna)

### Ipopt needs relaxed complementarity: known issues

- An interior-point method needs strictly feasible points. `slack * mu = 0`
  has none, so the pairs must be relaxed or penalized:
  - Scholtes `0 <= slack * mu <= epsilon` with continuation, as in the old
    `mpec_relaxed.py`;
  - a penalty `rho * sum(slack * mu)` in the objective, exact for large enough
    rho and often more robust with interior points (Leyffer, Lopez-Calva &
    Nocedal 2006);
  - smoothing, for example Fischer-Burmeister.
- **Weak stationarity.** As epsilon goes to 0, limit points are in general only
  C-stationary (Scholtes 2001; Ralph & Wright 2004), so not necessarily local
  optima of the MPEC. This fails mostly at pairs with slack = mu = 0, and our
  optima at the step are exactly such points.
- **Optimistic at the step.** There the relaxed objective can exceed the true
  profit, using the epsilon slack on the 0.02 MW ramp. Report only the exact
  reclear profit.
- **Local and start-dependent**, as seen before:
  - 2026-10-09: a different start gave a worse N6 optimum (3 794);
  - 2026-10-11: I4 at capex 155 stopped at 6.4 MW (109 128) while the step
    battery was worth 160 303.
- **Degenerate pairs.** Shedding at zero demand and generators at zero capacity
  leave their multipliers undetermined. Gurobi's presolve removed 328 of them on
  IEEE-9; Ipopt has no presolve, so leave them out of the formulation.
- **Scaling.** Multipliers reach about 1e4 (the shedding pairs) while slacks reach
  hundreds of MW, so a single epsilon relaxes the pairs unevenly. Consider
  scaled products.
- **Procedure:**
  1. start from the step search (a few starts);
  2. solve the relaxed problem;
  3. polish: fix the active set and solve the fixed QP
     (`active_set_start.kkt_point`), which gives an exact KKT point;
  4. take the exact reclear profit and a local check (the step-search
     refinement);
  5. verify with Gurobi only at the end.

### Why the owners' root bound is weak (V6, I3, measured)

The root relaxation (SOS1 dropped) compared with the best KKT point (step start):

| | root relaxation | best KKT point |
|---|---|---|
| profit | 129 943 | 53 478 |
| N2 price, night hours | 52 | about 0 |
| wind dispatch | 3 420 MWh | 3 420 MWh |
| wind valuation | scarcity rent 179 053, I3 share 0.8: 143 242 | revenue 50 267 |
| PV valuation, I3 share 0.2 | 5 571 | revenue 27 374 x 0.2 |

- Most of the gap is one family of pairs: **the owner's own wind upper limit.**
  Wind is curtailed (slack > 0), yet `mu_gen_upper` > 0, so the relaxation
  values it at G1's 52 from outside the pocket. At a KKT point a curtailed
  plant sets the price at about 0.
- **The general pattern.** The root violates the active investor's own pairs,
  because its objective gains from that:
  - for a merchant these are its storage pairs (16:19: 99.5 % of the root
    violation in `mu_power`), worth only the arbitrage. Its root equals the
    planner value (17:56), which is close on V6 but not in general (IEEE-9 N9:
    7 743 vs 6 902);
  - for an owner they include its plants' pairs, worth the whole renewable output.
- **Ruled out:**
  - bounding the duals (section 6);
  - warm-starting the duals: our MIP starts are already complete KKT points,
    primal and dual, and a MIP start does not change the relaxations.
- Scratch diagnostic, given by its specification: `build_mpec(..., "I3")` on V6,
  all SOS1 constraints deactivated, Gurobi dual simplex. Compared were
  `price[N2, t]`, `generation_capacity * mu_gen_upper` per owned plant, and the
  exact reclear at `model/output/step_starts/ieee5_v6_I3.json`.

### Idea for the Gurobi certificate (not tested): branching priority on the owner's pairs

- Gurobi has no priority for SOS sets. Of the attributes, only `BranchPriority`
  is relevant, and it applies to integer variables (checked in gurobipy).
- For the owner's 48 `sos_gen_upper` pairs (2 plants x 24 h), use binaries with
  indicator constraints: `z = 1 -> slack = 0`, `z = 0 -> mu = 0` (no user Big-M).
  Give them a high `BranchPriority`, possibly also the active investor's storage
  pairs; all other pairs stay SOS1.
- Test: V6 I3, gap after 600 s with and without priorities (now 61-100 %).
  This decides whether the final certificate can be tight for owners.
