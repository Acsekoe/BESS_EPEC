## Decisions

- **Relaxed MPEC is back** as `model/mpec_relaxed.py`: the same blocks as
  `mpec.py`, with Scholtes products `slack * mu <= epsilon` and Ipopt continuation
  (epsilon 1 to 1e-6). It is a fast local method; Gurobi SOS1 stays the proof.
- **Strategic bids are an option** (`--strategic-bids`, both MPECs). Truthful
  bids are the default, so all earlier results are unchanged.
  - Each storage has an hourly charge bid (price to buy, truthful -0.5 d) and a
    discharge offer (price to sell, truthful 0.5 d) per node.
  - The active investor chooses its bids in [-VOLL, VOLL] with
    eta^2 * offer >= bid (no negative-cost same-hour loop). Rival bids are fixed
    profile inputs (`charge_bid_eur_per_mwh`, `discharge_offer_eur_per_mwh`).
  - Bids need `--balancing-eps > 0` (unique prices for the exact reclear).
- **Objective stays `profit_linear`** (strong duality). It counts the active
  storage at its true degradation cost instead of its bids, so no bid * quantity
  product appears (Ruiz and Conejo 2009). No return to the bilinear objective.
- **Settlement is pay-as-clear.** Bids enter only the ISO's objective (as-bid
  cost) and the charge/discharge stationarity. Everyone is paid the LMP (the
  multiplier of the nodal balance); the investor's profit subtracts its true
  costs. A bid moves the LMP through the dispatch, and sets it when the storage
  is the marginal unit at its node.

### Decision: bid-based best responses with Gurobi, but no equilibrium loop yet

- Direction (user): move to the bid-based MPEC (Gurobi SOS1) as the main best
  response; it proves optima and has no cliffs at the steps.
- **Do not run a full equilibrium loop yet.** First add a bid selection rule.

### Open issue: the equilibrium could depend on an arbitrary solver choice

- A best response determines the dispatch and prices, not the bids: any bid
  beyond the price range supports the same dispatch. For one best response this
  does not matter.
- In an equilibrium loop it does. Rivals' bids are held fixed while one investor
  deviates, and the fixed bid level decides how a rival reacts. Example: offers
  of 60 and 6 729 EUR/MWh both mean "do not discharge" at a price of 52; if a
  deviator pushes the price to 61, the first discharges and the second does not.
- Gurobi currently returns an arbitrary supporting bid, often an extreme one.
  Extreme bids freeze rivals' quantities, so the game behaves more like a
  quantity game than a price game. The equilibrium outcome could therefore
  depend on an arbitrary solver choice.
- **Before running equilibria with bids, add a rule that picks a definite bid**,
  for example the bid closest to truthful that still supports the chosen
  dispatch (a second stage at the optimal dispatch and prices). Not implemented.

## Update 11:52 (2026-10-13, Europe/Vienna)

### Bid selection rule: design (not implemented)

**Truthful bids** are the storage's physical marginal cost per direction:
discharge offer +0.5 d = 1.5 EUR/MWh, charge bid -0.5 d = -1.5 EUR/MWh
(d = 3 EUR/MWh). The dispatch slope (1e-4 EUR/MWh per MW) always stays
truthful; bids replace only the 0.5 d part. They contain no opportunity cost
of stored energy, because the ISO optimizes the SOC itself (`soc_value`). A
deviation from truthful is therefore a pure strategic markup on top of what
an informed ISO does anyway.

**Where the non-uniqueness sits.** The market problem (LLP) is already unique
(eps for prices, dispatch slope for storage). The non-uniqueness is one level
up: many bids give the same market outcome. It cannot be fixed inside the LLP:
bids are constants there, so a bid penalty in the ISO objective does nothing,
and a dispatch penalty would change the market clearing.

**Options considered:**

- *Budget, sum of bids <= B (user idea): rejected.* It restricts the strategy
  set instead of selecting a bid (bids stay arbitrary when it does not bind; the
  optimum changes when it binds). A sum of EUR/MWh over hours with both signs
  is no economic quantity, no market rule couples bids across hours, and B
  would be arbitrary. Restricting market power on purpose has a real analogue:
  per-bid markup caps (US ISO market-power mitigation, offer <= reference cost
  + markup). Possible later sensitivity, separate from the selection rule.
- *Penalty, profit - rho * sum (bid - truthful)^2: not as standard.* One solve
  and unique bids, but it biases the result (capacity, dispatch and prices move
  with an unsourced rho). A small rho falls below the optimality tolerance
  (MIP gap 1e-6 of about 8 000 EUR = 0.01 EUR) and the bids become arbitrary
  again, so rho must be large enough to bias. Reporting becomes ambiguous.
  Usable as an explicit behavioural scenario (markup aversion).
- *Lexicographic two-step rule: chosen.*

**The rule (lexicographic best response):**

1. Solve the bid MPEC as now: optimal profit pi*, capacity X*.
2. Post-processing solve of the **same MPEC** (all KKT conditions and SOS1
   pairs) with X fixed at X*, the constraint `profit_linear >= pi* - delta`
   (delta tiny, e.g. 1e-6 * pi*), and the objective
   `min sum_n,t (bid_nt + d/2)^2 + (offer_nt - d/2)^2`.

- It is a second solve, not something the MPEC yields on its own; the MPEC
  alone fixes only capacity, dispatch, prices and profit. The result is still
  an optimum of the MPEC; the rule only picks one of them.
- Exact: profit stays at the optimum. Unique: the squared distance is strictly
  convex in the bids, so one bid vector, whatever the solver path.
- Robust: the full MPEC in step 2 avoids guessing the active set by threshold
  (the cause of the 12 Oct warm-start bug).
- Cheap: fixed-capacity bid MPECs solved in under 1 s in the step scan.
- If several market outcomes give the same profit, step 2 also picks the one
  with the smallest markups; consistent with the rule.
- Capacity is fixed in step 2: profit is nearly flat in MW (1 EUR/MW power
  cost), so a free capacity could drift in MW to wherever bids look smallest.
- Not one solve: Gurobi's hierarchical multi-objective allows only linear
  objectives, and `profit_linear` is quadratic (balancing, curtailment,
  dispatch slope); Pyomo's interface does not expose it either.
- Code plan: one explicit function, e.g. `select_bids(m)` in `mpec.py`, called
  by `run.py mpec --strategic-bids` after the first solve; both solves and a
  reclear check are reported.

**The rule is an assumption about rivals.** Minimal markups put a held-back
battery's offer exactly at the price where the ISO would start discharging it;
fixed as a rival, it reacts as soon as anyone moves the price (competitive end,
closer to price competition). Extreme bids, as Gurobi returns now, freeze
rivals' quantities (closer to a quantity game). Document this; running both can
bound how much the equilibrium depends on the choice.

2. Reclear with the selected bids: same dispatch, prices and profit to 1e-6.
3. Re-solve from another start or seed: same bids.
