**Research question (user, 13:30):** how do investors (merchants, RES owners)
compete for scarce capacity in a grid with a high RES share, and which
allocation mechanism resolves it?

## Decisions

- **Mechanism tested: variant 1, a uniform hosting-capacity price.**
  - Each investor pays p_n per MW at node n and may alone build up to H_n.
  - The price clears the node: 0 <= p_n complementary to H_n - sum_i X_i,n >= 0.
  - This is the variational equilibrium of the shared limit (one common
    multiplier). The energy market (LLP) is unchanged.
  - Variant 2 (investors submit capacity bids) would need a second KKT layer;
    not pursued.
- **Clearing with truthful bids (capacity only).** With strategic bids, two
  batteries in one pocket cycle in their bids (section 1). The user chose
  capacity only.
- **Multiple equilibria are acceptable but must be verified with Gurobi.**
  New `verify` command.

## 1. Strategic bids cycle at a shared pocket (why capacity only)

N5 limited to 100 MW, price 0. I3 (N5 100 MW) and I4 (N5 46.8 MW) alternate
between two states with the same MW, MWh and dispatch (replayed):

| state | midday N5 price | I3 | I4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| after I4 moves | 42.73 | 114 878 | 105 016 |
| after I3 moves | 0 | 123 779 | 56 655 |

- **Cause:** at midday both batteries are energy-limited and no PV is
  curtailed. In the LP, any pocket price between 0 (PV) and 42.73 (stored
  energy value) is then consistent with the same dispatch.
- **The selection is a knife-edge:** the balancing and dispatch slopes (1e-4)
  pick the price, and N5 bid differences of 4e-4 EUR/MWh decide it.
- **Interests are opposite:** I3 (net buyer at midday, 20 % of the PV) wants 0,
  I4 (80 % of the PV) wants 42.73.

## 3. Why the step exists and how to smooth it

**Mechanism.** In a pocket (wind at N2, PV at N5) the export line is congested
during surplus hours, so the surplus s_t must be stored or curtailed.

- **Storage below the surplus** (sum of storage < S = sum_t s_t): some RES is
  curtailed, and the pocket price is the curtailment offer, -slope * u, which
  is about 0.
- **Storage above the surplus:** nothing is curtailed, so storage is the
  marginal buyer. The price jumps to its opportunity value, about
  eta^2 * later price - d (42.7 at N5).
- **The jump is vertical.** Nothing in the pocket has a willingness to pay
  between 0 and that value. Its width is set only by eps (price = b / eps,
  1e4 EUR/MWh per MW at eps = 1e-4).

The payoff of capacity is therefore a step function of the pocket total. That
produces many equilibria (any split of the step), first-mover selection, cycles
when interests are opposite (buyer below, seller above), and knife-edge best
responses.

**Smoothing means: give the pocket price a finite slope between 0 and the
storage value.** Options, from quickest to most defensible:

1. **Larger balancing slope eps (no code change; quick test).**
   - The existing term b = eps * price is an elastic resource.
   - At eps = 1 MW per EUR/MWh the price rises over about 43 MW of extra
     storage instead of 0.004 MW.
   - Caveat: centered at price 0 and active at every node and hour (5 MW
     supply at 52 EUR/MWh at eps = 0.1), so it distorts prices elsewhere.
   - Use only as a sensitivity: eps in {1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1, 1}.
2. **Price-elastic local demand at pocket nodes (small code change; physical).**
   - A flexible load (P2H, electrolyser, industrial demand response) with a
     linear willingness to pay from p_hi at 0 MW down to 0 at D_max.
   - Adds one variable per node and hour, a concave utility in the ISO
     objective, one stationarity equation and two bound pairs
     (urbs-style `*_rule`s).
   - The pocket price then rises along this demand curve as storage displaces it.
   - Needs sourced D_max and p_hi; it also competes with storage (a finding in
     itself).
3. **Several RES days (scenarios; largest change; most defensible).**
   - The step exists because one deterministic day fixes S exactly. With
     several representative days, S differs per day, and the expected profit
     becomes a staircase of many small steps (smooth in the limit).
   - Needs a day index on every LLP equation and KKT block (model size times
     the number of days), plus RES profiles per day.
4. Not recommended:
   - a larger dispatch slope (smooths only the storage's own value, by about
     0.01 EUR/MWh per 100 MW);
   - soft line limits (change the physics).

**Expected effect.** With a smooth pocket price, the PV owners' game becomes a
standard private-provision game with continuous payoffs (Bergstrom, Blume and
Varian 1986). Generically the owner with the larger share (I4, 80 %) provides
and the other free-rides, so B-like outcomes should be selected and the
continuum of splits should shrink. This is a hypothesis to test, not a result.

**Recommendation:**

- First option 1 as a quick test: rerun A and B and a step scan at eps 1e-2,
  1e-1 and 1 to see whether the knife-edges vanish and whether A and B survive.
- Then decide between option 2 (cheap, physical) and option 3 (thesis-grade)
  for the main model.

### B: no profitable small move, but the payoffs sit on an eps knife-edge

Probes: each owner adds 0.01 MW / 0.04 MWh or 1 MW / 4 MWh, removes 1 MW, or
removes 0.5 MWh, at N2 and at N5. None gains. Largest effects:

| move | own gain | rival change | midday N2 price (h 8-17) |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (B) | | | 52.0 |
| I4 adds 0.01 / 0.04 at N2 | -7 283 | I3 -45 132 | 14.6 |
| I4 adds 1 / 4 at N2 | -9 667 | I3 -58 965 | 3.1 |
| I3 removes 1 MW at N2 | -52 873 | I4 -8 835 | 3.1 (h 12: 52) |
| I4 removes 0.5 MWh at N5 | -26 183 | I3 -13 292 | 52.0 |

- 52 is the system price; 3.1 is storage's break-even after charging at
  -0.1, (-0.1 + 1.5 (1 + eta^2)) / eta^2. Any price between them supports the
  same dispatch.
- 52 -> 14.6 is a balancing difference of 0.0037 MW (b = eps * price). At B
  the 1e-4 balancing term, not the economics, decides whether I3 earns
  about 62 000 or 4 000 EUR/day.
- Hypothesis (not traced): at N2 the step is on the discharge side (midday
  export headroom), not on the charging side as section 3 describes for N5.
  Why 1 MW less power also collapses the price is not explained.
