## Decisions

- **Smoothing by a pocket flexible demand, not by eps.** Price-responsive load
  (e.g. power-to-heat) at N2 10 MW and N5 25 MW, willingness to pay 60 EUR/MWh
  at 0 MW falling linearly to 0. Stylized, not sourced. 20 / 50 MW is the
  sensitivity (`--flexible-demand-scale 2`); 0 reproduces the earlier system.
- **eps stays 1e-4** as a numerical tie-breaker only.
- **Exact equilibria are not the goal yet.** First compare how scarce nodal
  capacity is shared.

## Findings

### 1. eps does not smooth the pocket step

- I4 alone at N5 without flexible demand: its gain jumps by about 29 000
  within 0.005 MW (eps 1e-4), 0.7 MW (1e-2) or about 8 MW (1e-1).
- At 1e-1 the balancing term adds about 28 MW of fictitious hub supply.
  Evening hub price falls by 1.5 EUR/MWh; owners' profits without storage
  fall 6-18 %.
- Best responses against zero rivals barely change up to eps 1e-2 (I4 N5
  146.8 -> 147.3 MW).

### 2. Flexible demand removes the step

- In a congested pocket, storage first displaces the load, and the price
  rises along its curve to the storage break-even (N5 47.7, N2 41-42 EUR/MWh).
- Without the load, N2 also failed on the discharge side: beyond about 77 MW
  of storage the day price fell to 3.1, selected by eps alone.
- Size scan (0.1-2 x 20 / 50 MW): the ramp width and the merchant's loss
  both grow with the size. At 10 / 25 MW the ramps are 16 MW (N2) and 35 MW
  (N5); a merchant keeps 82-89 % of its arbitrage; hub prices move by
  0.1 EUR/MWh.
- Table and figures: `model/README.md` ("Flexible demand in the pockets"),
  `workflow/flexible_demand_2026-10-14.md` and folder.

### 3. Unconstrained baseline (10 / 25 MW, no hosting limit)

| | profit | N2 MW / MWh | N5 MW / MWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1, I2 (merchants) | 0 | out | out |
| I3 (80 % wind) | 117 090 | 71.6 / 432 | 45.8-47.2 / ~205 |
| I4 (80 % PV) | 98 939 | - | 94.4-95.7 / ~356 |

- Both move orders reach the same point (order-independent).
- Against zero rivals: I1 8 531, I2 2 959, I3 116 148, I4 75 153. I3 got only
  57 815 from the 20 MW start, so it is still start-dependent.
- **Owners overbuild.** Unconstrained planner: N2 63 / 359 and N5
  126.8 / 479, social cost 236 624. The equilibrium costs 3 009 more.
- The planner's storage just breaks even for a merchant (6 EUR/day).
- Cause: a pocket battery lifts the price on all of the owner's RES output,
  which is a transfer, not welfare.

### 4. Scarce capacity: N5 limited to 100 MW

Social cost = market cost (less the flexible demand's benefit) + all capex.
Payments are transfers. Planner capped at 100 MW: 237 144.

| rule | N5 holders (MW) | N5 total | social cost vs planner |
|---|---|---|---|
| queue, owners first | I3 100 | 100 | +1 541 |
| queue, merchants first | I1 98, I2 2 | 100 | +1 622 |
| unconstrained equilibrium (no limit) | I3 46, I4 96 | 141.6 | +2 489 |
| access b = 0.5 / 1 | I3 ~46, I4 ~95 | ~141 | +2 489 / +2 467 |
| uniform price (stops at p = 200) | I4 61.4 | 61.4 (38.6 unused) | +4 167 |
| access b = 2 / 5 | I3 14 / 2.5-7, I4 47 / 41-46 | 61.5 / 48.1 | +4 166 / +5 011 |
| no storage | - | 0 | +13 170 |

**Queue (first come, first served):**
- The first in line takes the node and keeps it. Owners first converged in
  2 rounds; merchants first was still trading N2 between I1 and I3 after
  6 rounds.
- Welfare is nearly the same either way. Under truthful bids the ISO
  dispatches every battery alike, so ownership does not change welfare at
  given MW and MWh.
- The order mostly redistributes: I4 earns 53 029 without storage when I3
  holds N5, against 98 939 unconstrained.

**Hard limit with a uniform price (one dual for all; `clearing --hosting`):**
- No clearing price exists. Bisection brackets [193.75, 200]: 109.5 MW
  (overfull) at 193.75, 61.4 MW at 200.
- In between, I3's demand jumps from about 48 MW to 0. An owner's value of
  pocket capacity is S-shaped: convex where its battery lifts the pocket
  price. This non-concavity is economic, not numerical.
- As a result the uniform price is the worst capped rule here.

**Quadratic access cost (`--access-slope`: every MW pays b × node total):**
- It hits the same jump: b <= 1 rations nothing; b = 2 drops I3 out of N5.
  No slope reaches 100 MW.
- The total is the same in both orders (48.08 at b = 5) and it converges
  fastest, but the split between I3 and I4 at N5 is not unique.
- Grid-operator revenue at b = 2: 7 551.

**Soft-linked auction** (`scripts/diagnostics/auction_softlink.py`):
- Each round: bid curves (best responses at 8 capacity prices against the
  current holdings), a uniform clearing price for 100 MW, best responses at
  that price, pro-rata scaling if overfull, then feedback (Jacobi).
- Pure Jacobi cycles with period 2 (p* 146, 133, 94, 337, 235): all rush into
  the unlimited N2, then all leave.
- Damping 0.5: p* 146, 166, 43, 146, 144; largest change down to 4.9 MW.
  Stopped at round 5, unconverged.
- Bid curves are noisy and not always monotone (local optima).
- Bid curves: I4 pays about 200-300 EUR/MW/day for N5, I3 less, merchants at
  most 100-150.

**Merchants never enter** an equilibrium, even without a limit; they hold
capacity only when first in the queue.

## Limitations

- **Scope:** one representative day; optimistic MPECs; Ipopt best responses
  are local and start-dependent. Nothing is verified with Gurobi; no run is a
  proven equilibrium.
- **Unconverged runs:**
  - queue, merchants first;
  - access b = 2 and b = 5 merchants first;
  - unconstrained merchants first (10 rounds, last gains 20 / 1 111);
  - damped auction.
- **Scarcity tested only at N5 = 100 MW**; N2 is never limited.
- **Planner** uses I1's costs (8 % WACC, as I3 and I4); I2 has 12 %.
- **Unsourced inputs:** flexible demand size and price, access slopes, and
  the G1 cost slope.
- **Earlier result tables** in `model/README.md` (best responses of
  2026-10-14 morning) are without flexible demand; labelled so.

## Next steps (suggested tests)

1. **Uniform price with rationing.** When the bracket closes with unused MW,
   allocate at the lower price and scale the marginal bids pro rata (small
   change to `clear_capacity`). This makes the price rule well-defined despite
   the S-shaped owner values.
2. **Scarcity sweep.** N5 = 60, 80, 100, 120 MW (later N2 too) for queue (both
   orders), uniform price with rationing, and planner. Plot welfare, MW/MWh,
   curtailment and the profit split against scarcity.
3. **Decide the mechanism set for the thesis.**
   - Candidates: queue as status quo, uniform price with rationing, planner as
     benchmark.
   - Access cost as a reinforcement-cost variant, not as a rationing device.
   - Auction soft link only with rationing and smoother bid curves (profit
     scans over own N5 MW, concave hull).
4. **Pro-rata rationing and pay-as-bid** as further rules if wanted.
5. **Non-firm (operational) connection** as an alternative: storage may exceed
   H but the node's hourly storage injection and withdrawal are capped and
   priced in the market (a new LLP constraint with its own multiplier).
6. **Robustness:**
   - flexible demand at 20 / 50 MW (`--flexible-demand-scale 2`);
   - a daily energy cap on the flexible load;
   - Gurobi `verify` of the unconstrained baseline;
   - representative days later (linear size growth; Gurobi proofs then out
     of reach).
7. **Commit** when asked. Inputs `model/input/five_bus_*.json` and
   `scripts/data/build_five_bus.py` are untracked and must be added with the
   staged deletions.
