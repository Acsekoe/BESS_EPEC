## Uniform hosting price: it works

One price per MW and day per node, the same for all investors (variational
equilibrium of the shared node limits). Loop: at given prices, Gauss-Seidel
best responses; every node's price bisects on its own excess demand, all
nodes at once (`clear_capacity`, now multi-node). Result: a clearing price
per node, i.e. the marginal willingness to pay per MW, plus the split.

| case | N5 price | N2 price | N5 holder | N2 holder | vs planner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flexible demand 10 / 25 (scale 1), N5 = 100 | none: [193.75, 200], 38.6 MW unused | - | I4 61.4 | I3 (no limit) | +4 167 |
| scale 2, N5 = 100 | **203.1**, full | - | I4 100 / 464 | I3 67.7 (no limit) | +4 429 |
| scale 2, N2 = 50, N5 = 100 | **203.1**, full | **403.1**, 0.6 MW unused | I4 100 / 464 | I3 49.4 / 388 | +4 243 |

- **Clears from scale 2 on.** With the larger flexible demand I3's demand at
  N5 falls smoothly and I3 leaves N5 (above 200) before I4 drops from its
  100 MW cap to about 46 MW (between 225 and 250). Every price in between
  clears; the search returns the low end. At scale 1 the demand jumps across
  the limit and no price clears.
- **Why it can fail:** an owner's value of MW in its pocket is S-shaped (it
  lifts the price on all its RES output only beyond a size), so demand jumps
  as the price rises. The flexible demand removed the price step (an artefact
  decided by eps); this non-concavity is economics and remains.
- **Who wins and what it costs:** the price allocates to the owner with the
  largest RES share in the pocket (I4 at N5, I3 at N2). I4 builds 464 MWh at
  N5 against the planner's 364; among sole N5 holders it is the costliest
  (+4 432 against +2 916 for I3, scale 2).
- **Prices are far above social value:** planner shadow prices at scale 2 are
  N5 9.3 and N2 0.6 EUR/MW/day (scale 1: N5 34.6). The clearing prices price
  the owners' gain from lifting pocket prices, not scarcity.
- **Per-MW price, longer batteries:** at N2 I3 goes from 67.7 MW / 5.8 h to
  49.4 MW / 7.9 h (MWh almost unchanged); I4 keeps a profitable 0.26 MW / 8 h
  sliver at N2, which alone sets the N2 price at about 400.

## Limitations

- One representative day; local equilibria from one order and start.
- The uniform price clears only at the scale-2 flexible demand (stylized,
  unsourced); the N2 limit of 50 MW is a stylized choice.
- Clearing prices are not unique (whole ranges clear); the search reports the
  low end.
- No payments in the welfare (transfers); grid-operator revenue not analysed.
